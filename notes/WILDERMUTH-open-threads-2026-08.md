# Wildermuth / Fleming — open threads, August 2026

## 1. Did Wollmershäuser find the Ludwigsburg emigration file?

Friedrich R. Wollmershäuser, accredited genealogist, Oberdischingen, wrote on **27 February 1989** promising to look for **the emigration file of Johann Michael Wildermuth** at the Staatsarchiv Ludwigsburg on his next visit, and to quote for a copy and transcript. His fee for the 1815–1914 emigration service was **DM 300**, which included a transcript and English summary of the emigration papers.

**Nothing later from him is in the photographed papers.** Look for: a follow-up letter, an invoice, a receipt, or a transcript. If he found it, the file gives the permit date and stated destination, which is the closest lead to identifying the 1847 crossing.

If he did not, the enquiry is still live today. Ask Ludwigsburg for **both** the Marbach series (**E 143 Oberamt Marbach Auswanderungen**) and the equivalent **Oberamt Backnang** emigration series, since Großaspach sat in Backnang.

## 2. Where does the Rielingshausen Ahnenliste join our line?

The offprint Wollmershäuser enclosed — *Südwestdeutsche Blätter für Familien- und Wappenkunde*, vol. 18 no. 9, March 1987 — is the ancestor table of the Swiss novelist **Max Frisch**, and it carries Rielingshausen Wildermuths back to **Zacharias Wildermuth, Schultheiß, born 4 February 1600**.

Our deepest is Johannes, b. c. 1650, same village. The two are almost certainly the same family. **Nobody has demonstrated the join.** The work is in the Rielingshausen parish registers between Andreas (b. 1745) and the numbered entries in the printed list.

The triple surname on Verona Belle wants explaining: born Kelley, raised Dunbar, or a first marriage to a Sheppard.

## 5. Who was Lewis Fleming's father?

The charts say **John Fleming**, b. 11 October 1781 Hampshire County, d. 3 November 1853 at Arnold's Creek. [Lewis's page](/family/lewis-fleming/) says **Edward Fleming** of the Simpson Creek farm, a Revolutionary soldier, on Joy Gilchrist's authority in *The Squires Family*. Two different men. Needs resolving before any Roe/Meeks branch is attached, since the whole New Jersey line hangs off it.

Also: the charts make **Clarissa Roe** (b. 1762) the daughter of **John Roe** (b. c. 1758). Impossible as printed; one date is wrong.

## 6. Resolved

**Lewis Fleming died about 1862**, in Taylor County — as the archive has it. The "d. 1849" on the Sadye chart is Synthia's death year misplaced, and the 1854 deed settles it independently.
