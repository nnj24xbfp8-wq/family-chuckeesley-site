## Three problems the edits introduced ⚠️

### W1. The unsourced 1867 got laundered
Nancy Bartlett (`L1ZC-CZY`) now carries **d. 1867** and **b. 1789** — both inherited from the merged duplicate. This is exactly the hazard flagged in round 1: the 1867 had no source and no place on `L6T9-J13`, and it now sits on a well-populated profile looking authoritative. **Either source it or demote it to an alternate with a reason statement.** The census/death-register checks in round 1 item 9 are still the way to settle it.

### W2. Two Elizabeths, and the Maine record survived
The Maine profile was **renamed rather than detached**. There are now two Elizabeth Bartletts attached as children of Thomas and Sarah:

- `L4B5-LTH` — b. 1788, Harrison County, Virginia ← the real one
- `KNX4-N5Q` — b. 24 Dec **1808, Cumberland, Maine**, d. 2 Dec 1845, Mason Twp., Oxford Co., Maine ← still attached

Renaming it "Elizabeth" made the bad attachment look like a legitimate duplicate sibling. **Detach `KNX4-N5Q`.** Sarah would have been about fifty in 1808, two daughters were already married by then, and the family had no Maine connection.

### W3. Hannah Thornsberry is married to both a father and his son
The Bailey line now reads:

> I.B. / J.B. Bailey (`PQX9-QRC`) + **Hannah Thornsberry** (`PQX9-GPV`, b. 1728)
> → Thomas Bailey (`PQX9-L3D`) + **Hannah Thornsberry** (`PXYY-G6Y`)
> → Thomas Bailey (`GHDT-ZLX`) + Nancy Bartlett

Two Hannah Thornsberrys, one married to each generation. A woman cannot be both her son's mother and his wife, so one of those links is wrong.

FGR-0019 has no middle generation at all: it makes **I.B.Y./J.B.Y. Bailey and Hannah Thornsberry the parents of the Thomas Bailey who married Nancy Bartlett**. Either the two Thomas Baileys (`PQX9-L3D` and `GHDT-ZLX`) are the same man and should be merged, or FamilySearch has a genuine extra generation and the duplicate Hannahs need sorting out. **Do not leave it as it stands.**

## Two new impossible dates found in this pass

**Thomas Bailey Fleming's children** still number thirteen, and two pairs cannot both be right:

1. **Henrietta** b. Jun 1853 and **Margaret Virginia** b. 15 Feb 1854 — eight months apart.
2. **Mary Lavina** b. 20 Oct 1868 and **John Ellsworth** b. 26 Oct 1868 — **six days apart.**

The second is new, and it is useful: Robert Earl's [sketch](/docs/john-michael-wildermuth-sketch/) gives *Levennia* 20 Oct 1868 and *John E.* 26 Oct **1866**. That reading removes the impossibility, which is a point in favour of the sketch's 1866 over FamilySearch's 1868. Combined with Henrietta's eight-month problem, the sketch's **ten** children look better than FamilySearch's thirteen.
