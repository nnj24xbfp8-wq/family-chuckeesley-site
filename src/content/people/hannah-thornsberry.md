**Hannah Thornsberry** is Chuck's maternal **6×-great-grandmother** on the Fleming line — wife of **[J.B.Y. Bailey](/family/jby-bailey/)** and mother of **[Thomas Bailey](/family/thomas-bailey/)**.

Her surname did not disappear when she married into the Baileys: her grandson, Thomas's eldest son, was named **Thornsberry Bailey Jr.** (b. August 1804, d. 25 May 1857 in Preston County) — the same pattern of carrying a mother's family name forward as a given name that appears one generation later in **[Thomas Bailey Fleming](/family/thomas-bailey-fleming/)**. Twice in this line, a woman's surname survives as a man's first name.

## Did she really live to a hundred and three?

The Family Group Record gave no dates for her at all. Two of Robert Earl's later chart sets do, and they agree: **born 1728, died 1831**.

That is **about a hundred and three years** — an age that should be doubted precisely because it is remarkable. Set against it: on those dates she was **around fifty-two** when her son Thomas married in 1803, which is possible but late, and she would have outlived him. She was born in the reign of George I and died in the year the Baileys' grandchildren were being born in western Virginia.

**Recorded, not adopted.** The two sources are not independent — both are Robert Earl's own database, printed at different times — so this is one claim stated twice, not two claims agreeing. A gravestone or a death notice would settle it, and neither has been looked for.

No birthplace or deathplace is given on any sheet.

> *Sources: the [Bailey Family Group Record](/archive/bailey-family-group-record-0019/), compiled by [Robert Earl Wildermuth](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/), c. 1990 (no dates); the [printed ancestor charts](/archive/wildermuth-fleming-ancestor-chart-set/) and the [1998 pedigree charts](/archive/pedigree-charts-1998/), which both give 1728–1831.*
