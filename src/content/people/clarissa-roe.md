**Clarissa Roe** — *Clary* on her husband's chart — was born **18 June 1782 in Essex County, New Jersey**, three years into her parents' wartime marriage. Her father **[John Roe](/archive/john-fleming-family-legacy/)** was away with Colonel Dayton's Third New Jersey Regiment for much of it; her mother **Susannah Meeks** had stayed behind at Westfield to nurse an invalid mother.

## Carried over the mountains

She was the eldest of six. The family left Westfield for **New York City in the spring of 1784**, stayed six years, and in **1790** set out for **Fayette County, Pennsylvania** — the western frontier, diagonally across the whole state. Robert Earl Wildermuth described the party plainly:

> *"three young children under the age of 8 and a pregnant wife journied that long, tortous route through the forests and over the Allegheny Mountains."*

**Clarissa was one of those three children**, and she was eight. From Fayette County the Roes moved on into the Eastern District of Harrison County, Virginia, where her father died in 1829.

## The woman who broke the chain of Johns

On **20 January 1803** she married **[John Fleming IV](/family/john-fleming-iv/)** in the Eastern District of Harrison County. They had **thirteen children** — and it is here that she leaves her mark on the record.

The Flemings had named their eldest son **John** for five straight generations. Clarissa's eldest son was called **Lewis**. Robert Earl puzzled over the break for years before he found her family:

> *"For a long time, I was perplexed by this for the Scottish custom was always to name the first born male John. After finding John Roe's family it's easy to understand how it came about for John and Clarissa named their children after Clarissa's brothers and sisters. **Guess who 'wore the pants' in that family when it came to naming children.**"*

Her brother **Lewis Roe** was born 23 August 1784. Her sister **Elizabeth** in 1788, her brother **Edward** in 1791, her brother **James** in 1797 — and John and Clarissa's own children include an Elizabeth, an Edward and a James. A century-old Scottish naming custom gave way to a New Jersey woman's family, and **[Lewis Fleming](/family/lewis-fleming/)** carried a Roe name into West Virginia because of it.

She **died about 1838**, after which her husband sold the eastern farm and moved west to Arnold's Creek. No death date, no burial place, and no birth record beyond the chart's precise 18 June 1782.

She is **Chuck's maternal 5×-great-grandmother**: Clarissa → [Lewis](/family/lewis-fleming/) → [Thomas Bailey Fleming](/family/thomas-bailey-fleming/) → [James Wesley](/family/wesley-fleming/) → [Sadye](/family/sadye-fleming-wildermuth/) → [Robert Earl](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/) → Terrie → Chuck.

## A date that was wrong, and one that still is

An [earlier chart](/archive/wildermuth-fleming-ancestor-chart-set/) gave her birth as **1762**, which made her the daughter of a father born in 1758. This archive flagged it as impossible. The [1998 chart](/archive/pedigree-charts-1998/) and the typescript both give **1782**, against her parents' 1779 marriage — consistent, and the 1762 is withdrawn.

Her mother's date is still broken. **Susannah Meeks** is entered on the 1998 chart as **born 1770**, which would make her nine at her own marriage and twelve at Clarissa's birth. Something nearer 1760 is meant. Recorded, not adopted.
