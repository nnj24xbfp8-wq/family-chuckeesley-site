Maria Margaretha Schmidgall was born **25 October 1789** in **Röhrach** (the small Württemberg village near Aspach, in what is today the Rems-Murr-Kreis of Baden-Württemberg). Her father [Georg Wilhelm Schmidgall](/family/georg-wilhelm-schmidgall/) was a Röhrach farmer; her mother [Anna Catherina Landai](/family/anna-catherina-landai/) of the same village.

She married [Johann Christian Wildermuth](/family/johann-christian-wildermuth/) of Großaspach on **25 April 1819** at **Rietenau**, a village a few kilometres away. She was twenty-nine, five years his elder. Over the next fifteen years she bore **seven children** &mdash; and buried three of them, at thirteen days, five years, and four weeks. The [Familienregister](/archive/wildermuth-familienregister-grossaspach/) sets the household out in full.

She died at **Großaspach on 30 December 1841**, age fifty-two. Johann Christian died **ten months later**, on 7 October 1842, and their twelve-year-old son [Johann Michael](/family/johann-michael-wildermuth/) was left an orphan; at sixteen he sailed for New York.

> *Two corrections sit behind that paragraph. This page previously gave the marriage as **25 April 1842 in "Ristenau"** and read it as a late-life match six months before her husband's death &mdash; a transposed digit, which also spawned a phantom first wife on [his page](/family/johann-christian-wildermuth/). And it gave her death as **1849**, following the GEDCOM; both the Familienregister and the [pastor's annotated chart](/archive/rielingshausen-parish-correspondence/) read **1841**, which is also the only version under which her son is the orphan every other record describes. Robert Earl's [memoir](/docs/robert-earl-wildermuth-little-histories/) prints 1947, a typo for one or the other.*

The "Little Histories" sketch is short and bare-fact &mdash; Robert Earl's typical entry for ancestors three or four generations behind the emigrant. The Röhrach farming-village origin is the deepest Württemberg place-name in the maternal-Wildermuth half of the line that does not retrace to Rielingshausen or Großaspach themselves.
