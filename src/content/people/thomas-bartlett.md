**Thomas Bartlett** is the earliest documented migrant in this archive — a generation ahead of the Wildermuths' Atlantic crossing and three-quarters of a century ahead of the Eesleys' move from Ontario into Michigan. He is Chuck's maternal **6×-great-grandfather** on the Fleming line.

Two independent bodies of evidence describe him, and they agree on the shape of his life and disagree on two specifics. Both are set out below.

## What the family's own record says

[Robert Earl Wildermuth](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/)'s typed sketch, from his research file of **11 July 1990**:

> *"Thomas Bartlett, one of seven sons of William Bartlett, came to Western Virginia shortly after the Revolutionary War. He was among the early pioneers west of the Alleghany Mountains. He settled in the Glades of what is now Preston County, West Virginia. … Thomas Bartlett died in Preston County, Virginia (West Virginia) in June 1836."*

His [Family Group Record (form 0094)](/archive/bartlett-family-group-record-0094/) leaves his birth blank, gives no parents at all, and enters **died and buried, June 1836, Preston County**.

## What the FamilySearch tree adds

The [family GEDCOM](/docs/dale-eesley-familysearch-tree/), synced from FamilySearch in June 2026, carries the same household under the name **Thomas L. Bartlett Jr.** and fills in what the family sheet left empty:

- **Born 21 December 1756** in **Fauquier County, Virginia**
- **Married Sarah about 1783** in **Fauquier County** — the Piedmont county east of the Blue Ridge
- **Died 1832** in **Preston County**
- **Buried at Union Baptist Church Cemetery, Simpson, Taylor County, West Virginia**

The Fauquier marriage is the most useful thing here, because it answers a question the sketch raises and does not settle: **where the family was before it crossed the mountains.** A wedding in Fauquier in 1783 and a farm in the Glades soon after is exactly the movement the sketch describes as *"shortly after the Revolutionary War"* — and it makes him about 27 at the crossing.

## Two open conflicts

**His father.** The sketch says **William Bartlett**, and *"one of seven sons."* FamilySearch says **Thomas Bartlett** (b. 1733, Richmond, Virginia) and **Anne Settle**, married 1748. Neither is a casual claim, and there is a detail pulling each way:

- For FamilySearch — he is entered as **"Jr.,"** which only makes sense with a father of the same name; and the FamilySearch sibship contains seven brothers besides him (William James, John B, Robert, Benjamin B, Richard "Dickey," Reuben, James F). A garbled memory of *"one of seven sons"* alongside a brother named **William** is an easy way to arrive at *"one of seven sons of William Bartlett."*
- For the sketch — it is the family's own record, written by a careful researcher who elsewhere marked his own uncertainty plainly (he could not resolve whether the Bailey grandfather's initials were J.B.Y. or I.B.Y., and said so).

The archive does not assign him a father until this is resolved. No William Bartlett page exists for that reason.

**His death year.** **June 1836** (family sheet) versus **1832** (FamilySearch). Both put it in Preston County. A probate or estate record would settle it; a Union Baptist Church Cemetery stone, if it survives, would settle it faster.

## The Glades

He settled in **the Glades** — the upland wet meadows of what was then far-western Virginia and is now **Preston County, West Virginia**. The name is local and specific: a belt of natural open grassland at high elevation, valuable to a settler because it needed no clearing.

Everything the family did afterwards happened within a short radius of it. His daughters married in **Monongalia** and **Preston** Counties; the Baileys who married into the family are recorded in **Monongalia, Harrison, Taylor and Preston** — the whole cluster inside the northern Virginia panhandle that separated to become West Virginia in **1863**, a generation after his death.

## Wife and children

He married **[Sarah](/family/sarah-bartlett-wife/)**, whose surname was already lost by the time the family's genealogy was written down; the typed sketch renders her *"Sarah ( )"*, with the parenthesis left open, and FamilySearch does no better — it lists her under her married name.

They had **thirteen children**:

1. **[Nancy](/family/nancy-bartlett/)** — married **[Thomas Bailey](/family/thomas-bailey/)**, October 1803, Monongalia County. *Chuck's line.*
2. **Elizabeth** — married David Cleavenger
3. **Catherine** — married Joab Woolverton
4. **Edith** — married a Hopkins
5. **Mary "Polly"** — married **Thornsberry Bailey**, 24 August 1806, in the Glades
6. **Sarah** — married George Brown
7. **Starling**
8. **Charlotte** — married John Maulsby
9. **Amelia** — married the same John Maulsby, after Charlotte's death
10. **Benjamin**
11. **William**
12. **Elijah**
13. **Thomas L.**

Two patterns are worth naming. **Charlotte and Amelia married the same man**, Amelia stepping in after her sister died — a common and practical arrangement in frontier households with children already in them. And **two Bartlett sisters married two Baileys** three years apart, Nancy in 1803 and Mary in 1806. Mary's husband cannot be his own nephew Thornsberry Bailey Jr., who was born in August 1804; FamilySearch dates that Thornberry Bailey's birth to **1782** and his death to **24 January 1853** in Taylor County, which makes him a near-contemporary of Thomas Bailey (b. c. 1780). The likeliest reading is that they were **brothers**, both sons of [Hannah Thornsberry](/family/hannah-thornsberry/) — but neither source states it.

## The line forward

Nancy's daughter **[Synthia Bailey](/family/synthia-bailey/)** — born in Preston County, died 1849 in Harrison County — married **[Lewis B Fleming](/family/lewis-fleming/)** in Harrison County in 1827, and the descent runs from there: Synthia's son **[Thomas Bailey Fleming](/family/thomas-bailey-fleming/)** (b. 1830) → **[James Wesley Fleming](/family/wesley-fleming/)** (1855) → **[Sadye Fleming Wildermuth](/family/sadye-fleming-wildermuth/)** → the Wildermuths of Marietta.

## What is open

His father. His death year. Sarah's surname. And Robert Earl's own closing line is still the best lead anyone has: *"Little research has been done on this ancestor but the Bartlett records are said to be better than average."*
