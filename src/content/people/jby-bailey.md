The deepest **Bailey** ancestor the record reaches, and Chuck's maternal **6×-great-grandfather** on the Fleming line. He was the husband of **[Hannah Thornsberry](/family/hannah-thornsberry/)** and the father of **[Thomas Bailey](/family/thomas-bailey/)**, who married [Nancy Bartlett](/family/nancy-bartlett/) in Monongalia County in 1803.

## A man with a birthday and no name

The [1998 pedigree chart](/archive/pedigree-charts-1998/) gives him a date: **born 26 May 1732**. Precise to the day — which normally means somebody had a Bible page, a register entry, or a gravestone.

And in the same box, in the field where the name goes, it still reads:

> **I.B.Y. OR J.B.Y. BAILEY**

That is how it is printed. Robert Earl had been at this since the 1970s; by 1998 he had a birth date accurate to the day and **still could not tell whether the first initial was an I or a J**, so he typed both into the name field and left it. Eight years of the record and he would not guess.

It is a small thing and a characteristic one. The same hand that [caught an impossible birthplace](/archive/thomas-bailey-fleming-biographical-sketch/) in a census, and that wrote *"no death record could be found for Thomas"* rather than inventing one, declined to pick a letter.

*(The two are probably not different names anyway: in eighteenth-century hands, capital **I** and **J** are frequently the same stroke. Whatever document he was reading likely could not distinguish them either.)*

> *A candidate for future research: the initials, and any first name behind them, are unresolved. The precision of the 26 May 1732 date suggests a source that was never cited.*
