For thirty-odd years this archive carried a sentence it could not explain. [Robert Earl Wildermuth](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/) closed the German travelogue in his [1989 memoir](/docs/robert-earl-wildermuth-memoir/) with the research he still meant to finish:

> *"I hope to return to the home towns of grandma Catherina Boeshar and hopefully that of **Johann Peter Schlicher** at some later date."*

Nobody knew who Johann Peter Schlicher *was*. [Theobald's page](/family/theobald-david-schlicher/) recorded the problem honestly and left it open — same man, father, or earlier ancestor, no way to tell.

**Robert Earl's own [printed ancestor charts](/archive/wildermuth-fleming-ancestor-chart-set/) make him Theobald's father, of Adenbach.** The answer was sitting in his own papers.

**Read that with the caution it deserves.** The charts are computer output from whatever database he kept, not a document — and this archive's rule is that where a chart meets a record, the record wins. On this branch there is no record to test it against: no parish register, no passenger list, no Marietta burial yet found. The charts are also undated beyond "c. early 1990s," so even the appealing story — that he cracked it after the 1989 memoir and never told anyone — is a guess. He may equally have had it from a correspondent, or from a published descendancy, before he ever wrote that sentence.

What follows is therefore **the best current reading of one unverified source**, set down so the next person has somewhere to start.

## Adenbach

**Adenbach** is a very small place — an *Ortsgemeinde* of **under two hundred people** (191 at the 1994 count) in the **Odenbach valley**, in the North Palatine Uplands of the Western Palatinate, in what is today the **Landkreis Kusel** of Rhineland-Palatinate. Its whole municipal area is under three square kilometres.

When Johann Peter was born there in **1797**, the left bank of the Rhine was **under French occupation** — the formal annexation came in 1801. The Congress of Vienna awarded the Palatinate to the **Kingdom of Bavaria**, and the transfer took effect in **1816** under the Treaty of Munich, when he was nineteen. Adenbach thereafter belonged to the Bavarian *Rheinkreis* — which is why his descendants in Ohio would describe the family as coming from Bavaria.

That detail matters for reading the American records. The Schlichers were **Palatines** who were **Bavarian subjects**, and both words are true.

## The two hometowns are in the same district

If the charts are right, here is the part Robert Earl would have enjoyed most, and may never have noticed.

The two German home towns he named in one breath in 1989 — *"grandma Catherina Boeshar and … Johann Peter Schlicher"* — are **in the same district**. [Catharina Boeshar](/family/catharina-boeshar-wildermuth/) was born at **Breitenbach** and her mother [Maria Margaretha Jung](/family/maria-margaretha-jung/) at **Frohnhofen**, three and a half kilometres apart. Adenbach lies at the northern end of the **same Landkreis Kusel**, up in the Odenbach valley.

Two entirely separate immigrant lines, married into each other in Marietta, Ohio — [Catharina Boeshar](/family/catharina-boeshar-wildermuth/) to [Johann Michael Wildermuth](/family/johann-michael-wildermuth/), and this family's [Flora](/family/flora-schlicher-wildermuth/) to their son [William Clifford](/family/william-wildermuth/) — and both of them came out of the same corner of the Palatinate. Robert Earl's grandson's archive can now say that the Kusel district appears **twice** in his ancestry, from two directions.

*(The Wildermuths proper are the exception: they came from **Großaspach in Württemberg**, some 250 km southeast. It is the Palatine lines — Boeshar, Jung, Schlicher — that converge on Kusel.)*

## Marietta, and 1861

He married **[Maria Magdalina Weyrich](/family/maria-magdalina-weyrich/)** (b. 1807), and their son **[Theobald "David" Schlicher](/family/theobald-david-schlicher/)** was born **21 February 1835** — in Germany, which fixes the family as still at home that year. By **20 January 1861** Theobald was in Washington County, Ohio, marrying [Eva Barbara Schmidt](/family/eva-schmidt-schlicher/). So **the Schlicher emigration falls between 1835 and 1861**, in the same mid-century German wave that carried the [Boeshars](/family/catharina-boeshar-wildermuth/) — also Palatines — and the Bavarian [Schmidts](/family/john-jacob-schmidt/) to the Ohio Valley.

Johann Peter **died at Marietta in 1861** — the same year his son married. The charts give no month, so whether he lived to see the January wedding is unknown. He was about sixty-four, and he had crossed an ocean to die in an Ohio river town. Whether he travelled with the family or followed them is not recorded; nothing in this archive places his wife [Maria Magdalina](/family/maria-magdalina-weyrich/) in America at all.

His own parents, per the same chart, were **Johann Friedrich Schlicher** and **Maria Elizabeth Burgarth** — recorded by name only, with no dates, and not given pages here until something more than a name turns up.

He is **Chuck's maternal four-times-great-grandfather**: Johann Peter → [Theobald David Schlicher](/family/theobald-david-schlicher/) → [Flora Schlicher Wildermuth](/family/flora-schlicher-wildermuth/) → [Earl Adam Wildermuth](/family/earl-a-wildermuth/) → [Robert Earl Wildermuth](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/) → [Terrie](/family/terrie-lee-eesley/) → Chuck.

## Still open

The charts name the village but prove nothing about it. What would:

- **The Adenbach parish registers.** Adenbach was overwhelmingly **Evangelical** (145 of 191 inhabitants as late as 1994), so the Protestant church books for Adenbach or the mother parish at **Odenbach** are where an 1797 baptism would sit.
- **A Bavarian emigration file.** Leaving the Rheinkreis required permission, exactly as it did in Württemberg — the same kind of record the [Ludwigsburg lead](/archive/stadt-marbach-reply-1986/) opened on the Wildermuth side. For the Palatinate the equivalent holdings are at the **Landesarchiv Speyer**.
- **A Marietta burial.** He died there in 1861; a stone or a sexton's record would give the month and confirm the year.
