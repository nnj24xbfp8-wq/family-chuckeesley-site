Born **4 February 1889 in Marietta, Ohio**, the **eldest son** of [William Clifford Wildermuth](/family/william-wildermuth/) and [Flora Schlicher](/family/flora-schlicher-wildermuth/) — and **second-eldest of their nine children** (after Mae Clara, b. 1886). Visible in the **[c. 1896 family group portrait](/archive/william-wildermuth-family-group-portrait/)** at about age seven — most likely the small boy in dark clothes in the front row. **Older brother of [Earl Adam](/family/earl-a-wildermuth/)** (b. 1899) — making Charles Daniel **Robert Earl Wildermuth's paternal uncle**.

## The Kansas move

Of the nine William-Clifford-and-Flora children, **Charles is the one who left Ohio for the West**. He died **10 October 1945 in Kansas** at age **56** — just weeks after the Japanese surrender that ended WWII — and was buried at **Hoisington Cemetery in Hoisington, Barton County, Kansas**. **Hoisington** is a small farming town in central Kansas, the kind of place a Marietta-born midwesterner could have ended up after working a railroad or wheat-belt career.

He is the **"Charlie"** of his brother [Earl Adam Wildermuth's 17 January 1972 family-history letter](/docs/letters/earl-adam-to-robert-earl-1972-01-17/), the only C-name son among the nine siblings. Earl Adam recorded the Kansas chapter from family memory:

> *Bob your Uncle Charlie met his wife in Wichita Kans. She was a Marietta girl but went to Wichita with the people Charlie worked for she was their hired girl then they lived in Wichita thats where Charlie died and was burried.*

Earl Adam's account places the marriage and death at **Wichita**, and remembers the wife as a **Marietta girl who had gone to Wichita as a family's hired girl**; the GEDCOM instead records the burial at Hoisington (about 90 miles northwest of Wichita) and names the wife as **Eva "Effie" Barbara Schmidt**. The two sources may describe the same Kansas life from different angles — a Wichita marriage and a later Hoisington burial — or the family memory may simply have blurred the central-Kansas geography. Children and the date and circumstances of the move are queued research.
