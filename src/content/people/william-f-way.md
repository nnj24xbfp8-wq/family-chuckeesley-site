William F. Way is the **earliest documented ancestor on the Way line** &mdash; Chuck's maternal **five-times-great-grandfather**, and the man who brought the Way name across the Atlantic.

## The 1820 crossing

Per the biographical sketch preserved in [Robert Earl Wildermuth's genealogy](/docs/wildermuth-family-of-marietta-book/) (which quotes the 1887 *History of Noble County, Ohio*), William F. Way was **born in England** and **came to the United States in 1820 with his family &mdash; his wife and two children, a son and a daughter.** He **landed at Norfolk, Virginia**, and from there made his way to **Duck Creek, Washington County, Ohio**, where he followed his trade &mdash; **blacksmithing** &mdash; before a final move across to **Wood County, Virginia** (now West Virginia), where he **died in 1846.** The county history remembers him as *"an honest, industrious man of unquestioned integrity, and highly esteemed for his sturdy manhood."*

His wife was **Harriet Rowlands**. The son who crossed with them in 1820 was **[Edward Taylor Way](/family/edward-taylor-way/)** &mdash; born on the **Isle of Wight in 1812**, so about eight years old at the crossing &mdash; who grew up poor near Crooked Tree, built a 600-acre Noble County farm, and fathered the line that runs through [Edward E. Way](/family/edward-e-way/) and [Victoria Anna Way Davis](/family/victoria-anna-way-davis/) down to Chuck. The daughter who came with them is named only as "a daughter" in the record.

## What's open

- **William's given-name middle initial "F.", his birth date and English birthplace** (the Isle-of-Wight birth of his son suggests the family's English home was on or near the island).
- **Harriet Rowlands's dates and family**, and the identity of the daughter who emigrated with them in 1820.

> *Source: [Wildermuth Family of Marietta, Ohio](/docs/wildermuth-family-of-marietta-book/) (Wildermuth & Bain), the "Way, Edward T., I" section, quoting the biographical sketch of William F. Way in the 1887 *History of Noble County, Ohio* (Noble County Historical Society, pp. 558&ndash;559).*
