A printed biographical sketch of **[John F. Eesley](/family/john-f-eesley/)** — "Businessmen of Plainwell, as of other enterprising towns, are its bone and sinew" — from a Michigan county history of the sort sold by subscription in the 1890s. It is the **earliest narrative source** this archive holds on the Plainwell miller, and it is contemporary with him: written while he was operating the mill, in the present tense.

## What it adds

**The journeyman years.** He learned the miller's trade under his father, then worked it across five states before owning anything: **Merrill Milling Company** at Plainwell from spring 1881 (about eighteen months), **Lockport, Illinois** (about twelve months), then **Eufaula, Alabama**, **Huntington, Indiana**, and **Coshocton** and **Frazeysburg, Ohio** — returning to Plainwell in **1887** *"determined to make that city his permanent abiding place."* The 1887 mill purchase was a **return**, not a first arrival.

**The Goldie millwrights.** Of his mother's family the sketch says: *"For two hundred years or more the male portion of the Goldie family have been millwrights."* [Jennie Goldie](/family/jeanie-goldie/) was born in **Ayr, Scotland**, and came to America in **1844**, settling at Montreal. This locates the deeper origin of the family trade on the **maternal** side.

**The father.** [Alfred Robert Eesley](/family/albert-robert-eesley/) — the sketch spells the first name *Alfred*, where the cemetery and tree records give *Albert* — born at **Stafford, Warwickshire, England**, came to the New World in **1856**, milled for several years, and at the time of writing was **still in the trade at Birmingham, Oakland County, Michigan**; a Baptist and "one of the prominent and influential men in his County."

**Schooling.** Village schools of **Plottsville, Ontario**; then **New York City** during a period when the family lived there; then **Birmingham, Michigan** after the move south.

**The mill as of writing.** "The only steam mill in Plainwell, and it is equipped with all the modern appliances by which he can turn out flour in a remarkably short time." Products: flour, feed, buckwheat flour, "and supplies an increasing demand at home and in the neighboring towns."

**The marriage and the man.** *"Miss Belle Scott became the wife of our subject in 1885"* — a native of **Gun Plain Township**, Allegan County, daughter of **Henry R. and Eliza Scott**, natives of the Empire State. He *"votes the Prohibition ticket,"* was a **Knight of the Maccabees**, and belonged to the **Baptist** society.

## Cautions

Subscription county histories were **flattering by commission** — subjects typically paid for inclusion, and the closing line here ("we are pleased to be able to present to our readers a sketch of so worthy a gentleman") is the genre's house style. The factual spine — dates, places, employers — is generally reliable and checkable; the estimations of character are advertising.

Two details conflict with other records and are worth noting rather than resolving here: the **father's given name** (*Alfred* here, *Albert* on the Find a Grave and tree records), and the family size — the sketch says **"eldest of a family of ten children, all of whom are living,"** while the cemetery record lists John plus eight siblings.
