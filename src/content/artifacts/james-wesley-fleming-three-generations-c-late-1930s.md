An outdoor group photograph of **three generations of the Fleming family**, c. late 1930s &mdash; the aging patriarch [James Wesley Fleming](/family/wesley-fleming/) standing at the center in a dark suit, flanked by two of his grown daughters, each with a young daughter of her own in front. It is among the **earliest photographs in the maternal Fleming line** held in this archive, and it catches Wesley near the very end of his life.

## Who is in the frame

- **[James Wesley Fleming](/family/wesley-fleming/)** (1855&ndash;1940) &mdash; center, the patriarch; Chuck's maternal great-great-grandfather, father of [Sadye Fleming Wildermuth](/family/sadye-fleming-wildermuth/). His identity is confirmed by comparison with his [standing portrait](/family/wesley-fleming/) already in the archive: the same prominent nose, gaunt angular face, and mustache, here twenty-odd years older and frailer.
- **[Etha May (Fleming) Becker](/family/etha-may-fleming-becker/)** (b. 1904) &mdash; back left, Wesley's daughter and Sadye's younger sister; Chuck's great-grandaunt.
- **[Mary Evelyn (Becker) Mitchell](/family/mary-evelyn-becker-mitchell/)** (b. c. 1929&ndash;1931) &mdash; front left, Etha's daughter and Wesley's granddaughter.

**Still to identify:** the **woman at back right** is almost certainly another of Wesley's daughters &mdash; one of Etha's thirteen siblings, perhaps her twin Diatha, or Della, Nora, Mary, or LaVerna &mdash; and the **girl at front right** is that woman's own daughter, another Fleming granddaughter. Both are open for family confirmation.

## Dating

Wesley Fleming was born in December 1855 and **died 2 October 1940 in Marietta**. He reads here as a very elderly, frail man &mdash; markedly older than in his earlier standing portrait &mdash; while his daughter [Etha](/family/etha-may-fleming-becker/) (b. 1904) appears to be in her early-to-mid thirties and the granddaughters are small children. Together those place the photograph in the **second half of the 1930s**, in the last years of the patriarch's life. His wife [Verona](/family/verona-sheppard-fleming/) had died in 1922, so by this gathering Wesley was long a widower, surrounded instead by his daughters and their children.
