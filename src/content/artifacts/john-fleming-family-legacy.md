Not a chart. **Seven pages of continuous prose**, generation by generation, each headed with the man's name and his exact relationship — *5th Great-grandfather*, *4th Great-grandfather* — and running from an emigrant weaver in the 1710s down to Chuck's great-great-grandfather. It reads like the [1988 Little Histories](/docs/robert-earl-wildermuth-little-histories/) grown up: sourced, argued, occasionally funny, and willing to say *"I have some reservations"* and *"more research needs to be done here."*

It is the companion text to the [1998 pedigree charts](/archive/pedigree-charts-1998/), and it is where he put the reasoning the charts only assert.

## It settles the parentage of Lewis Fleming

For years this archive named Lewis's father **Edward Fleming**, of the Simpson Creek farm, on the authority of Joy Gilchrist's *The Squires Family* — and cited an 1827 note in which *"Edward"* let his son *"go out from under my house."*

**Page 5 quotes that note in full, and it is not Edward's.**

> *"In 1827, Lewis gained his father's permission to marry Synthia Bailey, daughter of Thomas and Nancy (Bartlett) Bailey. In a note to the 'Clearke' of Harrison County, **John Fleming** wrote the following:*
>
> ***'This is to certify that I, John Fleming have nothing against my son giting married, going from under my house this 14 day 1827 in the year of our Lord. Mr. John Fleming'.***
>
> *Shortly thereafter, Lewis gained the permission of Synthia's father, Thomas Bailey, Lewis and Synthia were married 14 March 1827."*

The signature is there twice. **Lewis Fleming's father was John Fleming.**

**And the Edward is accounted for.** Page 4 lists John Fleming IV's thirteen children, and among them: **Edward Fleming, born 2 July 1817, married Mary Turner** — *Lewis's younger brother*, ten years his junior. A real Edward Fleming on the same farm in the same county, one generation displaced. That is more than enough to produce the misattribution the archive inherited.

The Simpson Creek detail survives intact; only the name attached to it was wrong. Lewis was *"born circa 1807 on his father's farm on Simpson's Creek in the eastern sector of Harrison County."*

## The line, in his own words

**John Fleming Sr.** — *6th great-grandfather.* Came to **Cecil County, Maryland in 1712**, *"from either Scotland or Ireland but he was of Scottish origin."* Wife **Jean**, maiden name unknown. A **weaver by trade** — and he explains why that mattered:

> *"Scottish woven fabrics were much wanted throughout Europe and that is one reason for a large emigration of Scots. The King of England had placed an embargo on Scottish woolens that demanded that all woven materials be sent to London where a pitifully small price was paid."*

He found the man's **will**. Family tradition said he died at "Derry, Pennsylvania"; Robert Earl spent a long time hunting a *town* called Derry before realising it was a **township** — Derry Township, then Lancaster County, **now Dauphin County** — where John Sr. appears on the **tax rolls of 1754, 1755 and 1756**. An archivist in Chester County found the will from the known death date. **Probated 17 May 1777.** Seven children named, with spouses. Of a supposed son Samuel he notes: *"I have some reservations … because John Sr. never mentioned him in his Will."*

*Note the qualification:* **"either Scotland or Ireland."** His own [Chart 4](/archive/pedigree-charts-1998/) prints **Ballyclare, Antrim County, Ireland** as a flat fact. The prose is more honest than the form, and the prose should govern.

**John Fleming Jr.** — *5th great-grandfather.* Born **Cecil County 1714**, lived his whole life in **Frederick County, Maryland (now Montgomery)**. A **prosperous tobacco grower** with a large plantation and smaller farms near a trading post called **Cabin John**, on ground *"near what is now Washington, D.C. but there was no Washington D.C. then."* Married **Anne White**. In **1778 he and his sons John III and James took Maryland's Oath of Fidelity and Support** — which, he notes with evident satisfaction, *"made them all sworn patriots and is the basis for all female descendents to become members of the Daughters of the American Revolution."* **Died Christmas Day 1796, aged 82; will probated 16 January 1797.**

**Robert White** — *5th great-grandfather*, *"the other patriarch of our line."* A prosperous tobacco grower of **Prince Georges County**. Married **Anne Burgess** in Anne Arundel County, **22 September 1709**. Five children. **Will probated 11 April 1769.** *(The chart says he died April 1768; the probate date here implies 1769.)*

**John Fleming III** — *4th great-grandfather.* Born **c. 1744, Frederick County**. Married **Nancy Ann Hopkins** 20 November 1777. In **1781** he moved with his father-in-law and *"several of the Wallaces and Whites"* to **Fallowfield Township, Washington County, Pennsylvania** — *"Quite a move for a one year old son."* He and John Hopkins served in the **5th Battalion, Washington County Militia**, under **Colonel Thomas Crooks**, company commander **Benjamin White**. **Died 7 July 1829, Pike Run Township; will probated 10 August 1829.**

> *A note on this page.* Most of it is a long, graphic account of frontier warfare on the Ohio — bounties paid for scalps, and one atrocity described at length. It is written in the language of the period he was describing rather than the period he was writing in, and it treats the Wyandotte only as perpetrators. This archive records that the passage exists and what militia service it documents, and does not reproduce it.

**John Hopkins** — *5th great-grandfather.* **Came from Scotland** to Montgomery County, Maryland **about 1775**. Married **Eleanor Wallace, daughter of James Wallace**. Seven children, one of them *"Richard Hopkins believed killed by the Indians."* Died **circa 1796**.

**John Roe** — *4th great-grandfather*, and the best story in the seven pages. Born **Morris County, New Jersey c. 1760**. **Enlisted in the Revolutionary Army in 1779 and stayed for the duration** — **Captain Richard Cox's Company, Colonel Dayton's Third New Jersey Regiment**. And:

> *"While bivouaced near the British lines in Westfield, Essex County, New Jersey, John met and married Miss Susannah Meeks on 27 March 1779. She stayed behind to care for her invalid mother while John went off to fight the British."*

He fought at **Springfield, New Jersey**, and was **under Lafayette at Jamestown, Virginia** when Cornwallis surrendered. Then Westfield until 1784, **New York City until 1790**, and out to **Fayette County, Pennsylvania** — *"three young children under the age of 8 and a pregnant wife journied that long, tortous route through the forests and over the Allegheny Mountains."* **Died 29 September 1829** in the Eastern District of Harrison County.

**John Fleming IV** — *3rd great-grandfather.* Born **Montgomery County, Maryland, 11 October 1781**; **raised in Washington County, Pennsylvania**; married **Clarissa Roe 20 January 1803** in the Eastern District of Harrison County. He was an **assistant surveyor for his cousin James Fleming**, a Revolutionary officer — *"This surveying experience enabled John IV to aquire many acres of land and in his latter days he was known as a land baron."*

About **1838** Clarissa died; he sold the eastern farm to sons **John V. and Patrick** and moved west to what became Doddridge County, where **Lewis was already settled**. In **1858** he married **Susannah Prunty**, kin to the founders of Pruntytown. **Died at his farm on Arnold's Creek, about six miles west of West Union, 3 November 1853.**

**Thirteen children**, including Lewis (1807), the twins **John and Patrick** (both 23 Nov 1813), **Edward** (1817), and **Thomas and Clarissa** (both 4 Aug 1823).

## Why the eldest son was not called John

He noticed the break himself, and worked out why:

> *"Lewis Fleming represents a break in the long line of first born males named John. For a long time, I was perplexed by this for the Scottish custom was always to name the first born male John. After finding John Roe's family it's easy to understand how it came about for John and Clarissa named their children after Clarissa's brothers and sisters. **Guess who 'wore the pants' in that family when it came to naming children.**"*

**Clarissa's brother was Lewis Roe**, born 23 August 1784. Five generations of Johns, and the chain breaks because a woman from New Jersey named her children after her own family.

## The Bailey and Bartlett pages

**Thomas Bartlett** married **Sarah** and had **thirteen children**, all named here with their spouses — including **Nancy** (m. Thomas Bailey) and **Mary Polly** (m. Thornberry Bailey), *two Bartlett sisters marrying two Bailey brothers*, and **Charlotte** and then **Amelia** both marrying **John Maulsby**, Amelia *"after her sister Charlotte's death."*

**J.B.Y. Bailey** born **26 May 1732**, married **Hannah Thornberry**, *"born in 1728 and died in 1831 at the advanced age of 103."* Stated outright, and followed by *"More research needs to be done here."*

**Two research leads he left:**

- A chancery file at the **Taylor County Court House in Grafton, box 2** — a lawsuit ***"Bailey vs. Bailey."***
- A book, ***"Bailey Genealogy,"*** in the **History and Genealogical Library, Clarksburg, West Virginia**.

| Mary Lake's three children | named: **Granville, Martha, William** | unnamed |

*The Martha Turner year is a straight conflict — 1903 here, 1904 on his own chart. Neither is sourced. Unresolved.*

*Two internal slips worth knowing about:* the Thomas Bailey section opens *"Thomas Bailey born 1830"* (impossible — he married in 1803) and says he married *"Sarah Bartlett"* before naming his wife as **Nancy Bartlett** four lines later. The child lists elsewhere make it plain: **Nancy** married Thomas Bailey; **Sarah** married George Brown. Typing errors, not new facts.
