This is the document all the others were copied from — and the archive has been working from its descendants for years without having seen it.

A printed bilingual **Ahnentafel** form, headed **"Ahnentafel Wildermuth Großaspach"**, filled out by hand in a German script and dated **2 December 1988**. Its source line is the important part:

> **Bemerkungen u. Quellen / REMARKS AND SOURCES:**
> *Evangelische Kirchenbücher von Großaspach, Rietenau*
> *(the Protestant parish registers of Großaspach and Rietenau)*

Somebody in Germany went to the church books and wrote the family out. This is the *"positive answer"* [Robert Earl](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/) [mentioned getting from Großaspach](/archive/little-histories-original-pages/), and it is the origin of the [Little Histories](/docs/robert-earl-wildermuth-little-histories/) he began writing that same month — his census notes are dated **December 1988** too.

## What it settles

**Three trades, in the German original.**

- **Andreas Wildermuth — *Weingärtner*, Rielingshausen.** Not *Gärtner*. A **vine-dresser**, written by a German compiler reading a German register. The archive's long-standing "Gartner (vineyard gardener)" was a good gloss on an imprecise word; this is the word.
- **Johann Georg Wolf — *Schuhmacher in Großaspach*.** The shoemaking trade, in the parish register, a generation before the Wildermuths took it up. Confirms what [his sketch](/archive/little-histories-original-pages/) said in English and settles that [the trade came in through the Wolf line](/family/johann-georg-wolf/).
- **Adam Wildermuth — *Weber*.** Weaver, matching the [Familienregister](/archive/wildermuth-familienregister-grossaspach/) exactly.

Also **Georg Wilhelm Schmidgall — *Bauer*** (farmer) at Röhrach, **Jacob Schmidgall — *Bauer* at Großhöchberg**, and **Joseph Landai — *Taglöhner*** (day labourer) at Röhrach. Farmer, farmer, day labourer, vine-dresser, weaver, shoemaker: six trades across four generations, all of them in villages within about twenty kilometres of each other.

**Two dates the archive had wrong, now confirmed from the registers.**

- **Johann Christian's marriage: 25 April 1819 at Rietenau.** Not 1842, not "Ristenau." Third independent confirmation, and the strongest.
- **Maria Margaretha's death: 30 December 1841.** Not 1849 (the GEDCOM), not 1847 (his own sketch). This is the reading that makes [Johann Michael](/family/johann-michael-wildermuth/) an orphan at twelve.
- And **Adam's marriage: 25 October 1791**, not the 1797 his sketch gave.

## Why Andreas is blank

Row 8 carries his name and his trade and **not one date**. The German compiler working the Großaspach and Rietenau registers in 1988 could go no further back on the Wildermuth male line, because **Andreas belonged to Rielingshausen** — a different parish, in a different district.

That is exactly what Robert Earl wrote at the time: *"I have not attempted to find anything out about Andreus … I am sure that Andreus records are in the Protestant Church Books in the town of Rielingshausen."* He was right, and it took him until about 1992 — and [a letter to that parish](/archive/rielingshausen-parish-correspondence/) — to fill the row in. The blank line here is the shape of the gap he then went and closed.

## Small discrepancies, left open

The handwriting is confident but the photograph is a photograph, and a few digits are readings rather than certainties:

- **Adam's birth** looks like **26.10.1769**; the archive and the GEDCOM have **1768**, and Robert Earl's own 1989 chart has 28 October 1768. Three values for one day.
- **Balthus Wolf's marriage** reads **27.1.1733** here (27 January, in German day-first order) against **27 July 1733** in the sketch — the kind of slip a reader unused to German date order would make.
- **Magdalena Übele's birth** could be 1711 or 1717; her sketch says 1717.
- **Joseph Landai's marriage** reads **23.1.1744**; his sketch says 2 April 1744 and the 1989 chart 2 August 1744.
- **Johann Georg Wolf's birthplace** is written **Rohrbach** while the rest of the family is **Röhrach** — possibly the same village, possibly not.

None of these touch the direct line's structure. They are noted so that whoever holds the sheet next can settle them from the paper rather than from a photograph.
