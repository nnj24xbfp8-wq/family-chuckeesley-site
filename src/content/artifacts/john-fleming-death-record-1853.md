A one-page abstract, and the only **civil record** of [John Fleming IV](/family/john-fleming-iv/) this archive holds. It matters for two reasons: it confirms the man, and it explains where one of the archive's stubborn wrong facts came from.

Fleming, born Hampshire County Virginia, consort of Ann Fleming, informant Eli B. Fleming, son — with a footnote reading that the starred information is in error from other records and sources.](../../assets/family/originals/john-fleming-death-record-1853.jpeg)

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Name in Full | John Fleming |
| Date of Death | **3 November 1853** |
| Place of Death | **Arnold's Creek, Dodderidge County, Va.** |
| Cause of Death | Not Known |
| Years of Age | 73\* |
| Name of Parents | John & A. Fleming\* |
| Where Born | Hampshire County, Virginia\* |
| Consort of | Ann Fleming\* |
| Person Giving Info | **Eli B. Fleming** |
| Designation of Informant | **Son** |

> *\* This information is in error from other records and sources.* — Robert Earl's own footnote, and the asterisks are his.

## What it confirms

**The informant was Eli B. Fleming, son.** [John Fleming IV's child list](/family/john-fleming-iv/) includes **Eli B. Fleming, born 28 February 1831** — and here he is, twenty-two years old, giving the county the particulars of his father's death. That is an independent link between the death and the family, from a contemporary record rather than a chart.

**The place and date match exactly** what the [Family Legacy typescript](/docs/john-fleming-family-legacy/) says: died at his farm on **Arnold's Creek** on **3 November 1853**. The typescript adds "about six miles west of West Union."

## What it explains

**This is the source of "Hampshire County."** [An earlier boxed chart](/archive/wildermuth-fleming-ancestor-chart-set/) gave John Fleming's birthplace as **Hampshire County, Virginia**, and the 1998 chart contradicted it with **Montgomery County, Maryland**. This archive recorded that as an unexplained inconsistency between two of his own charts.

It was not an inconsistency — it was a **source disagreement he had already resolved.** The death register says Hampshire County; he starred it as erroneous and used Montgomery County, Maryland instead. The early chart followed the record; the later one followed his conclusion.

**"Name of Parents: John & A. Fleming"** is worth noting on its own. In 1853 the family told the county that John's parents were a John and an "A." — which is exactly the four-Johns tradition that Josephine Fleming and Mike Roberts later gave him, and which he [retracted in November 1998](/archive/fleming-retraction-1998/). So the tradition was already in the family in 1853, a century and a half before he tested it. That does not make it true. It does show it was not invented by twentieth-century hobbyists.

## What is wrong with it, and why that is normal

Four of the eight substantive fields are starred as erroneous. Nothing unusual in that: a death register records **what a bereaved twenty-two-year-old said at the courthouse**, not what a researcher can verify.

- **Age 73** implies a birth about 1780; the charts and typescript give **11 October 1781**, which would make him 72.
- **"Consort of Ann Fleming"** names no wife this archive knows. His first wife was [Clarissa Roe](/family/clarissa-roe/), who died about 1838; the typescript gives a later wife as **Susannah Prunty**. Robert Earl [also came to think there was a third](/archive/fleming-dear-cousins-1998/) — *"Old John (1781) must have married three times."* An "Ann" may be one of them, or may be Eli's confusion with his grandmother **Nancy Ann**.
