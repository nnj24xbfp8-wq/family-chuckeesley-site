Three records from Washington County that turn [Johann Michael Wildermuth](/family/johann-michael-wildermuth/) from an immigrant in someone else's household into a householder of his own.

## The marriage application, 3 September 1862

![A photocopy of a handwritten Washington County marriage application of 3 September 1862, in which John M. Wildermuth of Marietta City swears to the ages of himself and Catharine Boeshar, that neither has a living spouse, and that they are not nearer of kin than first cousins, signed by him and sworn before the Probate Judge.](../../assets/family/originals/john-michael-wildermuth-marriage-application-1862.jpeg)

**DOCUMENT #58.** *John M. Wildermuth of Marietta City* comes before the Probate Judge and swears the standard three things: his own age, that **Catharine Boeshar** *"has no husband living,"* that he *"has no wife living,"* and that the two *"are not nearer of kin than first cousins."* **Subscribed and sworn 3 September 1862**, signed in his own hand — *John M. Wildermuth*, the American form of the name he had been using since at least the 1853 petition.

## The marriage, 7 September 1862

![A photocopy of a Washington County marriage record certification, volume 3 page 505, certifying that on the 7th day of September 1862 the minister joined in matrimony John Michael Wildermuth and Catharine Boeshar by virtue of a license, recorded 1 December 1862 and attested by the Probate Judge.](../../assets/family/originals/john-michael-wildermuth-marriage-record-certification-1862.jpeg)

**DOCUMENT #59**, Marriage Record Certification, **Volume 3, page 505**:

> *"This is to certify that on the 7th day of Sept 1862, I joined in matrimony **John Michael Wildermuth & Catharine Boeshar** by virtue of a license for that purpose."* — signed by the officiating minister, recorded 1 December 1862, attested by the Probate Judge.

Four days between application and wedding. Note which name he gave the court: the application is signed **John M.**, but the marriage entry records him as **John Michael Wildermuth** — the German form surfacing for the one occasion that warranted it.

## The 1870 census — Third Ward, Marietta

![A typed transcription of page 5 of the 1870 federal census schedule for the Third Ward of Marietta, Washington County, Ohio, enumerated 6 July 1870, listing John Wildermuth aged 39, work in shoe shop, born Wurtemburg; Catherine 30, keeping house, born Bavaria; John C. 7, at school, born Ohio; and William C. 4, at home, born Ohio.](../../assets/family/originals/john-michael-wildermuth-1870-census-marietta.jpeg)

**DOCUMENT 5**, page 5, Schedule 1 — *Inhabitants in Third Ward, Marietta*, enumerated **6 July 1870** by Asst. Marshal Joseph Kelly:

| Name | Age | | Occupation | Birthplace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wildermuth, John | 39 | M W | Work in shoe shop | **Wurtemburg** |
| Catherine | 30 | F W | Keeping house | **Bavaria** |
| John C. | 7 | M W | At school | Ohio |
| **William C.** | 4 | M W | At home | Ohio |

The two birthplaces are the marriage in one line: **Württemberg** and **Bavaria**, two German states three hundred kilometres apart, both landed in a river town in southeastern Ohio. The boy of four is **[William Clifford](/family/william-wildermuth/)** — Robert Earl's grandfather, Chuck's great-great-grandfather.

*(The enumerator's ages give birth years of about 1831 and 1840, against the documented 1830 and 1840. Census ages were what someone in the house told a marshal at the door.)*

## Ten years the archive still can't see

Between the 1853 oath in Philadelphia and the 1860 census in Ohio there is almost nothing. Robert Earl put it plainly: *"Little can be found of John during the next ten years."* What the 1860 census does show is that he was **still living in the William Roessar household** — and that the Roessars' fifth child, Charles, aged two, had been born in Ohio while the first four were born in Pennsylvania. So [the whole group moved west around 1858](/docs/john-michael-wildermuth-sketch/), and Johann Michael moved with them, still unmarried at twenty-eight, eleven years after they had crossed together.
