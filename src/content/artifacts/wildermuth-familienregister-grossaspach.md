A page copied out of a Württemberg **Familienregister** — the parish family-book that recorded a household as a unit rather than event by event — in a careful clerical hand, on ruled paper. It came back to Casselberry with the rest of [Robert Earl Wildermuth](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/)'s German correspondence, and it is the single densest German document in the archive.

It also corrects two things the archive had wrong for years.

![A handwritten Familienregister extract listing Johann Christian Wildermuth, born 10 October 1794 at Großaspach, citizen and shoemaker, died 7 October 1842; married 25 April 1819 at Rietenau to Maria Margaretha Schmidgall, born 25 October 1789 at Röhrach, died 30 December 1841; with their parents named and seven children listed with birth and death dates.](../../assets/family/originals/wildermuth-familienregister-grossaspach-extract.jpeg)

## The household

> **Johann Christian Wildermuth**
> \* 10.10.1794 Großaspach · **Bürger u. Schuhmacher zu Großaspach** · † 7.10.1842 Großaspach
> ∞ **25.4.1819 Rietenau**
>
> **Maria Margaretha Schmidgall**
> \* 25.10.1789 Röhrach · † 30.12.1841 Großaspach
>
> *Sohn des* **Adam Wildermuth, Bgr u. Weber zu Großaspach** *u. der* **Catharina Dorothea Wolf** *von Großaspach.* — FR II/262
> *Tochter des* **Wilhelm Schmidgall, Bgr zu Röhrach** *u. der †* **Anna Catharina Landai** *von Röhrach.*

Two occupations the archive did not have. Johann Christian was **Bürger und Schuhmacher** — citizen and **shoemaker** — which confirms the trade his son carried to Marietta. And his father [Adam](/family/adam-wildermuth/) was **Bürger und Weber**, citizen and **weaver**, not a vineyard *Gartner* like Adam's own father [Andreas](/family/andreus-wildermuth/). The trade changed when the family moved from Rielingshausen to Großaspach.

## The seven children

| Born | Name | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Großaspach 2.11.1819 | Catharina Dorothea | † Großaspach 15.11.1819 — *thirteen days* |
| Großaspach 22.10.1820 | Johann Jacob | ∞ 3.5.1846 Erbstetten — Rosina Thaiß of Erbstetten (FR IV/192) |
| Großaspach 18.8.1822 | Carolina | † Großaspach 17.11.1827 — *age five* |
| Großaspach 22.1.1825 | Johann Christian | *Lehrer in der Schweiz* — ∞ 27.12.1856 Kapperswyl, Anna Maria Stauffer of Rüthi |
| Großaspach 8.6.1828 | **Anna Maria** | ***nach Amerika*** |
| Großaspach 23.8.1830 | **[Johann Michael](/family/johann-michael-wildermuth/)** | ***1847 nach Amerika*** |
| Großaspach 11.6.1834 | Carolina | † Großaspach 9.7.1834 — *four weeks* |

Seven children in fifteen years. **Three died small** — thirteen days, five years, four weeks — and the name Carolina was given twice, the second time seven years after the first Carolina was buried. Of the four who lived, one became a **schoolteacher in Switzerland**, one married locally at Erbstetten, and **two went to America**.

## Correction 1 — the marriage was 1819, not 1842

The archive had Johann Christian marrying Maria Margaretha on **25 April 1842 at "Ristenau,"** six months before he died — and built a whole hypothesis on it: that Johann Michael, twelve years old in 1842, must be the son of an earlier unrecorded wife.

The register reads **25.4.1819**, at **Rietenau** — a real village a few kilometres from Aspach. The couple were married **twenty-three years**, and every one of the seven children above is theirs. There was no first wife. **[Maria Margaretha Schmidgall](/family/maria-margaretha-schmidgall/) is Johann Michael's mother**, as the tree always had it.

A single transposed digit had produced a phantom marriage and a phantom missing woman.

## Correction 2 — she died in 1841, not 1849

The register and [the annotated ancestor chart](/archive/rielingshausen-parish-correspondence/) both give Maria Margaretha's death as **30 December 1841**. The GEDCOM says 1849, and Robert Earl's own memoir prints 1947 — plainly a typo for something.

**1841 is the reading that makes the family make sense.** She died 30 December 1841; Johann Christian died 7 October 1842, ten months later. Their son Johann Michael was **twelve, and an orphan** — which is exactly how the archive has always described him at his 1847 departure. Under the 1849 date she would still have been alive when he sailed.

## Anna Maria, and a standing question

Robert Earl's 1990 research raised the possibility that **Frederika Rossar** — wife of the Baden shoemaker William Rossar, in whose Marietta household [Johann Michael](/family/johann-michael-wildermuth/) was living at the 1860 census — was Johann Michael's sister, making the crossing an extended-family undertaking.

The register does confirm that **a sister emigrated**: **Anna Maria**, born 8 June 1828, two years his elder, marked *nach Amerika*. It does not confirm she is Frederika — the names do not match, and the register gives no date for her going. But there was a Wildermuth sister in America, and that is new.

**Still open:** whether Anna Maria and Frederika Rossar are the same woman; when Anna Maria sailed; and what became of Johann Jacob's and Johann Christian junior's families, both of which the register points to by cross-reference (FR IV/192, and the Swiss marriage at Kapperswyl).
