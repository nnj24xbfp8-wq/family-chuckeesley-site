A color photograph of the **Historic Eesley Mill** sign in [Plainwell, Michigan](/places/plainwell-eesley-mill/) &mdash; the contemporary commercial-district sign that carries the Eesley name forward into the present day, more than a century after [John F. Eesley](/family/john-f-eesley/) built the mill in the 1880s and ran it as the town's principal flour mill for the next several decades.

## What the sign shows

A tall vertical hanging sign, painted green and red with cream lettering and trim, mounted on an iron bracket arm projecting from a downtown Plainwell storefront. The sign is laid out in four stacked panels under a green-and-cream header reading *"Historic EESLEY MILL"* with a stylized wheat-sheaf emblem above the name. The four panels below name the current businesses operating on the mill site:

- **PLAINWELL COFFEE MILL** (a coffee shop)
- **DINNER IS SERVED CATERING**
- **EESLEY PLACE BANQUETS**
- **HEAVENS PETALS FLOWERS**

The background sky behind the sign is bright blue with high cumulus; bare branches of a deciduous tree at right, and a red-and-white striped barrier post in the foreground &mdash; suggesting a late-fall or early-spring photograph. The current commercial life of the mill is preserved in the name on every business that occupies the building today.

## The line back to John F. Eesley

The mill was built by **[John F. Eesley](/family/john-f-eesley/)** in the 1880s; he was [Charles Leonard Eesley](/family/charles-leonard-eesley/)'s uncle &mdash; Chuck's paternal great-great-great-uncle. The Plainwell flour mill was the family's most public economic enterprise in the second half of the nineteenth century, and the mill building has carried the **Eesley** name continuously since &mdash; through ownership changes, repurposing as a banquet venue and coffee shop, and the present-day commercial revival the sign records.

The sign is the third surviving visible piece of the Eesley name on the American landscape, alongside the [Old Stratford Rother Street address](/places/old-stratford-rother-street/) the English-side family departed from in the 19th century and the [Banbury market town](/places/banbury/) that preceded it. On the [Plainwell place page](/places/plainwell-eesley-mill/) the sign is the documentary anchor of the mill's continuing local presence.
