## The archive had the year wrong. He had it right all along.

This archive has carried **20 December 1855** for [James Wesley Fleming](/family/wesley-fleming/), and has said so on several pages, against Robert Earl Wildermuth's own consistent **1857**. The reasoning was that he died on 2 October 1940 *"recorded as age 84,"* which requires a late-1855 birth.

**That reasoning is now overturned by a contemporaneous civil registration.** Virginia had required births to be registered since 1853; this is **Book 1, page 9** of the county's original register, and it says **1857**.

**And the reason the death record was wrong is the whole reason this archive exists.** Robert Earl wrote of his grandfather's death:

> *"At the time of his death, **noone knew who his father and mother were, when or where he was born nor his age.**"*

The *"age 84"* was supplied by people who, by his own account, **did not know how old the man was.** It was never evidence. He went to the Doddridge County courthouse in 1984 and got the register entry, and it settles the matter: **born 20 December 1857**, which made him **82** when he died.

The archive spent this session arguing against him on his own grandfather's birthday, using an informant's guess. The certificate corrects it.

## What else it confirms

- **Thomas Fleming and Martha as the parents**, on a county record independent of every chart and typescript — so the [Thomas Bailey Fleming](/family/thomas-bailey-fleming/) → Wesley link no longer rests on the GEDCOM alone.
- **Arnolds Creek** as the birthplace, exactly as held.
- **Father's occupation: Farmer** — the plainest official word on what Thomas Bailey Fleming did for a living, in the year he was still working the fifty acres he had [bought from his own father](/archive/fleming-marriage-record-and-deed/) and mortgaged straight back out.

## One thing it opens

The register names him **"Jno. Wesley Fleming."** *Jno.* is the standard nineteenth-century abbreviation for **John** — not James.

Every other source in this archive, including Robert Earl's own typescripts, calls him **James** Wesley Fleming. He was known in the family simply as **Wesley**, which may be why the first name drifted. Complicating it: a later brother is recorded as **John E. Fleming**, born 26 October 1866, so the household would have held two Johns.

Possibilities, none settled: the register recorded John and the family used James; the 1984 clerk expanded an ambiguous abbreviation; or the child was registered under one name and grew up with another. **Recorded as written.** Robert Earl held this certificate for the last twenty years of his life and went on calling him James, which suggests he had reason to.
