Set into the churchyard wall at **[Rielingshausen](/places/rielingshausen-cemetery/)** — the documented **cradle of the Wildermuth clan** — is the village's memorial to its dead of the two world wars. A carved relief of a mourning woman stands between the tablets, above the inscription **"ZUM EHRENDEN GEDENKEN UNSERER GEFALLENEN UND VERMISSTEN"** — *"to the honored memory of our fallen and missing."* A separate **"Vermisst"** (missing) tablet lists those never recovered.

The Wildermuth name recurs down every panel — the cost of the two wars to the village's most populous family. This is **the mirror image of Chuck's own line**: while these men stayed in Württemberg and died in the trenches and on the Eastern Front, **[Johann Michael Wildermuth](/family/johann-michael-wildermuth/)** had left Rielingshausen's neighbor-village Großaspach for Marietta, Ohio in **1847** — two or three generations before these names were carved. His American descendants fought in the same two wars from the other side of the ocean ([Robert Earl](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/) as a B-24 navigator in the Pacific).

## The Wildermuth war dead

**1914–1918** — Wildermuth Wilhelm (30.8.1914), Gottlieb (23.9.1914), Ernst (7.12.1914), Karl (17.7.1915), Karl (20.7.1915), Theodor (17.8.1916), Karl (8.6.1917), Albert (26.9.1918).

**1939–1945** — Wildermuth Walter, Wildermuth Walter, Wildermuth Herbert (8.5.1945), Wildermuth Ernst (12.12.1945).

**Missing (Vermisst)** — Wildermuth Kurt, among the village's unrecovered.

![The three memorial tablets — 1914–18 on the left and right, 1939–45 in the center — the Wildermuth name recurring down each.](../../assets/family/places/rielingshausen-cemetery/2018/IMG_1853.jpeg)

Robert Earl Wildermuth's 1989 manuscript counted *"23 Wildermuths"* on the village memorial; reading the tablets, that figure looks conservative.

## The clan behind the names

Rielingshausen is not incidental to the Wildermuths — it is their origin. Per **Heinz Erich Walter's *"Members of the Wildermuth-Clan,"*** the village was the clan's cradle, and Wildermuths served as its mayors across more than a century (Zacharias, 1640–1672; Jakob, 1721–1740; Adam, 1740–1762 — the last murdered by highway robbers in 1768). The same clan produced the 19th-century writer **Ottilie Wildermuth**, the psychiatrist **Dr. Hermann Wildermuth**, and **Eberhard Wildermuth** — decorated commander of Le Havre in WWII and the **first housing minister of the Federal Republic of Germany** under Adenauer. The men on this wall are of that same deep Rielingshausen stock — the family that ran the village, and paid for two wars in its sons.
