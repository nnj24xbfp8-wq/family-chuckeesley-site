A summer 1947 outdoor group portrait on a park bench, printed by the family with a full caption naming everyone &mdash; the maternal **Hill–Davis** line across its living generations.

## Who is in the frame

Per the printed caption:

- **Standing:** **[Bessie Marie (Hill) Davis](/family/bessie-hill-davis/)** (1905&ndash;1950) and her father **[Harvey Hendershot Hill](/family/harvey-hendershot-hill/)** (1882&ndash;1951).
- **Seated:** **[Mary Louise (Davis) Murdock](/family/mary-louise-davis-murdock/)** (1924&ndash;2002), holding her infant son **[John "Jack" Anthony Murdock, Jr.](/family/john-anthony-murdock-jr/)** (1946&ndash;1977), beside **[Ida J. "Nettie" (Ullman) Hill](/family/ida-jeanetta-ullman-hill/)** (1892&ndash;1977).

## The generations — and the caption

The print is labeled a **"five generation picture."** The direct line of descent it captures is in fact **four generations**: [Harvey Hill](/family/harvey-hendershot-hill/) → his daughter [Bessie](/family/bessie-hill-davis/) → her daughter [Mary Louise](/family/mary-louise-davis-murdock/) → her son, the infant [Jack](/family/john-anthony-murdock-jr/). The fifth figure, **[Nettie](/family/ida-jeanetta-ullman-hill/)**, is not a fifth generation but Bessie's **aunt** &mdash; the younger sister of Bessie's late mother [Clara Ullmann Hill](/family/clara-victoria-ullmann/), who had died in 1941. Nettie stands in, in a sense, for the missing matriarch: Clara's generation, present at the gathering through her sister.

It is a poignant frame for another reason. Of the five, three would be gone within a few years or had hard endings ahead: **Bessie died in 1950**, at 45; **Harvey in 1951**; and the baby, **Jack, died in 1977**, at about 31. In 1947, though, they are five people across four generations on a bench in the Ohio summer.
