**This is the will he spent years hunting** — and it is not quite the will he thought he was hunting.

He had chased a *"John Fleming Sr."* said to have died at Derry, Pennsylvania in 1777, and eventually [obtained a will through a Chester County archivist](/archive/fleming-dear-cousins-1998/). This is a different and much fuller document: **John Fleming of Montgomery County, Maryland**, who styles himself **"John Fleming Senr"** in his own signature, made in **January 1795** and **proved 16 January 1797**.

The date matches the man the [Family Legacy typescript](/docs/john-fleming-family-legacy/) calls **John Fleming Jr.** — *"died on Christmas Day, 1796 … His will was probated on 16 January 1797."* The "Senior" in the heading is his own, to distinguish himself from his son John. It is a compact illustration of why five generations of Johns defeated everyone who tried to sort them out.

> **A caution that governs this whole page.** In November 1998 Robert Earl [withdrew this branch from the family's ancestry](/archive/fleming-retraction-1998/), concluding that *"our John IV was not the son of John III of Pennsylvania."* This will is a real document about real people; whether they are **Chuck's** people is exactly what he retracted. Read it as evidence, not as lineage.

## What the will says

The testator provides first for his wife **Ann Fleming**, who is to hold his lands and personal property for her natural life and whom he names **executrix**; **David Lowe** is executor. Three named tracts recur: **Heensley**, **Fleming's Addition** to Heensley, and **Bedfordshire Carrier**.

The people named:

| Named in the will | Matching the typescript's family of John Fleming Jr. |
|---|---|
| wife **Ann Fleming** | Anne White ✓ |
| **David and Margaret Lowe** | daughter Margaret m. David Lowe ✓ |
| **Catherine Magruder** | daughter Catherine m. Joseph Magruder ✓ |
| **Ann Willet**, "wife of Ninien Willet" | daughter Ann m. Ninien Willet ✓ |
| **son John Fleming** | John Fleming III ✓ |
| **Elizabeth Fleming, "wife of my late son James Fleming"** | son James m. Elizabeth Burgess ✓ — *and already dead by 1795* |

Independent confirmation, from a probate record, of five of the six children the typescript lists. It also settles something Robert Earl had noted as a loose end: he wrote that **James had died before the will was written**, and here is the will saying so — *"my late son James Fleming."*

## The six grandchildren, and why they matter now

The will twice enumerates **the heirs of James Fleming**:

> **John Fleming, Robert Fleming, Burgess Fleming, Cephas Fleming, Mary Fleming and Catherine Fleming**

That list is worth more than anything else on the page. Robert Earl's [retraction](/archive/fleming-retraction-1998/) concluded that the line runs not through John but through **a James Fleming** — and this document names a James Fleming's children, from probate, in 1795. **If the retraction is right, the connection this archive is missing may be somewhere in that sentence.**

## Slaveholding, stated plainly

The will disposes of two enslaved people, and the archive records it without softening:

- **A woman named Nancy** is bequeathed to David Lowe, together with two feather beds and furniture — listed among the household goods.
- **A man named Frank** is left to the testator's son John Fleming and to David Lowe **"to be by them set free"** under the relevant Maryland assembly provisions.

So the household held people in slavery, and the will freed one man and passed on a woman as property in consecutive clauses. **Nancy and Frank are named here because they were named in the document, and because they are the only trace of them this archive has.** Nothing else about either of them is known — not their ages, their kin, whether the manumission was carried out, or what became of Nancy in the Lowe household.

## Execution and proof

Signed and sealed by **John Fleming Senr** in the presence of **James Anderson** and **Ozias Offutt**, who appeared at Montgomery County on **16 January 1797** and swore to the signature. Certified by **Saml Turner, Register**.
