An exchange of letters between **Casselberry, Florida** and a village parish office in **Baden-Württemberg** — Robert Earl Wildermuth writing to the one institution that could read the eighteenth-century Wildermuth record, and the pastor writing back with something nobody in the family knew.

## 1. The letter out

![A typed form letter addressed to the Evangelisches Pfarramt, D-7157 Rielingshausen, Bundesrepublik Deutschland, written in English in the upper half and repeated in German below, requesting transcripts from the local church books and enclosing an ancestor chart and two international reply coupons, signed Robert E. Wildermuth.](../../assets/family/originals/wildermuth-letter-to-rielingshausen-pfarramt.jpeg)

> *Sehr verehrter Herr Pfarrer!*
>
> *According to the genealogical sources I have collected my ancestors supposedly come from Rielingshausen, Wuerttemburg, Germany, so I most courteousley request some information from the local church books.*
>
> *I am enclosing an ancestor chart so that you can see exactly whom this concerns. I most courteousely request a complete transcript or photocopies from the pertinent birth, marriage, and death records for my ancestors from your town.*
>
> *I am enclosing two coupons that can be exchanged at your post office for postage stamps.*
>
> *Thank you in advance for your efforts and cooperation, I remain*
>
> *Most respectfully,* **ROBERT E. WILDERMUTH**

The same text is repeated below in German, and closes with a line that is the most human thing on the page:

> *P.S. Ich verstehe nur ein wenig Deutsch.*
> *("P.S. I understand only a little German.")*

He addressed it to **D-7157 Rielingshausen** — a postal code that had already lapsed when Rielingshausen was absorbed into Marbach am Neckar in 1972. It arrived anyway.

## 2. The chart he enclosed

![A printed Ahnentafel family chart filled in by hand in black capitals with the German Wildermuth line back to the 1710s, heavily annotated in blue ink by the parish pastor and circled in red, showing Andreas Wildermuth born 30 November 1745 and Margaretha Lämmle with a death date of 8 May 1778.](../../assets/family/originals/wildermuth-ahnentafel-chart-306-annotated.jpeg)

Robert Earl's own pedigree chart, filled out in his block capitals, carrying the line from **Johann Michael Wildermuth** (b. 1830 Großaspach, *"NACH AMERIKA 1847"*) back through Johann Christian, Adam, and the Wolf, Schmidgall and Landai families of Großaspach and Röhrach.

It came back covered in the pastor's blue handwriting, with two boxes ringed in red and **BITTE** — *please* — written across the top. The pastor filled in what Robert Earl had left blank and corrected what he had wrong:

| | On the returned chart |
|---|---|
| **Andreas Wildermuth** | b. 30 Nov 1745, Rielingshausen · m. 19 Juli 1768 · d. **1. Juni 1771**, Rielingshausen |
| **Margaretha Lämmle** | b. 7 März 1746, Rielingshausen · d. **8. Mai 1778**, Rielingshausen |
| *Andreas's parents* | **Hans Adam** Wildermuth and **Rosina** |
| *Margaretha's parents* | **Johann Michael Lämmle** and **Catharina** |

Every one of those matches what the archive independently holds from the FamilySearch tree — which matters, because this is a clergyman reading the actual **Kirchenbücher** in the village, not a database. The deep German dates on [Andreas](/family/andreus-wildermuth/)'s and [Margaretha](/family/margaretha-laemmle/)'s pages now rest on two independent sources.

*The chart is dated **18 April 1959** in Robert Earl's hand, at the Casselberry address. That sits oddly against the correspondence, which must postdate 1972 on the postal codes alone. Either he had been keeping this chart for thirty years before he mailed it, or the year is a misreading of 1989.*

## 3. The reply

![A typed letter on the letterhead of the Evangelisches Pfarramt, 7142 Marbach/N.-Rielingshausen, Rathausplatz, telling Herr Wildermuth that some data could be gathered, and recording that Margaretha née Lämmle married Michael Eisenbarth of neighbouring Erdmannhausen after her husband Andreas Wildermuth's death, and died on 8 May in childbirth. Signed by the pastor.](../../assets/family/originals/rielingshausen-pfarrer-reply-letter.jpeg)

> **Sehr geehrter Herr Wildermuth,**
>
> wie Sie sehen, haben wir einige Daten erheben können.
>
> Margaretha geb. Lämmle hat nach dem Tod ihres Mannes Andreas Wildermuth im Jahr 1772 Michael Eisenbarth aus unserem Nachbarort Erdmannhausen geheiratet. Sie starb 1778 am 8. Mai bei der Geburt eines Kindes.
>
> *Mit freundlichen Grüßen!* — (Pfarrer)

In English:

> **Dear Mr Wildermuth,**
>
> As you can see, we were able to gather some data.
>
> Margaretha née Lämmle, after the death of her husband Andreas Wildermuth, married Michael Eisenbarth from our neighbouring village of Erdmannhausen in 1772. She died on 8 May 1778 giving birth to a child.

One sentence, and it changes a life the archive had recorded only as two dates.

![An airmail envelope from the Evangelisches Pfarramt, Rathausplatz 18, 7142 Marbach/N.-Rielingshausen, with a MIT LUFTPOST / PAR AVION label and a Marbach postmark, addressed by hand to Mr. Robert E. Wildermuth, 410 Eagle Circle, Casselberry, Florida 32707, USA.](../../assets/family/originals/rielingshausen-pfarramt-airmail-envelope.jpeg)

*The envelope it came in — MIT LUFTPOST, PAR AVION, hand-addressed to Casselberry.*

## What the parish supplied that nobody had

[Margaretha Lämmle](/family/margaretha-laemmle/) was widowed at **twenty-five**, when [Andreas](/family/andreus-wildermuth/) died on 1 June 1771 leaving her with a three-year-old son. Within a year she **married again — Michael Eisenbarth, from Erdmannhausen**, the next village over. Six years after that she **died in childbirth**, at thirty-two.

The archive had her death date. It did not have the second husband, the second village, or the reason. The child whose birth killed her was an Eisenbarth, not a Wildermuth — which is very likely why the family record lost both.

It is also worth noticing what the exchange cost: a form letter, two international reply coupons, and a stranger's willingness to go and read a parish register. That is the whole apparatus by which this part of the archive exists.

**Still open:** what became of the Eisenbarth child, and whether Margaretha had further children between 1772 and 1778. The pastor began a further sentence — *"Wir haben zu dritt…"* — and struck it out before signing.
