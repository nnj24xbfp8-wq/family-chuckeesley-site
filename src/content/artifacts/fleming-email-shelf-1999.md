Four email printouts from **April 1999** — five months after [the "STOP THE PRESSES" retraction](/archive/fleming-retraction-1998/) — and they change the shape of the Fleming problem again.

**This archive has spent several passes assembling a case that James Fleming was his conclusion.** These emails show it was not. It was a way-station.

## He gave up, and he was leaning somewhere else

Writing to his cousins under the subject line **"HE'S ON THE SHELF"**:

> *"Thanks to your good suggestion and after many frustrating hours of trying to piece things together to make a logical descendancy, **I've decided to turn my back on our John Fleming b. 1781.** Maybe something will turn up unexpectedly. **Still leaning towards Samuel or Patrick.**"*

So the sequence of his own positions runs:

| Date | John Fleming IV's father |
|---|---|
| **March 1998** | John Fleming III, in a four-John Maryland line — [the printed charts](/archive/pedigree-charts-1998/) |
| **May 1998** | *"a stumbling block… so many Johns, James, Patricks and Samuels"* — [already stuck](/archive/wildermuth-email-bartlett-researcher-1998/) |
| **November 1998** | **James Fleming** — [remove John Jr. and John III, insert James](/archive/fleming-retraction-1998/) |
| **April 1999** | **"Still leaning towards Samuel or Patrick"** — and shelving the question |

**Four positions in thirteen months, and the last one is neither John nor James.** He put the man on a shelf and went to work on the Baileys and Bartletts instead:

> *"Now I'm turning my efforts to my Baileys and Bartletts. They all left scads of records in Preston and Harrison Counties so maybe I won't be so frustrated in finding nothing."*

And closed with the joke that gives the thread its name — going off to *dust off the shelf and see if old John might be hiding in there.*

**What this archive is changing.** [John Fleming IV's page](/family/john-fleming-iv/) and the [1795 deed](/archive/berry-to-james-fleming-deed-1795/) were framed as "James Fleming is probably the answer." **That overstated it.** The James Fleming land trail is real evidence and still the best documentary thread in the line — but **his own last recorded view was Samuel or Patrick**, and the honest summary is that **he never settled it and neither has anyone since.**

*Two candidate names now on the table.* A **Samuel Fleming** he described in 1998 as *"one of the original sons,"* a justice of the peace who *"really cut a swath through Frederick County, Maryland."* And a **Patrick** — worth noting that the [1820 Virginia census list](/archive/doddridge-births-harrison-marriages-hampshire-research/) in his papers records **"Patrick — HMP"**, a Patrick Fleming in **Hampshire County**, the county John IV's death register names as his birthplace.

## What he confirms in passing

Two useful confirmations of documents this archive now holds:

> *"I found many of his records (**wedding vow with John Roe**, **permission for my great-great grandfather to marry Synthia Baily**)."*

The [1803 marriage return](/archive/fleming-roe-marriage-banns-1803/) and the [1827 consent note](/archive/john-fleming-consent-note-1827/) — described in his own words, and Lewis identified as his great-great-grandfather.

He also explains why Taylor County kept failing him:

> *"John lived in the Eastern Sector of Harrison County before it became Taylor County… I think one needs to search for John's records in Harrison and/or Doddridge Counties."*

And he was still hunting Lewis's death: *"all I know is that he died in Taylor County c. 1862 after moving back there from Doddridge Co."* **He never found it.** [Lewis's death is still unverified in this archive.](/family/lewis-fleming/)

## Taylor County research leads, from Norman Collier

Three of the four pages are a forwarded reply from a **Norman Collier**, and they are pure practical intelligence — the kind of thing that is normally lost when a researcher dies:

- **Marguerite Baugh**, at the **Taylor County Library**, resident custodian of the **Taylor County Genealogy Society** — thirty years in the post, would do specific look-ups for the cost of copies. *(A 1999 phone number is on the page; not reproduced here.)*
- **Melba Zinn** of Route 3, Box 169, Grafton — compiler of ***Taylor County, WV Deed Abstracts, Books One and Two, 1844–1852***, and available for paid research, *"but more expensive."*
- ***Taylor County, WV Marriages 1853–1905***, compiled by **Wes Cochran** of Parkersburg, 1987 — from which Collier sent a page-indexed list of **Fleming marriages** running to some fifty entries, from Abbie to Zachariah.
- ***A Taylor County History of West Virginia***, Taylor County Genealogy Society, 1986 — **three Fleming write-ups at pages 218–220**, both of which, Collier notes, *"indicate that the Flemmings came to Taylor County about the time of the Civil War."*
- **Nation Cemetery, Grafton** — a **Flemming, John P.**, died **19 April 1864**.
- And a deed abstract of February 1845 referring to part of a 300-acre grant to a **William McClurg** *"and by him conveyed to **John Fleming heirs** and Henry Little."*

**"John Fleming heirs"** in a Taylor County deed of the 1840s is worth chasing — an heirs conveyance normally has to name them.

Collier's verdict on the main question was blunt and correct: ***"Nothing on John."***
