[Chuck](/family/charles-eric-eesley/) teaching in **Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam** &mdash; the city his father knew as **Saigon**. He is standing before a seminar room of students at the **UEH International School of Business (UEH–ISB)**, microphone in hand, under the school's *Leadership Program* banner and the *UEH University* sign; the projected slide reads *"Ignite Your Business Potential with Entrepreneurship."*

The frame is a quiet generational bookend. [Charlie Eesley](/family/charles-eesley/) shipped to [Saigon](/places/saigon/) in 1969 as a twenty-two-year-old soldier and rotated out in 1971; the [Letters from Vietnam](/docs/letters-from-vietnam/) are that year of his life, and he **never wanted to go back** &mdash; when Chuck was visiting the city decades later and called home, Charlie, on the other end, declined to come. A generation on, his son returns to the same city not at war but to **teach young Vietnamese founders how to build companies** &mdash; the family's [entrepreneurship-as-research-program](/family/charles-eric-eesley/) carried into the country the father had crossed an ocean to fight in. Same place; the opposite errand.
