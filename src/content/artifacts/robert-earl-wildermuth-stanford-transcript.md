**Robert Earl Wildermuth's Stanford University registrar's card** — the official academic record of his **1946–1948 GI Bill undergraduate years at Stanford**, the period he transitioned from B-24 navigator to civilian and earned the **BA in Biological Sciences** conferred 1 October 1948.

## Three addresses at the top

The card carries **WILDERMUTH, Robert Earl** in block capitals, and beneath it — in the *"Name and Address of Parent or Guardian"* band — **three** addresses, in two different hands. They are the first Bay Area addresses this archive has ever held for the family.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Box 276, Pine Lane, Los Altos** | His own local address, written in the primary hand |
| **[number] S. Stanford Av., Menlo Park** | Interlineated above in fainter pencil — an update, or a first address later replaced |
| **Mr. E. A. Wildermuth, 320 Harmar St., Marietta, O.** | The parent/guardian line — his father [Earl A. Wildermuth](/family/earl-a-wildermuth/) |

**The Menlo Park house number is not recoverable** from this scan. The pencil is faint and overwritten by the typed rule above it; the leading digit reads plausibly as a **7** and the rest does not resolve. The street and town are legible. A sharper photograph of that one band, or the original card under raking light, should settle it.

**Box 276, Pine Lane, Los Altos** is the more evocative of the two. A *box number* rather than a street number fits what Los Altos was in 1946–48: unincorporated orchard country — it would not become a city until **1952**. A married veteran on the GI Bill renting in the apricot orchards, driving up to campus.

And the [1948 *Quad*](/archive/robert-earl-wildermuth-stanford-quad-1948/) confirms the arrangement in one word: his yearbook entry reads ***"Off campus."*** He had married [Dorothy Davis](/family/dorothy-davis-wildermuth/) in April 1946, months before arriving; married veterans were not housed in the dormitories.

*Note on the Marietta address:* the card gives his father at **320 Harmar Street**. Robert Earl's memoir describes the family's Marietta "dream house" at **413 Harmar Street**. Two numbers on the same street — either a move, or one of the two is misremembered. He also attended Harmar Elementary; the family's centre of gravity was that street.

## What else the card records

*"School or College from which Credentials have been Preserved:"* — **Marietta (Ohio) College**. The transfer the archive knew from his memoir, in the registrar's own hand: he had begun pre-med at Marietta College before the war took him, and Stanford held those credentials on file.

The Stanford registrar's office filed the rest through the standard transcript-card format of the era: ruled rows for each course title, columns for unit count, grade, and semester.

Visible course entries include **English, Mathematics (Algebra)**, **Chemistry**, **Spanish (or another language)**, and other lower-division work consistent with a **pre-medical track of the late 1940s**. The handwritten ink in some cells records grades and quarter-credit annotations; the column headers point to Stanford's then-distinctive quarter-based curriculum.

The card is the **structured academic counterpart** to the [Stanford BA diploma](/archive/robert-earl-wildermuth-stanford-ba-1948/) already in this archive — together the two documents anchor Robert Earl's Stanford years in primary-source form. The diploma celebrates the conferral; this card records the work that earned it.

## In the memoir's frame

Robert Earl's [1989 memoir](/docs/robert-earl-wildermuth-memoir/) records that he came to Stanford on the **GI Bill** in 1946 after his Pacific tour and his marriage to Dorothy Davis. *"Stanford University turned out to be a dream,"* he wrote. *"Academically Stanford was rated number three in the United States when I attended and it is known as the 'Harvard of the West.' Millionaire's sons and daughters go there and movie star's children strived for admission and here I was a young guy from rural Ohio and from the wrong side of town at that."* He applied to medical school during his Stanford senior year and was denied; took the BA anyway and went to work in industry. The course grades on this card are the record of the work he did in that period of denied-medical-school disappointment.

## Why it matters

The Stanford transcript is the **earliest piece of structured American higher-education documentation** in this archive's Wildermuth-side records. It documents the **academic Robert Earl** — the disciplined post-war GI-Bill student whose grades determined whether he could go on to medical school. The denial of that medical-school admission is what redirected his life into industry, Korea, the Pentagon, the radar observer schools, and ultimately the Air Force career he made instead. **The grades on this card are the precise moment the life turned.**

## The second page

![A phone photograph of a two-page PDF of the Stanford transcript, the second page showing blank sections for academic honors, student activities and scholarships, a record-of-scholastic-deficiency grid, and a column of handwritten later entries listing dates with places including Chicago, Orlando, Ormond Beach, Tampa, Oklahoma and Midwest City.](../../assets/family/originals/robert-earl-wildermuth-stanford-transcript-pdf-pages.jpeg)

The card's reverse carries the printed sections for **Academic Honors**, **Student Activities** and **Scholarships** — all three left blank — beside a **Record of Scholastic Deficiency** grid. Below them, in a much later hand, runs a column of dated entries with places: *Am. Med. Record Assn., Chicago Ill.* · *Orlando* · *Ormond Beach, Fla.* · *Tampa, Florida* · *Oklahoma* · *Midwest City, Okla.* Those are transcript-request destinations — the registrar noting each time, over decades, that a copy was sent somewhere Robert Earl needed it. The blank honors sections and the long list of requests tell the same story from both ends: an unremarkable undergraduate record that he needed to produce again and again for the rest of his working life.
