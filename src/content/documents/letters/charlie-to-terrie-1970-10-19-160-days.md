## What the letter is

19 October 1970, a short field letter caught onto a chopper heading to the rear so it could be mailed. There is almost no news in it, and that is the point: rain under ponchos, a rumored rotation to the lowlands, the day-count ticking (*"160 days over here"*), and the flat, tired honesty of a man who *"really can't think of much to say."* What it does carry is the quiet hope that holds the whole correspondence together, the future with Terrie waiting on the far side of the war: *"keep studying and maybe we can have a good time next summer."*

## Transcription

> Dear Terrie,
>
> Well how are you? Someone is going to the rear by chopper today so I can mail this. It is raining today and doesn't seem too bad because we are under [ponchos]. I guess we will be going back to the lowlands soon but I've [heard] still in the rear that we will be back, they [say the] area (lowlands) around Nov 2.
>
> I'll just have to wait & see. I still have your last letters so I'll try to answer them by tomorrow when we get resupplied. I really can't think of much to say. I now have 160 days over here and don't know exactly when I'll get back. I just am hopeful that this war will end somehow but I doubt it. Keep studying and maybe we can have [page 2:] a good time next summer.
>
> Well I guess I'll end now.
>
> I love you.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**160 days.** *"I now have 160 days over here and don't know exactly when I'll get back."* The mid-tour arithmetic, 160 days in from an arrival around early May 1970, roughly the halfway mark that the [8 November letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-08-stand-down-and-sydney/)'s *"day 180... not even half way yet"* would restate three weeks later. The counting is the tour's constant background hum.

**The chopper mail.** *"Someone is going to the rear by chopper today so I can mail this."* Field mail depended on the resupply-and-chopper rhythm, letters written whenever paper and a moment allowed, then held until an aircraft was going back. The same logistics that govern the [late-November field letters](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-28-the-hawaii-answer/).

**The lowlands rotation.** *"We will be going back to the lowlands... around Nov 2."* The firebase-to-lowlands rotation that structured the fall-1970 field months, the same movement the [23 November](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-23-radio-operator-and-the-schedule/) and [30 November](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-30-disgusted-with-the-army/) letters track.

**"A good time next summer."** The letter's one forward look, past the war to the summer of 1971, keep studying, and there will be a life waiting. It is the same future the Hawaii R&R and the [August 1971 marriage](/family/charles-eesley/) would make real; here it's just a tired soldier's half-sentence at the end of a letter he couldn't think of much to put in.
