## What the letter is

14 January 1971, 1:45 a.m. Charlie isn't on guard this time &mdash; he just can't sleep, and writing is what he does with the sleeplessness. The news is one more turn of the drawdown rumor mill: no set 10-month tour after all, but *big drops which are just as good.* Around that, the small domestic furniture of the correspondence &mdash; grades, a reel of tape he can't get to play, the silverware on its way home &mdash; and, dropped in plainly between them, the line that needs no decoration: *God how I hate it over here.*

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**The tape, and the 10-month-tour rumor**

Two period details. The *"tape"* whose speed has him puzzled was a **reel-to-reel audio letter** &mdash; couples separated by the war mailed spoken recordings back and forth, and a reel recorded at one tape speed (3¾ vs. 7½ ips) would play back garbled on a machine set to another, exactly the trouble Charlie describes. The family later noted reel-to-reel recordings among Terrie's keepsakes.

The *"no set 10 month tour, just big drops"* line tracks the live 1971 question of whether the Army would formally shorten the standard 12-month Vietnam tour or simply keep handing out individual early-out **drops** as units drew down. For Charlie the distinction was academic &mdash; both got him home sooner &mdash; which is why he shrugs that the drops are *"just as good."*

*Source: [Wikipedia: Vietnamization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamization).*

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Jan 14*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> Well here it is 1:45 in the morning. I'm not on guard but I might as well be since I can't really sleep. There are still a lot of rumors going around but nothing definite except there is no set 10 month tour, just big drops which are just as good if the rumors pan out.
>
> Well Margaret got a 2.1 for her first semester. I had fun in school but that is all that counts. How are you doing for grades?
>
> Everyone is talking about [Tet] again this year. It may be true but I don't think too much will happen. God how I hate it over here. The army just isn't my bag.
>
> I still haven't heard all of your tape because the speed you used really has me puzzled. When it was [recorded at] two speeds &mdash; I'll try it again on another machine.
>
> Have you got the silverware yet? I guess I'll close &mdash;
>
> Love Charles

## What the letter records

**"God how I hate it over here. The army just isn't my bag."** The plainest homesickness in the corpus, set down without drama between a note about grades and a note about a tape. It is 1:45 in the morning and he can't sleep; the sentence has the unguarded flatness of the hour.

**The silverware.** One more piece of the married-life inventory Charlie was assembling by mail &mdash; the PACEX flatware that recurs across the winter letters, here just a question of whether it has reached home yet.

**"Big drops which are just as good."** His read on the tour-length debate: he doesn't care whether the Army cuts the official tour or simply keeps shortening his by fiat, so long as the number keeps falling.
