## What the letter is

12 November 1970, a *"short answer letter"* to a stack of about ten of Terrie's letters that had all arrived at once, the backlog finally breaking after the long mail gaps of early November. It is a housekeeping letter, the stand-down coming tomorrow, the apartment problem, the pay the Army owes him, bond stamps, but it carries the clearest statement yet of the arithmetic the rest of the tour runs on: *"I plan to extend and get out of here June 13, 1971... That leaves 213 days left at this writing."* The day-count is exact, and it dates the page on its own.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**Dating page 2 by the day-count**

Charlie writes *"That leaves 213 days left at this writing"* against a June 13, 1971 out-date. Counting back exactly 213 days from 13 June 1971 lands on **12 November 1970** &mdash; the date on page 1. The arithmetic confirms the two scans belong to the same letter and independently fixes the date. The same June 13 out-date appears in the [5 November letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-05-extension-plan-and-congressman/)'s extension plan; the [19 January 1971 letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-01-19-ets-extension/) refines it to the June 14 ETS Charlie actually held to.

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Nov 12*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> This letter will be a short answer letter to about 10 of your letters as I got the lot of them 2 days ago. We go in tomorrow Nov 13 for 1 week stand-down "training" and then after that it will be the lowlands again. Another battalion will be replacing us up here but I don't know for how long and it really doesn't solve the problem of being here in the first place.
>
> Sorry about your apartment problem. You shouldn't worry about what my uncle writes. He likes you so don't worry.
>
> I should have an opportunity to get my pay straightened out. I figure they owe me $500 or so.
>
> Bond stamps sound like a good idea. Try it if you can.
>
> [page 2:] I only got one telegram from you and I was at Cam Ranh Bay at the time.
>
> There is nothing you can send to keep me dry. Don't waste your money in this area. It does get cold but not very in the sense you're used to, 60° is all, the colder it probably gets.
>
> The monsoon rain & fog will last til March 1 or so in this part of the country. At least they did last year.
>
> I plan to extend and get out of here June 13, 1971 and be done with the army at that time. That leaves 213 days left at this writing.
>
> You have my permission to use the $130. If I wanted it [saved] I would have sent it home. Use it to send me goodies.

## What the letter records

**The mail backlog breaks.** *"A short answer letter to about 10 of your letters as I got the lot of them 2 days ago."* The fourteen-to-sixteen-day mail gaps the [5](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-05-extension-plan-and-congressman/) and [8 November](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-08-stand-down-and-sydney/) letters had complained about resolved the way they usually did, with the whole stack arriving at once, leaving Charlie to answer ten letters in one.

**The Nov 13 stand-down, dated from the field.** *"We go in tomorrow Nov 13 for 1 week stand-down 'training' and then after that it will be the lowlands again."* The stand-down the surrounding letters reference from either side is here named the day before it starts, with Charlie's standing verdict on the rotation: another battalion replacing them *"really doesn't solve the problem of being here in the first place."*

**The uncle, and the apartment.** *"You shouldn't worry about what my uncle writes. He likes you."* A glimpse of the family network around the courtship, an uncle whose letters had evidently unsettled Terrie, and Charlie reassuring her, alongside the *"apartment problem"* that places her in off-campus housing at Maryland.

**Cam Ranh Bay.** *"I only got one telegram from you and I was at Cam Ranh Bay at the time."* The reference is to the September 1970 malaria hospitalization at Cam Ranh Bay, the rear-area medical evacuation that the unit-history thread tracks; a telegram from Terrie had reached him there.

**213 days, and the money.** The exact day-count against the June 13 out-date is the arithmetic the whole end-of-tour plan rests on. Around it sits the small steady accounting of the corpus, the $500 the Army owes him, the bond stamps, the $130 she has his permission to spend on *"goodies"* &mdash; every dollar tracked across eight thousand miles.
