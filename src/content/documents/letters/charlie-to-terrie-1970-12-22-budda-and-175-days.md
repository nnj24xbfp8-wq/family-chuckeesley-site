## What the letter is

22 December 1970. Charlie encloses a clipping from the army paper and lets it carry the bad news he then restates: no drop of any real size is coming, so he may be stuck in country until May or June. That pushes the reunion back toward a Hawaii R&R in March or April. The dominant feeling is dread &mdash; *"I'm getting more fearful of returning to the field than ever"* &mdash; offset by the short-timer's mantra that *"time marches on."* The second page turns the lens on himself, half-joking: heavier, balding, and christened *"Budda"* by the children across the wire.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**The drop that wouldn't come — yet**

Through December 1970 Charlie's whole future hinged on the *drop* &mdash; the early-out curtailment that the drawdown might or might not grant him. Here the news is discouraging: no big drop, so plan on staying. Within weeks the picture would flip (by the January letters the drops were *"getting big"*), but at year's end the outlook was bleak enough that he was bracing to be in Vietnam into the summer and, worse, to be sent back to the field. The *"175 days left"* count would, by mid-January, fall toward the 155 and then 136 of the new year's letters as the early-outs finally loosened.

*Source: [Wikipedia: Vietnamization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamization) (the 1970–71 U.S. drawdown and tour curtailments).*

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Dec 22*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> Enclosed is an article from the army paper we get here. It speaks for itself.
>
> Also things look bad for a drop of any great size so I may be here til May and maybe June. If that is the case then I'll [meet] you in Hawaii in March or April or so I guess. Things are really messed up and I'm getting more fearful of returning to the field than ever. Oh well, at least time marches on. At most now I have around 175 days left over here. I'm starting to read and all just to pass the time.
>
> I weighed myself and I'm up to 185 again. I don't have much hair left honey &mdash; sorry about that. It really shows up when I take a picture. I'll send you one anyway in a few days. The kids on the other side of the [wire] call me Budda! How does that grab you. Everything is fine here. Hope to hear from you soon.
>
> Love Charles

## What the letter records

**"More fearful of returning to the field than ever."** Charlie was in a rear job by late 1970, but the assignment was never guaranteed, and the dread of being sent back forward is the steady undertone of the December letters &mdash; the same fear that the [18 December "children at the fence" letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-18-fifty-fifty-and-the-fence/) shows could be triggered by something as small as refusing an order at a guard post.

**"Budda."** The self-deprecating heart of the letter: heavier, balding, and nicknamed by the Vietnamese children across the wire. The same kids who appear elsewhere as a security problem are here just kids who've given the round American sergeant a name.

**"I'm starting to read... just to pass the time."** The short-timer's war reduced to waiting &mdash; books as a way to burn the 175 days. It's the same register as the [14 January "1:45 a.m., can't sleep"](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-01-14-no-set-tour/) letter a few weeks later.
