## What the letter is

30 November 1970, written from a small hilly area in the field at the end of the month. The keynote is exhaustion-as-disgust: *"I am once again disgusted completely with the army and the people in it"* &mdash; not over the war as such but over the petty machinery of it, the several chances at a week or two in the rear that each got *"blown out the window by one thing or the other."* Around the complaint sits the physical record of the late monsoon: the misty rain that won't quit, and the two platoons pulled out of low ground when one group found itself ringed by slowly rising water. November ends with Charlie noting he has no sense of season at all.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**The flooding lowlands, late November 1970**

Charlie's note that *"they finally got smart and got our other 2 platoons out of the lower ground when one group found they were surrounded by slowly rising water"* is a concrete piece of the 1970 monsoon's effect on I Corps infantry operations: the low ground flooded, and units had to be moved to higher terrain. His own platoon had been *"operating on a small hilly area for the last 6 days"* and expected to stay until about 19 December &mdash; the same mid-December horizon the [23 November schedule letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-23-radio-operator-and-the-schedule/) had laid out (lowlands until 19 December, then back to the mountains).

*Source: [charlie1-506.com unit history](http://www.charlie1-506.com/) (1-506 Infantry, 101st Airborne Division, I Corps operations, 1970).*

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Nov 30*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> Well tomorrow the sorry asses plan to pay us and after sending our mail all over the place I shall get it too. I am once again disgusted completely with the army and the people in it. I have had several chances to get to the rear for a week or two but each one has been blown out the window by one thing or the other happening. It is really frustrating. Nothing much happening now. The rain is still very much with us as it is a misty rain most of the time. They finally got smart and got our other 2 platoons out of the lower ground when one group found they were surrounded by slowly rising water. We have been operating on a small hilly area for the last 6 days and should be [here] until Dec 19 or so.
>
> [page 2:] If you send any magazines over please get the latest copy and put it in an envelope because a package takes too long to get here and by then most of the news is second hand. Send something like Newsweek or Playboy if you send one.
>
> Well November is finally over. I guess time is going fast. I really couldn't tell though. I have no sense of season over here. It is a warm rain although I am cold, [and] the weather is fairly warm. There is no sense of holidays either since there is no communication with the outside world.
>
> Well there have to be better days ahead. I'll send another letter on the next resupply out here which should be in 4-5 days.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**Disgust at the machinery, not the war.** *"Disgusted completely with the army and the people in it"* &mdash; the target is the bureaucracy that kept blowing out his shots at a week in the rear, not the enemy or the mission. It's the recurring register of the short-timer: the war is endured, but the Army's own petty failures are what actually rankle.

**The flooding.** The two platoons surrounded by *"slowly rising water"* is the monsoon as an operational fact, not a backdrop. The line *"they finally got smart"* carries the enlisted man's standing verdict on the chain of command: the right call, made late.

**"Newsweek or Playboy."** The package economics of the [15](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-15-spec-4-and-inquiry-over/) and [23 November](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-23-radio-operator-and-the-schedule/) letters, refined to magazines: send the latest issue in an envelope, because a package arrives too late to carry news. A small window onto how slowly information reached a man in the field.

**No sense of season.** *"I have no sense of season over here... no sense of holidays either since there is no communication with the outside world."* The same flattening of time that the [25 November Thanksgiving letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-25-thanksgiving-field/) names &mdash; the calendar of home gone meaningless in the field &mdash; stated again as November closes.
