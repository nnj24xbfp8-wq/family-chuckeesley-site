## What the letter is

13 January 1971. Four of Terrie's letters had arrived together, and Charlie answers them in a loose, funny, affectionate register &mdash; the rats and cockroaches of firebase life offered as comedy, a friend reportedly stationed a mile or two away, the Bob Hope show damned with faint praise, and a teasing word against the poetry Terrie had apparently sent. Underneath the jokes is the same honest refrain of the winter: he doesn't know when he's coming home, the rumors swing good to bad, and he's filling the not-knowing with stereo plans and letters.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**Firebase fauna, the Bob Hope show, and Camp Evans**

The *"rats... and the cockroaches 2–3 inches long"* are not exaggeration: rodents and large tropical cockroaches were a constant of bunker and firebase life in I Corps, drawn by ration waste and standing water. Charlie offers to mail one home as proof.

The **Bob Hope show** was the annual USO Christmas tour, which played Vietnam bases every December through 1972; the 1970 tour was one of the last of the big in-country shows. Charlie's shrug &mdash; *"really wasn't worth much except to say you've been there"* &mdash; is the grunt's-eye verdict on a spectacle pitched largely at the cameras back home.

**Camp Evans**, which Charlie names as his base, was a major 101st Airborne installation on Route 1 northwest of Hue, in Thua Thien Province &mdash; the staging point for operations into the A Shau Valley and the western mountains. A friend *"2 clicks"* away means about two kilometers (a *click* / klick being a kilometer in GI usage).

*Sources: [Wikipedia: Bob Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hope) (USO Christmas tours of Vietnam); [Wikipedia: 101st Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division).*

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Jan 13*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> I have 4 long letters from you & now I'll answer them & I'll write a little more too I guess. [&hellip;] the only thing is that [the calls?] are pretty high. My poor parents haven't told me what I've cost them yet and I'm afraid to ask.
>
> If you think you have mouse problems you should see the rats around here and on the firebases. We don't bite them and they don't bite us and we get along fine. Also the cockroaches are 2&ndash;3 inches long. If you don't believe me say so and I'll mail one to you.
>
> Well I'm glad you are finally relieved by my letters of today. I sure hope I didn't get too mushy. Anne may be younger but she is not your little sister. I'll still love her. Please tell her for me.
>
> A friend of mine here told me that Abbott is 2 clicks or about 1½ miles from Camp Evans where I'm based. If it is true I'll see him if I can when I get back. It just goes to show how little they tell us infantry.
>
> The Bob Hope show really wasn't worth much except to say you've been there.
>
> Don't really ask me when I'll be home because I don't really know. The rumors are very good then bad. I just don't know.
>
> One piece of advice &mdash; stay away from poetry my dear, it just didn't make much sense.
>
> Well I sent home for $700 to start buying my stereo. I hope I can get most of it and order it from here. I hope the stainless steel ware arrives soon so I'll have an idea of how long it takes to arrive.
>
> Love Charles

## What the letter records

**Comedy as reassurance.** The rats-and-roaches routine &mdash; *"if you don't believe me say so and I'll mail one to you"* &mdash; is Charlie managing the distance the way he often does: turning the grim texture of where he lives into a bit Terrie can laugh at, right after worrying he'd gotten *"too mushy."*

**"How little they tell us infantry."** Even a small thing &mdash; whether his friend Abbott is really stationed nearby &mdash; comes to him as rumor. The line is a quiet indictment of the information vacuum a rifleman lived in.

**"Stay away from poetry my dear."** Terrie had evidently sent him some verse; his deadpan dismissal is pure Charlie &mdash; affectionate teasing dressed as blunt practicality.

**The stereo and the stainless steel.** The two material threads of the winter letters &mdash; audio equipment for himself, flatware for the marriage &mdash; both advancing by mail-order while he waits out the tour.
