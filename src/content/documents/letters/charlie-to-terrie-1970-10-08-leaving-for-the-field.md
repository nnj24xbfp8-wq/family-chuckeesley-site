## What the letter is

8 October 1970, a short letter written in the last minutes before he leaves for the field — the departure the [6 October letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-06-back-to-the-field-raise-hell/) had warned was coming. Two days of dread compressed into a page: disbelief that they'd keep him in the mountains through the monsoon, a small bureaucratic grievance (no time even to fix his pay), and the long horizon of an out date — *"5 months over with, with 3 left if I get out of the army in June"* — that *"seems like an awfully long time."* Then he has to pack, and the letter simply stops.

## Transcription

> *Oct 8*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> I leave for the damn field today. It isn't raining now and I feel all right. I still can't believe they will keep me in the mountains during the monsoons because of resupply and support problems. I guess we will just have to suffer a little.
>
> How I hate it. They haven't even given me time to get my finances straightened out. Oh well — time is one more [thing], I guess. I'll have to wire someone higher up in order to get them off their ass.
>
> I hope everything is all right with you and school isn't getting you down. By the time you get this letter I'll have 5 months over with, with 3 left if I get out of the army in June. That seems like an awfully long
>
> [page 2:] time.
>
> Well I have to pack up and be ready to go, so I'll end this letter now.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**"I leave for the damn field today."** The whole letter is the pause before going out — written, as so many are, against a deadline (*"I have to pack up and be ready to go"*). It is the shortest, plainest form of the tour's basic motion: rear to field, comfort to hardship, dry to wet.

**Five over, three left.** *"5 months over with, with 3 left if I get out of the army in June."* The out-date math that fills the fall and winter letters — a June discharge under an early drop, three months of field time still between him and it. The same countdown that the [3 October letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-03-malaria-cam-ranh-bay/)'s *"252 days left"* and the December letters' [drop rumors](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-28-ten-month-rumor-and-married-early/) keep re-totaling.

**The finances.** *"They haven't even given me time to get my finances straightened out."* A small, human grievance — the money he keeps sending Terrie, the pay problems he keeps having to *"wire someone higher up"* to fix — the domestic administration of a life run by mail from a war zone.
