## What the letter is

24 February 1971, evening, the "more comprehensive letter" he'd promised on the 21st. Five days into the Camp Eagle clerk job, the texture of the late tour is now entirely logistical: her health, his quarters, and above all the R&R math. The April Hawaii trip is the organizing hope of the spring, and the whole letter turns on its one obstacle, cost, and its one requirement, a firm commitment from Terrie before the 5-6 March allocations force his hand.

## Transcription

> *Feb 24, evening.*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> I got your letters dated Feb 16 today. Sorry to hear you are sick. I hope it is all in the past by the time you receive this letter. I've been here 5 days now and everything is fine. I'm not settled into my quarters yet but I should be in the not too distant future.
>
> As far as R&R the only thing I have against it is the cost. On March 5-6 the allocations come down and that is when I must make my decision for April. I should be able to get Hawaii. I hope
>
> [page 2:] you have started to write here now, I'll need an answer and we will have to plan things a little better if we are to meet.
>
> Yes, I think it would be very nice if we could meet there. If [it] isn't that much and we should have some fun.
>
> I'm tired now so I guess I'll go to bed.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**The R&R deadline.** This is the same allocation machinery as the 17 February letter, now narrowed to a date: 5-6 March is when he commits for April. The trip can't be claimed and casually dropped, so he keeps pressing Terrie for the firm answer the Army's calendar demands.

**"If we are to meet."** Hawaii R&R was, for many couples, the one chance to see each other across a 12-month tour. The planning here, addresses, allocation dates, who writes whom, is the practical scaffolding under that hope.
