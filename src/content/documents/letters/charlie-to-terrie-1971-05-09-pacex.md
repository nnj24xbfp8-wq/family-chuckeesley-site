## What the letter is

9 May 1971. A week after the rain letter, and Charlie is candid that there's nothing to report: *I haven't been very good at writing letters. There isn't anything to do or write about.* What movement there is is all about outfitting a future. He is still waiting on the **Sears** arrangement, and about **$300 has come through PACEX** &mdash; enough to add the goods he wants to "the list," the running inventory of things a soon-to-be-married soldier could buy cheap through the military mail-order catalogue. The other thread is the by-now familiar standoff with Terrie's parents: if they stay silent, he reasons, the two of them will just make plans anyway &mdash; with the genuinely *major* decisions held over until he is home.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**PACEX: the catalogue that furnished a generation of GI marriages**

**PACEX** &mdash; the Pacific Exchange &mdash; was the Army &amp; Air Force Exchange Service's mail-order catalogue for service members in the Pacific theater. It let a soldier in Vietnam buy big-ticket consumer goods &mdash; stereo equipment, cameras, watches, china, and stainless-steel flatware &mdash; at tax-free, deeply discounted prices, often shipped home ahead of him. For men like Charlie, finishing a tour and a courtship at the same time, the PACEX "list" was a standard way to assemble the household goods of a marriage on a private's pay. (In the parallel letters from this spring, Charlie's mother Peggy mentions the stainless steel he had already sent home &mdash; PACEX flatware making the same journey.) The *$300* here is the savings or credit that lets him add to that list.

*Source: [Wikipedia: Army &amp; Air Force Exchange Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_%26_Air_Force_Exchange_Service).*

</aside>

## Transcription

> *May 9*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> I haven't been very good at writing letters. There isn't anything to do or write about. I'm still waiting to hear from Sears. It seems my parents won about $300 from PACEX so I should be able to add the $300 worth of stuff I want on to the list. Let me know if you hear from your parents. If they don't say anything I guess we will have to make plans anyway. I guess we have until I get back to decide anything major. Well be good and keep exercising.
>
> Love Charles

## What the letter records

**"The list."** The household-goods list Charlie is building through Sears and PACEX is the most concrete evidence in the letters of how literally he was assembling a married life from Vietnam &mdash; piece by piece, by mail, before the wedding had even been settled with Terrie's parents.

**"We have until I get back to decide anything major."** After months of negotiating the Hawaii-and-marriage question by airmail, Charlie draws a sensible line: the small logistics can be handled now, but the big choices belong to the two of them, in person, once he is home. It is the letter of a man who has stopped trying to settle everything from eight thousand miles away.

**"Be good and keep exercising."** The throwaway sign-off is pure Charlie &mdash; affectionate, a little teasing, the ordinary register of a couple closing the last weeks of a long separation.
