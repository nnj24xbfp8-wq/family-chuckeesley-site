## What the letter is

28 November 1970, a short letter dashed off on a resupply day, the weather keeping him in and writing. Two threads carry it. First, a piece of real family news: Terrie and her family may get to Marietta over Christmas vacation, raising the prospect of the two families meeting &mdash; *"Are you going to try to introduce the parents or not?"* Second, the question Charlie has been pressing since the [25 November Thanksgiving letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-25-thanksgiving-field/): *"What is your answer to the Hawaii deal?"* &mdash; the R&R plan he wants her to commit to, framed here as her Christmas present.

## Transcription

> *Nov 28*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> Well how is everything now? We get resupplied tomorrow and I'll mail this then. It has been a shower type misty rain for the last 4 days. Every now & then we are able to dry out anything that we got wet so it isn't too bad.
>
> It seems that you and your family will [make it] to Marietta some time during X-mas vacation. Are you going to try to introduce the parents or not? Have fun! You should, you know!
>
> Not much else happening around here. I'm just passing time away. Due to weather conditions I'm just writing you and hope [you get] these last few letters. If the weather breaks before the chopper comes I'll try another letter &mdash;
>
> [page 2:] What is your answer to the Hawaii deal? That will be your X-mas present, with me as the prize. You can't win them all, you naughty girl. You will just have to take your chances.
>
> *[A few intimate lines are held private at the family's discretion.]*
>
> Well I guess I better end this letter as I'm getting rather silly.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**The families, maybe meeting.** *"It seems that you and your family will [make it] to Marietta some time during X-mas vacation. Are you going to try to introduce the parents or not?"* This is the courtship's two families edging toward each other while Charlie is eight thousand miles away &mdash; Terrie's people coming to his hometown, the parents perhaps meeting for the first time, with the soldier whose relationship it is able only to ask about it by mail. It is a small, concrete marker on the road to the [August 1971 marriage](/family/charles-eesley/).

**The Hawaii deal, pressed.** *"What is your answer to the Hawaii deal? That will be your X-mas present, with me as the prize."* The proposal first made seriously on the closing pages of the [25 November letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-25-thanksgiving-field/) is now a standing question he wants answered. The teasing Christmas-present framing is Charlie selling the plan &mdash; the same plan that becomes the [booked April 1971 R&R](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-03-29-second-rr-booking/) and the spring's parental-conflict thread.

**The field housekeeping.** Four days of shower-type misty rain, drying out between showers, *"so it isn't too bad"* &mdash; and the resupply-and-chopper rhythm that governed when a letter could be mailed (*"I'll mail this [when we get resupplied]... if the weather breaks before the chopper comes I'll try another letter"*). The same weather and logistics that run through the whole late-November run of letters.
