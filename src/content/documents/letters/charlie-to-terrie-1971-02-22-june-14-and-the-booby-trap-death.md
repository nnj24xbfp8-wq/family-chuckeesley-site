## What the letter is

22 February 1971, evening, written two days into the Camp Eagle job. It carries the two registers of the late tour side by side: the administrative future, the extension that fixes his out-date at 14 June, the long clerk hours, the R&R allocation calendar, and, breaking through it, the war he has just been pulled out of. Two men from his old platoon pass through on their way to Bangkok and bring the news that the soldier who stepped on a booby trap in December has died of his wounds. The contrast, a safe desk and a dead friend, is what sharpens the anti-war line that closes the letter.

## Transcription

> *Feb 22, evening*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> Well I've been here two days. I still haven't cleared my old company but everything is in order. I've extended and I will be out of this damn army June 14. The work isn't hard but the hours are long, 7:30 AM to 7:00 PM, 7 days a week.
>
> Allocations haven't come down yet for R&R in April. They should come around March 2 or so. I need a reply from you as to what you want. Yes I do want to see you but I want you to tell your parents too.
>
> I just saw 2 guys from my platoon who were on their way
>
> [page 2:] to R&R in Bangkok. They were telling me how it has been. I guess it has been pure hell out there. The guy who stepped on a booby trap last December while I was in Da Nang died 21 days later. It must have really shook them up. They all missed my being there and writing to my congressman about the horrible war and stupid army. I'm so anti army and war now I can barely describe it.
>
> Let me know about Hawaii from your end as soon as possible.
>
> I love you.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**The out-date.** "Out of this damn army June 14" fixes the date the whole spring is counting toward, the same horizon as the "down to 115" day-count in the 18 February letter. The extension was the price of the early-out arrangement.

**A death reported secondhand.** The soldier who stepped on a booby trap "last December while I was in Da Nang" connects to the [2 January 1971 booby-trap letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-01-02-booby-trap-and-the-drop/); here, weeks later and through two men passing toward Bangkok, Charlie learns the man died of his wounds. The fact arrives flat and lands hard.

**"So anti army and war now I can barely describe it."** The clearest statement of the politics that runs through the late letters, set directly against the safety of the clerk job, the men still in the field, and the friend who didn't make it.
