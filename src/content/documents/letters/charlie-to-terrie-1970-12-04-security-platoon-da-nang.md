## What the letter is

The letter that opens Charlie's rear-area winter. After the November field letters, with their [rising water](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-30-disgusted-with-the-army/), the [firebase](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-21-firebase/) and the ["really hairy"](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-18-really-hairy/) days, he has a new address and a sixty-day posting to the security platoon at XXIV Corps headquarters in Da Nang. Every December letter after this one, [the mess hall and the courts](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-08-call-and-the-mess-hall/), [the routine](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-12-routine-and-a-friend-killed/), [the EM club](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-16-em-club-photo/), [Bob Hope at Christmas](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-25-christmas-bob-hope/), is written from the job this letter announces.

It is better than stateside duty could ever be.
>
> The only thing on my mind now is money. A friend of mine has withdrawn from the 10% savings program and they have conveniently lost one of his receipts for $300 so he is out $300. I'm in the same boat and it worrys
>
> [page 2:] me a little. Otherwise things couldn't be brighter.
>
> How is everything with you? I'll be writing a few other letters today so I'll end this now and send another in tomorrow or so.
>
> It is hard to make a call around here but I'll try before X-mas.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**"The bad days are at least over."** The count is exact. Back with his company after 4 February, plus 36 days, gives about 12 March, the same homecoming date the [12 December letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-12-routine-and-a-friend-killed/) gives for the 10-month drop and the one the [31 December letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-31-ten-month-tour-definite/) treats as settled. On that schedule the field is finished. As it turned out, it wasn't: Charlie chose to extend to June 1971, and by mid-February he was [still in Da Nang, waiting on clerk-job orders](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-02-15-danang-awaiting-orders/).

**"Better than stateside duty could ever be."** This comes from the man who, about a year earlier in training, had written ["I hate this place."](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-training-physical-and-mental-tests/) The rear's comforts are listed in detail four days later, in the [8 December letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-08-call-and-the-mess-hall/).

**R&amp;R deferred.** This is the first sign that the Hawaii meeting would slip into 1971. He won't take R&amp;R until he's back with his company, and if the early drop came through, R&amp;R might not happen at all. The [12 December letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-12-routine-and-a-friend-killed/) says it outright: don't count on Hawaii.

**The phone call.** *"I'll try before X-mas"* is the first mention of the call he planned in the [8 December letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-08-call-and-the-mess-hall/) and made on 13 December ([14 December letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-14/)).

**XXIV Corps and the 10% savings program**

**XXIV Corps** took over command of U.S. ground forces in South Vietnam's northern provinces (I Corps) from the Marines' III Marine Amphibious Force in March 1970, and ran them from headquarters in Da Nang. A headquarters security platoon guarded the compound's perimeter and towers. Line companies rotated men through such details, which is why Charlie's posting had a fixed end date and a return to "our company." It is also why the later December letters are about [guard duty](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-17-tower-fired-on/) and [the fence](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-18-fifty-fifty-and-the-fence/).

The **"10% savings program"** was the Uniformed Services Savings Deposit Program, created in 1966. Service members in a combat zone could deposit pay and earn 10% annual interest, well above civilian rates, and withdraw it when they left the zone or, in limited cases, for an emergency or R&amp;R. Deposits were tracked with paper receipts. A lost receipt meant a long fight to prove the money was there, which is why Charlie worried about it.
