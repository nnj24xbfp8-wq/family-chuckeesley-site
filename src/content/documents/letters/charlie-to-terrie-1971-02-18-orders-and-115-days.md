## What the letter is

18 February 1971, Da Nang. A short follow-up to the previous day's long letter about the clerk-typist orders, the Hawaii R&R, and the stereo. The orders still haven't come, and almost everything Charlie wants from the rest of the tour, ETS, the Hawaii trip, getting his money straightened out, is waiting on that one piece of paper. The tone is steady and almost light: the days are going faster, there is nothing hard about where he is, and the worst news on offer is a rumor about a 30-day extension.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**ETS, DEROS, and the day-count**

Two Army acronyms sit under this letter. **ETS** (Expiration of Term of Service) is the date a soldier's enlistment ends; **DEROS** (Date Eligible for Return from Overseas) is the date the tour ends. Charlie's whole spring plan, the clerk job, the April Hawaii R&R with Terrie, the "early out" he's been angling for, hangs on lining these up. The **"down to 115"** is the short-timer's day-count toward DEROS that runs through the late letters; at 115 days on 18 February, his projected return falls in mid-June 1971. The rumor that the unit "may even be extended down here another 30 days" is exactly the kind of calendar uncertainty that made every plan provisional.

*Source: contemporary U.S. Army usage of ETS / DEROS; cross-referenced against the adjacent dated letters in this collection.*

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Feb 18*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> I'm still in Da Nang. I was expecting my orders today but they didn't come. I'll call tomorrow if they aren't here then. I've got to get that job if I'm to ETS and go to Hawaii and even clear up my finance problems.
>
> At least the days go faster. I'm now down to 115 if everything goes according to plan. There is nothing hard about being here so maybe I'm better off. I hear we may even be extended down here another 30 days. That would really be something. The weather has been pleasant and sunny the last few days and my sunburn didn't peel so I'm doing alright. Hope everything is fine with you.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**Everything waits on the job.** In a single sentence Charlie chains the clerk-typist orders to ETS, to Hawaii, to his finances, the administrative job has become the key that unlocks the whole end of the tour. The previous day's letter laid out the plan in detail; this one is just the daily check on whether the paperwork has moved.

**"Nothing hard about being here."** Written from the rear at Da Nang, the line measures how far the clerk job had pulled him out of the field. The danger that fills the earlier 1970 letters has receded to a rumor about an extension and a note about sunburn.
