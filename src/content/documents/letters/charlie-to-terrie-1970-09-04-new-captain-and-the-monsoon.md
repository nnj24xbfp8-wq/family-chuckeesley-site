## What the letter is

4 September 1970, page 1 of a letter written literally under a poncho in the rain — the monsoon settling in as a steady presence rather than a single storm. Charlie's real subject is a new captain whose judgment he already distrusts, laid out with the same unemotional tactical assessment he brings to every account of risk in these letters.

> *Page 1 only. The closing page for this letter has not yet been located in the archive; a nearby scan turned out to belong to a different letter instead. If a second page surfaces, it will be added here.*

## Transcription

> *Sept 4*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> I'm writing to you from under my poncho. This rain is just a drizzle, but the frog bark[ing] on the hill is definitely part of the monsoon picture. I don't know when we will go to the lowlands, but it should be in a week or so. The army is just messed up so I'm not really certain when we will go down.
>
> My new captain is not as good as the other, and has done some bad things — like sending us too far out on a patrol with just 6 of us and no immediate reinforcement. It won't matter once we get to the lowlands. It is going to be a most miserable experience any way I look at it. I just hope I find a few dry spots somewhere.

## What the letter records

**The monsoon as atmosphere, not event.** *"The frog bark on the hill is definitely part of the monsoon picture."* Charlie reaches for the sound of frogs rather than the rain itself to describe the season turning — a small, specific, almost literary detail rare in these otherwise practical letters.

**A new captain, judged and filed.** Charlie's assessment of a superior officer's tactical judgment — sending six men out too far with no backup — is delivered flatly, without editorializing beyond the bare fact. It's the same instinct for professional critique that surfaces later and more explicitly in the [28 October letter's](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-28-one-year-in-the-army/) plan to tell a congressional inquiry *"how stupid their tactics are."*

**"It won't matter once we get to the lowlands."** A recurring piece of field logic throughout this stretch of letters — risk assessed relative to terrain, with the lowlands consistently treated as the safer condition regardless of who's in command. The same lowlands-versus-mountains calculus anchors the [7 September letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-09-07-firebases-and-the-honeymoon/) written just three days later.
