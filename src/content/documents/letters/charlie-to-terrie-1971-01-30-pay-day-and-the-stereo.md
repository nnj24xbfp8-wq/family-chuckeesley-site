## What the letter is

30 January 1971. A short, sour note in the same grey key as the [29 January letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-01-29-monsoons-136-days/) written the day before &mdash; the pay clerks have fouled up again, the mail hasn&rsquo;t come, and the field is still waiting somewhere on the other side of the bad weather. Charlie is plainly irritated, and the only thing in the letter that lifts is the stereo: tomorrow he finishes buying it, and for a private who counts every dollar toward the wedding, the $850 of Japanese gear is a small, defiant indulgence.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**What was happening on 30 January 1971**

The date sits right at the opening of the Laos campaign&rsquo;s buildup. **30 January 1971 was the day [Operation Dewey Canyon II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Dewey_Canyon_II) began** &mdash; the US Army preparatory operation that reopened Route 9 toward the abandoned Khe Sanh Combat Base and staged the forces for [Operation Lam Son 719](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Lam_Son_719), the ARVN incursion into Laos that would launch on 8 February. Charlie, back in the Da Nang rear after his autumn malaria, doesn&rsquo;t mention any of it; his horizon is the pay line, the weather, and the field he still expects to be sent back to.

That expectation is what dates the letter so precisely in hindsight. Within about ten days the clerk job at [Camp Eagle](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-02-08-clerk-job-camp-eagle/) would take the field off the table for the rest of his tour &mdash; so this is one of the last letters written under the assumption that he would go back out.

*Sources: [Wikipedia: Operation Dewey Canyon II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Dewey_Canyon_II); [Wikipedia: Operation Lam Son 719](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Lam_Son_719).*

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Jan 30*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> We got paid today and they messed things up again. I didn't get a chance to even see anybody about my problem and it really makes me mad. I hope when we go back I'll have an opportunity to see someone before I'm sent back to the field.
>
> The weather is really going to be something when we go back I'm afraid. I really dread it.
>
> Still no mail from you today.
>
> I will buy the rest of my stereo equipment tomorrow. That will be $850 worth of stuff that we will have. That is the most I've spent on a luxury item in my life &mdash; it better last.
>
> Not much else to say. Love Charles

## What the letter records

**&ldquo;My problem.&rdquo;** The letter never says what it is &mdash; an administrative matter (the ETS/&ldquo;drop&rdquo; paperwork he was chasing through January) or something medical (he was only weeks past the malaria that had pulled him out of the field). Either way the frustration is the same recurring one of the late tour: the Army&rsquo;s clerks and their own pace standing between him and what he needs.

**The stereo.** The $850 of gear is the running purchase that surfaces across the winter letters and comes together in the [17 February letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-02-17-typing-job-and-stereo/) &mdash; the TEAC tape deck, the Pioneer speakers and receiver, bought piece by piece through the PACEX mail-order catalog. &ldquo;Stuff that *we* will have&rdquo; is the tell: everything he buys in these months is already furnishing the married life he is counting the days toward.

**Still expecting the field.** *&ldquo;When we go back&hellip; before I&rsquo;m sent back to the field&rdquo;* is the assumption that the clerk job would shortly overturn. Read next to the [14 February valentine](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-02-14-valentines-clerk-orders/), the relief of that reprieve is easier to feel.
