## What the letter is

Early 1970, Fort Bragg, somewhere in the months of training before the orders. A small, domestic, slightly bored letter &mdash; the kind written between a morning on the pistol range and a planned weekend pass. Charlie thanks Terrie for the shirts, gently tells her to stop spending money on him (*"you will need all you have & more for the next year and a half"*), and reports the day's work with a shrug: the .45 today, grenades tomorrow, the whole thing *"slow and boring."* It's the ordinary texture of becoming a soldier, before any of it turned real.

**Dating this letter**

The letter carries no date header. It is placed in early 1970 on internal evidence: it is written from **Fort Bragg** during training, the weapons familiarization (pistol, grenades) is basic/advanced individual training content, and the *"next year and a half"* of service points to early 1970 for a soldier inducted in late 1969 (consistent with the [12 April 1970 letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-04-12-orders-on-my-mind/), where Charlie notes 20 weeks served). It sits among the pre-deployment letters, before his Vietnam orders came through that spring.

</aside>

## Transcription

> Dear Terrie,
>
> I got your shirts today. Thanks. I like the dark one, the light one not as much. I bought another blue one in between (your colors) at Ft. Bragg.
>
> Seriously, please don't send or buy anything else for me. You will need all you have & more for the next year and a half. I have no needs for the immediate future, except a warm body.
>
> Today was rather cold but it warmed up by this afternoon. I fired the .45 pistol today and tomorrow I throw some hand grenades. The training is rather slow and boring.
>
> I hope I can get away this weekend. It would be good to get out of here for once.
>
> I've written to Wilkerson & Jay but so far no reply. I'll write this weekend more in depth.
>
> Love Charles

## What the letter records

**Soldiering as routine.** *"Fired the .45 pistol today and tomorrow I throw some hand grenades... slow and boring."* The weapons of the trade are recorded with the flatness of any other workday's tasks &mdash; a reminder that the war, for the men sent to it, began as tedium and paperwork and ranges.

**"You will need all you have & more for the next year and a half."** Even in training, Charlie is doing the couple's arithmetic, waving off Terrie's gifts and pointing at the long stretch of service still ahead. The frugality that runs through the Vietnam letters &mdash; the stereo math, the PACEX list, the R&R budgeting &mdash; starts here.

**The friends.** Wilkerson, Jay, and (elsewhere) Fisher, Ferguson, Abbott &mdash; the network of young men Charlie wrote to and about, scattering toward the same war. The training letters are where that cast first appears.
