## What the letter is

14 October 1970, and one of the quiet hinge-letters of the corpus: this is where **Hawaii is born.** *"Do you want to meet in Hawaii in Feb?"* — floated tentatively, half talked out of in the same breath (*"I don't really think it would be a good idea"*), it is the first appearance of the R&R-reunion plan that dominates the winter and spring letters and becomes, by [25 November](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-25-thanksgiving-field/), *"the Hawaii deal."* Around it, the tour's timeline is coming into focus for the first time: *"big drops,"* home maybe in March, out of the field by **1 May 1971**, 140 field-days left of 240. The end is *"still a long way off, but at least in sight."*

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**Where the Hawaii plan goes**

The idea first raised here — meet in Hawaii on R&R — runs the length of the correspondence. By the [25 November Thanksgiving letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-25-thanksgiving-field/) it is *"the Hawaii deal"* he keeps pressing her to answer; through the winter it becomes a concrete, booked plan, and the spring letters carry the orders and the fare arrangements (Terrie listed as *"Mrs. Eesley"* on the paperwork so she could get the reduced military fare). The *"1 May 1971"* out-of-the-field date he cites here is the Vietnamization drawdown horizon — all U.S. combat troops being pulled from the field — that made the reunion, and the eventual [August 1971 marriage](/family/charles-eesley/), possible.

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Oct 14*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> It rains now every day, and I sort of like spring showers. They last long enough to get us wet and then no rain. I'm still in the lowlands but still don't know for how long.
>
> Well, now that papers is out I don't know when I'll go. Do you want to meet in Hawaii in Feb? I don't really think it would be a good idea, but if it could be arranged it might be a possibility. They are giving big [drops] like crazy over here. I may get home in March but would still have 8 months in the army. I would probably go to school after this. Anyway, if I come home in June [12?], at least I'll be out of the field May 1, 1971, because all combat troops are being withdrawn. Of the 240 days left over here
>
> [page 2:] I figure only 140 left in the field.
>
> It is still a long way off, but at least the end is in sight. I have a little over 5 months in country now. I'll let you know if I hear anything more definite.
>
> How are things going? I have a chance to mail this letter today, so I guess I'll close.
>
> I love you.
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**Hawaii, first floated.** *"Do you want to meet in Hawaii in Feb?"* The origin of the tour's central plan, offered here with characteristic hedging — he raises it and doubts it in one sentence. Everything the [later letters](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-25-thanksgiving-field/) build into *"the Hawaii deal"* starts as this tentative question from the lowlands.

**The timeline resolves.** *"Out of the field May 1, 1971, because all combat troops are being withdrawn... only 140 [days] left in the field."* For the first time the drawdown becomes a personal calendar: a fixed date when the field ends, even if Army time continues. It is the hopeful counterpart to the [3 October letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-03-malaria-cam-ranh-bay/)'s dread-filled *"150 in the field,"* eleven days and a little hope later.

**"The end is in sight."** The register shifts. The [early October letters](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-06-back-to-the-field-raise-hell/) are angry and braced for silence; this one, mailed on a lucky chance, lets itself look forward — to Hawaii, to a spring out of the field, to school after the Army. Rain he *"sort of likes,"* an end he can finally see.
