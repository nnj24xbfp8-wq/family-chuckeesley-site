## What the letter is

28 December 1970, from the year-end rear-area *"school"* period, and one of the letters where the two engines of the whole tour, the drop rumor and the marriage plan, run side by side. The rumors now favor a **10-month tour** (home by mid-March), and Charlie lets himself get *"psyched about coming home"* &mdash; and, in the same breath, floats getting *"married early"* if it happens. The letter also carries a small, sharp piece of how fear moved through the rear: a passing soldier's claim that a platoon had been *"wiped out,"* believed, dreaded, and then quietly judged a lie.

## Transcription

> *Dec 28*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> It seems all the rumors are saying it will be a 10 month tour so I should be home in the middle of March if that is so. Still nothing definite.
>
> A week ago some guy passing through said a platoon was wiped out so I was really scared about going back, but it seemed he lied for some reason and not too much has happened in the field since we left to come here. It still rains a little here but not as much as it still is in the field, and I haven't been wet since I've been here.
>
> [page 2:] I'm really getting psyched about coming home even if I still would be in the damn army. If I do come back early we will see how things are and maybe even get married early. It then would depend on where I am stationed I guess.
>
> Stop sending magazines! I thought I wrote that on one of my letters. They have a library with all the magazines in it.
>
> I'm on guard now and I hope to catch up on my letter writing so &mdash;
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**The 10-month tour, and "get married early."** *"The rumors are saying it will be a 10 month tour so I should be home in the middle of March... maybe even get married early."* The drop rumor of the [12 December letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-12-routine-and-a-friend-killed/) (the March-12 date) here hardens into the *"10 month tour"* the [31 December letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-12-31-ten-month-tour-definite/) would treat as near-certain, and Charlie ties it directly to the marriage: an early return could mean an early wedding. It didn't come; the drop fell through, and the marriage waited until [August 1971](/family/charles-eesley/).

**The platoon that wasn't wiped out.** *"Some guy passing through said a platoon was wiped out so I was really scared about going back, but it seemed he lied."* A rare, honest look at how rumor and fear traveled the rear, a secondhand horror story that spiked his dread of returning to the field, then dissolved on closer reading. The fear was real even when the report wasn't.

**"Stop sending magazines!"** The small domestic reversal, and one of the corpus's quiet callbacks: the [30 November letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-30-disgusted-with-the-army/) had asked her to *"send something like Newsweek or Playboy,"* but now, in the rear with *"a library with all the magazines in it,"* he tells her to stop. The care-package economy adjusting to the comforts of the rear.

**Not wet since the rear.** *"I haven't been wet since I've been here."* The plainest measure of the difference between field and rear, after the months of monsoon the [November letters](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-30-disgusted-with-the-army/) are soaked in: dryness itself as a luxury worth reporting.
