## What the letter is

29 January 1971. A waiting letter, written in the flat grey of the late monsoon from the Da Nang rear. Nothing is happening &mdash; *another day has past* &mdash; and that is the whole mood: the weather worsening, the days to be gotten through, 136 of them left. Charlie still expects to be sent back to the field soon (*"where I will be going soon,"* *"I really dread going out in the field again"*), which makes this, in hindsight, the last letter of a particular kind: ten days later the clerk job at Camp Eagle would take the field off the table for good. The back half turns practical &mdash; the intricate, slightly hopeless logistics of getting Terrie to Hawaii on a private's pay and the Army's schedule.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**On the eve of the operation**

The date sits right at the hinge of the winter. *"The monsoons aren't officially over til March"* is accurate for I Corps, where the northeast monsoon runs roughly September to March and kept the Da Nang&ndash;Hue lowlands under low cloud and rain through the winter. The very next day &mdash; **30 January 1971** &mdash; Operation Dewey Canyon II opened the road to Khe Sanh and began the buildup for Lam Son 719, the campaign Charlie would describe in detail in his [2 February letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-02-02-laos-last-ditch/). On the 29th he doesn't mention it yet; his horizon is just the weather and the field he expects to return to.

The R&R arithmetic is the other half. Hawaii R&R required reserving an allocation in advance, and a serviceman could have his spouse fly at a reduced **dependent rate** by sending her a copy of his R&R orders &mdash; which is exactly why marriage and the trip had fused into one question across these letters. Charlie's complication is cash flow: the Army would not release his accrued pay until he was about to leave country, so he could only fund the trip *after* they met, not before.

*Sources: [Wikipedia: Operation Lam Son 719](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Lam_Son_719); [Wikipedia: R&R (military)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%26R_(military)).*

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Jan 29*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> Another day has past. No enemy activity reported but the weather is getting worse. I guess the monsoons aren't officially over til March and it is still raining where I will be going soon. Not much to look forward to. I just hope the days keep on passing by. At most now I have 136 days left.
>
> I didn't get to go to finance because of bad weather. Maybe tomorrow I'll be able to go. Instead I played tennis. I have (for me) a decent tan now, but I'm still rather pale. [One line omitted.]
>
> No mail today but that was probably partly to do with weather too.
>
> I really dread going out in the field again. At least I should lose some of the blubber I've picked up since I've been here these last two months.
>
> Nothing really new to report. Let me know when & if you can make Hawaii. I'll have to take an allocation a month in advance to be able to go. Can you get cut-rate fares for this or not. I don't know. If we were married I would send you a copy of my R&R orders and you would get a rate reduction. There are going to be a lot of problems involved but they will probably work out. I'll be able to give you money for the trip after I see you but I don't see how before because these people won't let me have my money unless & until I'm ready to leave which would be too late. Write & tell me what you think and how much air fare would be for you. Well I hope I have more to say tomorrow. I love you.
>
> Love Charles

## What the letter records

**"I just hope the days keep on passing by."** The short-timer's whole philosophy in one line. By late January the war had narrowed, for Charlie, to a calendar to be survived &mdash; 136 days, then 131, then 130, the number he would keep reporting through the spring.

**"I really dread going out in the field again."** The crucial fact that dates this letter precisely in the arc: as of 29 January, Charlie still believed he was going back to the field. The dread is real and specific. Ten days later the clerk job at [Camp Eagle](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-02-08-clerk-job-camp-eagle/) would remove that expectation, and the field would recede into something he watched on a map. Read against the February letters, this is the before.

**The R&R money problem.** The most concrete look in the corpus at the cash-flow bind of a drafted soldier trying to fund a trip: he can get Terrie a dependent rate reduction if they marry, but he can't touch his own pay until he is leaving, so the trip has to be financed on her end first and reimbursed after they meet. The romance of the Hawaii plan kept running into the plain arithmetic of a private's finances.
