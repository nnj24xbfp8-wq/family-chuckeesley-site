## What the letter is

Roughly mid-January 1971, from the Da Nang rear. This is the longest and most candid letter Charlie wrote &mdash; and for years it sat in the archive as two separate records, because its pages had come down through two different sources. Page 2 survived as a scan (`dad151`); pages 3 and the closing survived in a family PDF; page 4 turned up later in the dad-numbered scans. Read in order, they are obviously one letter: page 2 ends *"Also they shoot off"* and page 3 begins *"a lot of flares."* What it adds up to is the closest thing in the corpus to an honest sociology of the war as Charlie lived it in the rear &mdash; the base, the drugs, the boredom, the Red Cross women, and his own careful self-positioning just outside all of it.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**The Da Nang rear, January 1971 — and the people in it**

**Da Nang**, on the central coast of I Corps, was the largest U.S. base complex in the northern provinces &mdash; Marine and Air Force airfields, hospitals, and rear-area logistics for the 101st Airborne and other I Corps units. By January 1971 it was being staged as the principal rear hub for the imminent **Operation Lam Son 719**; within weeks its airfields would be processing helicopter casualties from Laos. Charlie's *"500–800-person headquarters base on a peninsula between river and ocean"* fits one of the smaller 101st detachments scattered across the complex, away from the main installations. The ARVN grenade incident he reports &mdash; an argument settled with a thrown grenade &mdash; and the constant firing into the air across the river read as exactly the low-grade indiscipline that strained ARVN units in the tense run-up to the Laos operation.

The **"Donut Dollies"** were real and specific: young American women of the **Red Cross's Supplemental Recreational Activities Overseas (SRAO)** program, who ran recreation and "a touch of home" for troops at bases across Vietnam. *Donut Dollies* was the affectionate GI name nationwide; the cruder nickname Charlie passes along was barracks slang. That he reports it *"not to their face"* is the tell &mdash; he is narrating the culture to Terrie, not endorsing it.

## Transcription

> *[Page 1 — date and salutation; not yet identified in the surviving scans.]*
>
> *[Page 2:]* &hellip; booze rather cheap (no tax at all) and we can drink anywhere we want. I think I'll go to the beach tomorrow (GI section). A few guys went today and had a good time. There is a GI bar there too with a larger PX. There are about 500&ndash;800 people on this base and it is a headquarters base. We are on a peninsula with the river on one side & ocean on the other with a narrow strip of land. There are a lot of Vietnamese around but they are pro-gov't and mostly Catholic. The main city of Danang is across the river and so are the marine and air force bases. It is a very secure area. (The position I said was fired at wasn't. A ARVN soldier had an argument with another and one fired a shot in the air and the other tossed a grenade which exploded 70 meters away from the tower. It is crazy and someone could get killed but no one was injured by it. We hear firing across the river in Danang all the time because the ARVN are firing in the air. Also they shoot off
>
> *[Page 3:]* a lot of flares &mdash; It should get pretty noisy around [here] til as it was noisy on Xmas & New Year's. They lit up the sky with flares.) I have only tried a few puffs of marijuana back at Evans and I was drunk at the time. I really don't go for it and neither do most around me, but it is wide spread as are other drugs. The kids will try to sell it to us through the fence along with anything else they have to sell. Also they want to buy our cigarettes as it costs us 17¢ [a] pack and theirs around .95. As far as your thinking marijuana is dangerous, I don't think so at all. I just hate smoking of all kinds and the army is cracking down on it and I don't want any more trouble besides. If you tell your medic friend I said [&hellip;]. Most everyone I know (married or not) [&hellip;]. Well believe it or not I'm still a good boy as far as [&hellip;] relations are concerned.
>
> *[Page 4:]* What is all this bull about you loafing your last semester and me paying for it. You are really dreaming now aren't you. Maybe I should stay over here an extra year to earn enough money to pay for all my expenses that will be waiting for me when I get back. I see you at least agree in writing to no pajamas. [&hellip;] I'll see whether you're over that hangup when I get back or maybe Hawaii. As far as your contraceptive I thought you had an examination and then one of the many types would be prescribed for you if you could take one. I guess you will have to see a doctor to see what he suggests. Well I hope you do alright on your finals and finally become a senior. I doubt if you will ever graduate though. Keep plugging &mdash; maybe one of these years they might give you a diploma just to get rid of you. At least Lesher & Ferguson started two years before you and they are still in school.
>
> *[Closing sheet:]* I hope you don't think I'm comparing you with anyone over here, though. There are some American Red Cross girls over here and are called "Donut Dollies"! Some are pretty sad looking even for [the GIs] and we fondly call them "Biscuit Bitches," although not to their face. Just thought you would like to know a little of what goes on. I'll be telling you for the rest of your life so don't get too curious. I'll write again tomorrow night &mdash; esp. if I receive another letter from you.
>
> Love,
> Charles

## What the letter records

**The tourist's eye.** The 1970 field letters report operations; this one reports a base &mdash; the bar, the beach, the PX, the headcount, the geography &mdash; with the cataloguing eye of someone with time on his hands and nothing shooting at him. The contrast is the point: Charlie has been pulled out of the war's tempo and is describing where he lives the way you'd describe a posting, not a battlefield.

**The ARVN grenade.** The day's news, rear-area edition: two ARVN soldiers, an argument, a warning shot, a grenade 70 meters from a tower, nobody hurt. He files it the way he'd once filed contact reports &mdash; *"it is crazy and someone could get killed but no one was injured by it"* &mdash; a hazard now observed rather than lived.

**Marijuana, and the careful distance.** The only place in the corpus where Charlie writes at length about drugs in the ranks. His position is exact and characteristic: he doesn't think marijuana is dangerous, he just hates smoking and won't risk Army trouble so close to getting out. He places himself *near* the culture and consistently outside it &mdash; the same move he makes about sex (*"I'm still a good boy"*) and about the lifers he worked beside.

**The Donut Dollies.** A real institution &mdash; the Red Cross SRAO women &mdash; reported with the GIs' own crude nickname and an immediate disclaimer (*"not to their face"*). The closing line is the tenderest thing in the letter: *"I'll be telling you for the rest of your life so don't get too curious."* He is narrating a world to the person he intends to spend that life with.

**Editorial note.** This letter's intimate-register lines are elided above with [&hellip;], consistent with the collection's policy; the substantive, documentary content is kept intact. The letter was previously catalogued as two separate entries &mdash; a Da Nang base description and a marijuana-and-Donut-Dollies letter &mdash; before its pages were recognized as a single physical letter and reconciled here.
