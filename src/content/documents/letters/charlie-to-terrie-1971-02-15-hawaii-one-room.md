## What the letter is

15 February 1971. A letter built as a numbered list &mdash; Charlie answering Terrie&rsquo;s letter of the 8th point by point, the way he does when several threads have piled up. In four items he covers the war, the wedding, the Hawaii room, and her grades, and the whole thing is characteristically flat and practical about matters that were, underneath, enormous: whether he was safe, whether they would sleep in one bed or two, and when exactly they would marry.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**&ldquo;No U.S. troops in Laos except support&rdquo; &mdash; and the news was telling the truth**

Charlie&rsquo;s reassurance is legally precise. Under the **[Cooper&ndash;Church Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper%E2%80%93Church_Amendment)**, in force from January 1971, U.S. ground forces were **barred from operating inside Laos and Cambodia**. So during [Operation Lam Son 719](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Lam_Son_719) the cross-border ground fighting was done by the ARVN; the American contribution was **air and artillery support** fired from and flown out of I Corps. His *&ldquo;the news is telling the truth&rdquo;* is a soldier reassuring a worried college girlfriend against alarming headlines &mdash; and, unusually for a man who often thought the coverage soft-pedaled the war, telling her the reporting was, on this point, accurate.

The wedding math is the other spine of the letter. The August plan is the one that held: Charlie and Terrie [married in 1971](/archive/eesley-wedding-1971-portrait/). The *&ldquo;one room &amp; one bed&rdquo;* versus the *&ldquo;separate rooms&rdquo;* his parents wanted the families told about is the small, funny, human friction of two sets of parents and a couple who had already made up their own minds &mdash; the same tension that runs on into the [April letters about the parents and Hawaii](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-04-02-parents-against-hawaii/).

*Sources: [Wikipedia: Cooper&ndash;Church Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper%E2%80%93Church_Amendment); [Wikipedia: Operation Lam Son 719](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Lam_Son_719).*

## Transcription

> *Feb 15*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> I got your letter dated Feb 8 and it seems I have a little mail still coming. [Answering your letter:]
>
> (1) I'll keep the April 9-19 in mind when I try to get my R&R. Please don't count on getting married then. It just isn't what I want.
>
> (2) There are no U.S. troops in Laos except support. The news is telling the truth.
>
> (3) My parents think we will have separate rooms in Hawaii and want me &/or you to tell your parents that arrangements are being made along that line. We will have just one room & one bed. It is simpler that way.
>
> (4) Sorry to hear about your F in English Lit. I hope it doesn't affect your graduation.
>
> [page 2:] I hope you can get it changed too. That would be even better. I guess the rest of your grades are in the letter I haven't received yet.
>
> Assuming all goes well we will be married I guess in August. (What serious date do you want?) There is no foreseeable problem with money for me. I'll probably come home with 2500-3500 cash plus what I have at home in stocks, so if you think we will be married in August or so I guess I could afford $350 for your tuition.
>
> I'm working on getting a tan. I'm red as hell right now. Maybe I'll look human by Hawaii.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**The reassurance about Laos.** Of all the war news in these months, the Laos incursion generated the most alarming coverage back home &mdash; and this is Charlie, from the rear, telling Terrie the frightening thing on her television was also, on the narrow point of American ground troops, accurate. It reads against his [4 February fore-warning](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-02-04-laos-fore-warning/) that the campaign was coming, and his [1 March account](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-03-01-lam-son-719/) of watching it go wrong.

**The one room.** The most quietly revealing item in the letter: two families&rsquo; expectations managed with a small fiction (&ldquo;separate rooms&rdquo;) while the couple quietly arranged the truth. It is the same negotiation, in miniature, that the [April Hawaii letters](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-04-05-mom-suggested-hawaii-marriage/) would carry on at length.

**August, and the tuition.** The wedding date is still a question he is putting to her (*&ldquo;what serious date do you want?&rdquo;*), but the money is settled: the savings-plan cash and the stocks add up to enough that he can float her $350 tuition. As ever, the finance and the marriage are one subject &mdash; and the plan named here, August, is the one that came true.
