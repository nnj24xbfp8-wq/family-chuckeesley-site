## What the letter is

10 October 1970, a resupply-day note of the plainest kind — *"I'm writing to let you know I'm still alive."* Two days back in the field after the [8 October departure](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-08-leaving-for-the-field/), Charlie sends the minimum: a weather report (lowlands, wetter but not the mountains), a line of encouragement about her degree, and a hope pinned on the news he can barely get — that *"Nixon announces a plan for faster withdrawal."* It is the letter the [6 October](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-06-back-to-the-field-raise-hell/) warning predicted: brief, resupply-dependent, written to beat the chopper.

## Transcription

> *Oct 10*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> We got resupplied today so I'm writing to let you know I'm still alive. We are in the lowlands for a few days because some other company here messed up and we are taking their place. They are in the mountains. How I dread going back to the mountains. It isn't raining now and doesn't rain as hard in the lowlands as in the mountains. It has rained a few times since I've been back and I'm wet now, but so far it hasn't been too bad.
>
> Well, [as for] school — you say it has started. You should at least make dean's list once in your college career. I haven't heard much of what is going on in the world. I hope Nixon announces a plan
>
> [page 2:] for faster withdrawal from over here.
>
> Well, this is just a short note and I must pack everything back together now, so I'll close.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**"I'm still alive."** The whole purpose of the note, stated in the first line. On resupply days a man in the field wrote what he could before the chopper left; the [6 October letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-06-back-to-the-field-raise-hell/) had told Terrie the letters might stop for weeks, so the mere fact of one — *proof of life* — was the message.

**Dean's list.** *"You should at least make dean's list once in your college career."* Even a two-page survival note carries the education push. It is relentless and affectionate — the same insistence on her degree that runs from the [training letters](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-training-orders-decide-everything/) through the [3 October letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-03-malaria-cam-ranh-bay/)'s *"that damn diploma."*

**"I hope Nixon announces a plan."** The hope of the man in the field, cut off from the news, pinned to the politics of the [6 October letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-06-back-to-the-field-raise-hell/) — the *"immediate pull out"* he was voting for, restated as a wish for a presidential withdrawal timetable that would shorten his own war.
