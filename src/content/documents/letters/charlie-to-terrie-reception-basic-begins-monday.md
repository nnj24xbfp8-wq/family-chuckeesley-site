## What the letter is

A tired half-page from the reception week, the holding period after induction and before basic training formally begins. Charlie has an hour to himself and spends it flat on his bunk; there is an hour of pointless walking around a building to do tonight, *"as does everyone else."* Basic *"begins Monday,"* and he plans to come up and see Terrie on 20 December first. It's one of the earliest letters in the correspondence, the road to Vietnam not yet even properly started.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**Undated — date on the envelope**

No date on the page. The postmark survives on the envelope and will be added later. It is placed just before the [first day of basic](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-basic-training-first-day/): this letter says *"Basic begins Monday,"* so it was written in the reception week before basic started, and plans a 20 December visit to Terrie.

</aside>

## Transcription

> Dear Terry,
>
> I actually got 1 hour to myself but it is here on my bunk. I have to walk around a building for an hour tonight as does everyone else and I have to go soon.
>
> I hope you are studying hard. I expect all A's from you. I'm tired so this is going to be a short letter. Basic begins Monday which ought to be great. I haven't done anything all week and I'm very tired.
>
> I'm looking forward to seeing you and I probably will come up 20 DEC.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**Reception week.** *"I actually got 1 hour to myself... I have to walk around a building for an hour tonight as does everyone else."* The pre-basic reception station, the days of processing, waiting, and make-work before training proper, is the Army at its most numbing, and Charlie's flat exhaustion (*"I haven't done anything all week and I'm very tired"*) is the fatigue of pure idleness and regimentation, not labor.

**"Basic begins Monday."** The letter sits on the hinge between civilian and soldier: basic hasn't started, but it's days away, and Charlie is already bracing (*"which ought to be great,"* dry). The [first-day-of-basic letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-basic-training-first-day/) picks up the following Monday, KP on day one.

**"I expect all A's from you."** Terrie is in school (she'd be at the University of Maryland), and Charlie's teasing insistence on her studying, *"I expect all A's from you"*, is the thread that runs to the very end of the corpus, the [October 1970 field letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-19-160-days/)'s *"keep studying and maybe we can have a good time next summer"* said from a firebase a year later.

**The 20 December visit.** The plan to *"come up 20 DEC"* to see Terrie before basic swallows him, one of the small in-person meetings that kept the courtship alive across the training year, on the way to the [August 1971 marriage](/family/charles-eesley/).
