## What the letter is

The concrete other half of a decision the [NCO-school letters](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1969-nco-school/) only name in passing. Charlie kept saying he'd refuse NCO school *"if I'm sent there instead of D.C."* &mdash; and here is what *"D.C."* meant: an interview for a **guard assignment in Washington**, guarding *"things like the unknown soldier,"* one day on and two days off. It is the elite ceremonial posting he was chasing as the alternative to the NCO-school path that fed Vietnam. The letter catches him mid-pursuit: interview missed, rescheduled, hopes up.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**The D.C. guard &mdash; the Old Guard and the Tomb**

Guarding *"the unknown soldier"* in Washington points to the **3rd U.S. Infantry Regiment ("The Old Guard")** at Fort Myer, Virginia, the Army's ceremonial unit that mans the **Tomb of the Unknown Soldier** at Arlington National Cemetery around the clock. The *"1 day on and 2 days off"* rotation Charlie describes matches the Tomb-guard duty cycle. It was a stateside, non-combat assignment &mdash; the opposite destination from the one NCO school tended toward. Whether Charlie got it is answered by the rest of the corpus: by 1970 he was in [Vietnam with the 101st Airborne](/docs/letters-from-vietnam/), not at Arlington.

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Sunday (mailed Monday) &mdash; 618 days left*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> Just a quick note to let you know what is going on.
>
> *[A short family-health passage is held private at the family's discretion.]*
>
> Now the good news. I missed my interview but will get to make it up next Saturday. They need guards in D.C. and after this nonsense here I would go to Washington and become a guard. I would be guarding things like the unknown soldier, etc. I wouldn't know for certain whether I get the assignment until the 8th week but if I pass the interview I understand the chances would be very good. I'll keep you posted on these developments.
>
> The job would be 1 day on and 2 days off so it would be a very good deal.
>
> I had a good time this weekend. I got drunk but that was all. I did forget the army for a while and that was good.
>
> *[a middle page appears to be missing; the letter continues on a later page:]* ...there from the 15 April on. I'll let you know after my interview Saturday for the "guard" job.
>
> Our mail seems to be messed up something awful. I get 2 letters, then no letters for several days, and then 2 again. I haven't heard from Al or Jay yet, or Abbott for all that matters.
>
> How was your slumber party? I hope you are not so lonely you can't have fun. Just don't date or do anything naughty.
>
> I hope everything is okay. What are you doing on your app? Just don't rush into it like I did. Also, what are you doing about transportation once you're out on your own?
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**What "D.C." was.** *"They need guards in D.C.... I would be guarding things like the unknown soldier."* The [NCO-school letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1969-nco-school/)'s *"if I'm sent there instead of D.C."* and the [mortar-training letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-training-mortar-and-nco-decision/)'s reasoning both point at this: a Washington guard posting was the safe, stateside alternative he was actively interviewing for. This letter is the only place in the corpus that says plainly what he was reaching for &mdash; and, by implication of everything that follows, did not get.

**"618 days left."** The service-obligation day-count in the header places the letter early &mdash; roughly a year and eight months of Army time still ahead, the decision-making months before deployment. It is the pre-Vietnam counterpart to the in-country day-counts (*"day 180... not even half way yet"*) that fill the [1970 field letters](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-11-08-stand-down-and-sydney/).

**Forgetting the army for a while.** *"I got drunk but that was all. I did forget the army for a while and that was good."* The weekend reprieve, said without apology &mdash; the same use of alcohol as a brief exit from the Army's grind that recurs through the training and field letters alike.

*A note on the withheld passage: the letter opens with family news that concerns the health of living family members; it is held in the private family archive at the family's discretion, and the rest of the letter is reproduced above.*
