## What the letter is

Early on a Wednesday morning, around mid-February 1970 &mdash; one of the pre-deployment training letters, written at the end of a cycle when *"everything in base is over except for turning in our equipment."* Orders are in, rumors are flying, and there had been a pointless late-night inspection that left Charlie sneering at the officers running it. It's a small, ordinary letter &mdash; pay day, a promised phone call, an apology for a letter he forgot to mail &mdash; but it sits at the very start of the whole correspondence, in the months before Vietnam.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**Dating it: "nearly 80 days in the army"**

The letter has no date header, but it dates itself. Charlie counts *"nearly 80 days in the army,"* and his [12 April 1970 letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-04-12-orders-on-my-mind/) puts him at 20 weeks (≈140 days) of service &mdash; placing his induction in late November 1969. Eighty days from there lands in mid-February 1970, and a Wednesday near then falls on 11 February. The *"food money over the Christmas break"* is the separate-rations pay (commuted rations) soldiers received when a mess hall closed for the holidays &mdash; here paid in arrears for the late-December 1969 break.

The contempt for the officers running a *"stupid"* inspection is the same anti-institutional streak that runs, fully formed, through every later letter from Vietnam.

</aside>

## Transcription

> Dear Terrie,
>
> Today is early Wednesday morning. We got our orders today and rumors are flying. Everything in base is over except for turning in our equipment. We had an inspection last night and it was all kind of stupid. Our officers were really pricks. I don't think they know how to hold inspection.
>
> Today I will have spent nearly 80 days in the army. I got paid yesterday and got an extra 22.50 for food money over the Christmas break. The army doesn't want you to starve so they pay you now. I should be calling tonight or so. You should receive two letters. I forgot to mail the other one yesterday.
>
> Love Charlie

## What the letter records

**The start of the road.** This is among the earliest letters in the correspondence &mdash; a training-base note from a man counting his first eighty days in uniform, with Vietnam still ahead of him. Read against the field and rear letters of 1970-71, it's the before-everything baseline.

**The institution he already disliked.** *"Our officers were really pricks. I don't think they know how to hold inspection."* The loathing of Army brass and "lifers" that defines the later letters didn't come from the war; it was there in basic, over a botched inspection.

**The ordinary machinery.** Pay day, commuted rations, a promised phone call, a letter forgotten in the mail &mdash; the small domestic texture that the correspondence would keep, even from the firebases.
