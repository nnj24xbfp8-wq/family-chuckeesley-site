## What the letter is

8 February 1971. This is the hinge of Charlie's tour. After months of angling for it &mdash; the extension math of the [19 January letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-01-19-ets-extension/), the warning that Laos was coming in the [4 February letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-02-04-laos-fore-warning/) &mdash; he has *finally got a clerk job*, and at **Camp Eagle**, the headquarters of the entire 101st Airborne Division. He calls it *a relatively safe place* and a *harder clerk job*, and he doesn't care which: indoors, out of the field, out of the weather. The plan is now locked: extend the tour so he can discharge straight from Vietnam on **14 June**. He sends his new HQ address and asks Terrie, once more, to pick R&R dates that work for her.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**Camp Eagle: the safest address of the tour**

**Camp Eagle was the base camp and headquarters of the 101st Airborne Division** &mdash; built in early 1968 about 7 km southeast of Hue and 9 km west of the Phu Bai Combat Base, in Thua Thien Province, I Corps. (Charlie's *"about 3 miles from Phu Bai"* is right.) The division moved its headquarters there in 1969, and it remained the 101st's nerve center until the division stood down and turned the base over to the ARVN on 1 February 1972. A clerk posting at division HQ &mdash; the *"Office Chief of Staff"* on his return address &mdash; was about as far from the line as an infantryman in the 101st could get while still in country: rear-area, fortified, administrative. For a soldier who had spent 1970 on firebases and in the mountains of the rocket belt, *"a relatively safe place"* was the whole point. It is the destination the entire extension strategy had been built to reach.

*Sources: [Wikipedia: Camp Eagle (Vietnam)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Eagle_(Vietnam)); [Wikipedia: 101st Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division).*

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Feb 8*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> I finally got a clerk job and I'm definitely going to extend to get out of the army Jun 14. I'll be at Camp Eagle about 3 miles from Phu Bai and in a relatively safe place. It is probably one of the harder clerk jobs but I don't care.
>
> The weather is miserable so I'm glad I'll be indoors.
>
> There is no rush or even definite time set for me to go on R&R so name some appropriate times you may want.
>
> I'll write again as soon as I get situated.
>
> My address: [service number withheld] / HHC, 101st Abn Div / Office Chief of Staff / APO SF 96383
>
> Love Charles

## What the letter records

**"I finally got a clerk job."** The single most consequential sentence of the winter letters. Everything in the surrounding correspondence &mdash; the extension to a 14 June ETS, the move to Da Nang, the "brass and lifers" he typed about a month later &mdash; flows from this posting. From here to the end of the tour Charlie is a rear-area clerk at division headquarters, not a rifleman, and the change is audible in the letters that follow: the war becomes something he reads about on a map rather than something he is standing in.

**"It is probably one of the harder clerk jobs but I don't care."** The trade he is happy to make: more demanding desk work in exchange for being out of the field. Difficulty was never the thing he was trying to escape; danger was.

**"Name some appropriate times you may want."** Even on the day he secures the safe job, the R&R-with-Terrie planning continues &mdash; the thread that would run all the way to the June rendezvous in Hawaii. With no fixed date imposed on him yet, he hands the scheduling to her.

**The address.** His return address places him squarely at division HQ &mdash; *HHC, 101st Abn Div, Office Chief of Staff, APO SF 96383.* His individual service number, which appeared at the top of the address block, is withheld here in keeping with the archive's privacy policy; the unit and APO are preserved as historical record.
