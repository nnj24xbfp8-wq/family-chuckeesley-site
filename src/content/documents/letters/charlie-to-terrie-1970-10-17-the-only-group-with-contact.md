## What the letter is

17 October 1970, two pages written waiting on a resupply helicopter, two days after the [15 October letter's](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-15-first-kill-in-three-months/) VIP visit. The letter opens on a small, weary observation — his company again the only one making contact — before settling into a typhoon that fizzled, an out-date estimate, and a gentle but firm instruction not to worry about things neither of them can control.

## Transcription

> *Oct 17*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> It seems that we are the only group that has contact anymore. No one else but ours, in every[thing]. Oh well.
>
> We were supposed to get a lot of rain from a typhoon that was in this area, but so far it hasn't materialized. We had some rain but nothing really bad. I still don't know how long we will be in the lowlands. It may be a week or a month or the rest of my tour. As of now I guess I'll be coming home June 12 or so. That is 240 or less days away. I'll be out of the army then.
>
> I guess some of my letters are rather depressing in tone, but I just write the way I feel. Don't really worry about my getting home. There is really nothing I can do, or you can do, about
>
> [page 2:] it for 240 days or so. The past few days have been passing rather quickly, which is good.
>
> I am in fairly good spirits now, just sitting around waiting for the [chopper] to pass — I think this year will [pass] more or less.
>
> Oh, don't get me wrong, but I doubt if I'll be getting anyone anything for Christmas. There just isn't anything worth sending over here — you can't send anything other than consumables, such as goodies.
>
> The helicopter is due any time now, so I guess I'll end this letter.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**"The only group that has contact anymore."** A quiet, weary complaint that his company keeps drawing the engagements other units seem to be avoiding — the same pattern noted two days earlier when his company made the brigade's first kill in three months. Whether accurate or just how it felt from inside the unit, it's a recurring note of specific bad luck running through this stretch of letters.

**240 days, named twice.** Charlie repeats the number across the page break — *"that is 240 or less days away"* and *"about it for 240 days or so"* — the kind of repetition that shows the count is less information than mantra, the fixed point he organizes the rest of the letter around.

**"I just write the way I feel."** A rare piece of explicit self-awareness about his own letters' tone, paired with an instruction not to worry rooted in practicality rather than reassurance — not *it will be fine*, but *there is nothing either of us can do about it*. It's a philosophy of endurance more than comfort, consistent with his approach throughout the corpus.

**No Christmas gifts, by necessity.** A small, concrete detail about what Vietnam actually made possible to send home — nothing but consumables — that quietly explains why so many of his letters ask Terrie to expect only letters, not presents, for the holidays.
