## What the letter is

The most emotionally exposed of the training letters, and one of the most self-aware pages in the whole corpus. Charlie warns Terrie up front that he is going to be blunt and won't edit himself &mdash; and then does two things at once: he tells her, in practical detail, to secure her own education without a financial trap; and he tells her *why*, which is that his orders are about to decide everything, and that he will not let the emotion of reunion push them into a *"spur-of-the-moment"* marriage. It is a young man trying to be fair to someone he loves from inside a future he can't yet see. *"A lot will be solved,"* he writes, on **17 April**. What his orders solved was Vietnam.

<aside class="not-prose my-8 border-l-4 border-amber-700 bg-amber-50/60 dark:bg-amber-950/20 px-6 py-5 rounded-r">

**"A lot depends on my orders now"**

The letter sits at the hinge the whole training run had been building toward: the [Washington guard posting](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-training-dc-guard/) he'd been chasing versus the overseas assignment he dreaded. The *"17 April"* on which *"a lot will be solved"* is the same mid-April 1970 orders window the [mortar-training letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-training-washington-after-april/) had named (*"in Washington after 18 April... at least I won't be going overseas"*). Here the confidence of that earlier letter has tightened into suspense. The orders, when they came, sent him to [Vietnam with the 101st Airborne](/docs/letters-from-vietnam/) &mdash; and the marriage he refused to rush happened anyway, in [August 1971](/family/charles-eesley/), on the far side of the war.

</aside>

## Transcription

> *Monday*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> We trained today in the rain. Everyone was miserable. It was so simple, but the rain, cold, and misery made it a bad day. You will probably get another letter with this one, as I forgot to mail it today.
>
> We have to have a little talk about a few things, and I'm going to present some of them rather bluntly, so don't lose any sleep over what I'm writing. I'm not going to edit these and can't. It may not mean as strongly as it sounds. But here it is anyway.
>
> Right now I feel that you should finish school with no expense hassle. Do not go part-time or establish residence. If you can get your loan, get it &mdash; 3% or 4% is cheap anytime. Watch where you live and what kind of contract you sign. Also be sure of your roommate. The thing that really concerns me is your part-time [job]. Please do everything you can to reach an easy break. Don't quit your job just for it, but if it is interfering with school, by all means quit.
>
> [page 2:] I guess what I'm trying to say is that a lot depends on my orders now. It will definitely decide our immediate future and maybe everything. I'm fairly certain I love you, but as I remember saying once before, absence really does [strange] things for our relationship. I mean, I probably would force you to marry me when I just see you, and I don't want that deep down &mdash; primarily because it would be a spur-of-the-moment cure-all. I guess I would owe [us] a lot of problems. I want things peaceful with your parents, school, and all. [...] I guess a lot will be solved 17 April.
>
> *[A short family-health passage is held private at the family's discretion.]*
>
> I must get up at 4 tomorrow, so I must go to bed now. Your brainwashing article was a big hit. What he said was true but a little strong. Oh well. At least this letter is different. I got a letter from &mdash;
>
> *[The closing page is not located among the surviving scans.]*

## What the letter records

**"A spur-of-the-moment cure-all."** *"I probably would force you to marry me when I just see you, and I don't want that deep down."* The rare, unguarded honesty of the corpus: Charlie naming his own likely impulse and refusing it, out of care rather than doubt. He wants the marriage to be a decision, not a reaction to reunion &mdash; and he wants it not to cost her *"peaceful"* relations with her parents and her degree. It is the same fairness the [M-72/ring letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-training-m72-and-the-ring/)'s *"I'm still willing to marry you, it is just that things such as discharges will have to work out"* was reaching for.

**"Finish school with no expense hassle."** The practical half of the same care: loans over part-time status, watch the contract and the roommate, don't let the job crowd out the degree. The [education-as-inheritance thread](/docs/family-threads/) in real time &mdash; Charlie pushing Terrie to protect her schooling exactly as she would later push Chuck's. It's the same *"keep studying"* that runs from the [clerk-training letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-training-clerk-e2/) to the [field letters](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1970-10-19-160-days/).

**The unedited letter.** *"I'm not going to edit these and can't. It may not mean as strongly as it sounds."* Charlie flagging his own bluntness &mdash; a soldier writing past midnight before a 4 a.m. wake-up, choosing not to soften what he means. It's why the page reads more raw than most.

**The brainwashing article.** *"Your brainwashing article was a big hit. What he said was true but a little strong."* Terrie mailing him anti-war / anti-Army reading &mdash; the same current that runs through the [Mothers-for-Peace correspondence with Dorothy Hauser](/docs/letters/charlie-to-dorothy-hauser-1971-01-01/) and Charlie's own *"beginning to hate the army"* refrain.
