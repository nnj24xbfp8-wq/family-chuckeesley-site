## What the letter is

1 January 1971, New Year's Day, a day before the [2 January booby-trap letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-01-02-booby-trap-and-the-drop/). The end-of-tour uncertainty that dominates the winter letters is at its peak here: a hundred rumors about when he gets out, none of them firm. It's a short, restless note, half a shrug at the rumor mill, half a quiet observation about the civilians around him who are untouched by the war.

## Transcription

> *Jan 1*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> The new year has finally arrived and I'm still in doubt whether & when I get out of here. There are a hundred gone rumors and theories. Hell &mdash; I may take the next flight out of here. Enclosed is a picture you may want.
>
> I've been talking with a few of the people around here and they don't even know there is a war going on. I guess they are just lucky.
>
> I'm really having difficulty writing letters to people I owe letters to. Oh well that is too bad I guess.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**The rumor mill.** "A hundred gone rumors and theories" about when he gets out is the same end-of-tour uncertainty the ETS-extension letters work to pin down; here it's still all rumor, no date.

**"They don't even know there is a war going on."** A glance at the civilians around him, untouched by what he's living through. The line carries the flat, slightly envious distance that runs through his observations of people outside the war.

**The picture.** An enclosure, "a picture you may want," one of the small physical things that traveled inside these envelopes alongside the words.
