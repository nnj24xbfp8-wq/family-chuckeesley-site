## What the letter is

5 November 1970, written from the field in the monsoon. It carries the physical misery of the line, fourteen days with no mail, a month without a shower, more than a week of unbroken rain, set against the first clear statement of the plan that organizes everything to come. The extension math here, extend 32 days to reach a 13 June 1971 out-date, with a possible 90-day early-out pulling it back toward mid-May, is the origin of the ETS strategy that the [19 January 1971 ETS-extension letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-01-19-ets-extension/) and the whole spring countdown refine and hold to.

## Transcription

> *Nov 5*
>
> Dear Terrie,
>
> It has now been 14 days since anyone has received any mail here. We came in to the rear area for 1 day yesterday but it was hardly worth it. It has been now over a month since I've taken a shower. It isn't raining but a few days ago it was for 8-9 days straight. You really can get miserable. Anyway I guess I'm alright.
>
> About a month ago Mom wrote our congressman and now I have to go to the rear for part of her inquiry into my situation (health wise I guess). It should be interesting and I'll let you know how it turns out.
>
> I'm not certain but I think I made E-4. I hope so as it will give me an extra $50 to my name.
>
> [page 2:] I plan to extend 32 days which would let me get out of the army June 13, 1971 with [a] 50 day early out. Right now they have [a] 90 day early out for a while and I may be able to get in on that too which, even if I did extend, I would get out May 14 or so. It all depends and everything is up in the air.
>
> Right now the sun is shining and I'm in pretty good spirits. I got paid yesterday but I figure the army still owes me $500 so I'll have to check up on it.
>
> I'm going to cut this letter short but I hope to get another one off soon.
>
> I love you.
>
> Love
> Charles

## What the letter records

**The monsoon field.** Fourteen days with no mail, a month without a shower, eight or nine days of straight rain, this is the physical baseline of the field tour, stated plainly and then shrugged off ("Anyway I guess I'm alright").

**The congressman inquiry.** "About a month ago Mom wrote our congressman" places the start of [Peggy McMaster Eesley](/family/margaret-mcmaster-eesley/)'s intervention on Charlie's behalf around early October 1970; here it's pulling him to the rear for the inquiry. The same congressional-letter campaign surfaces in the [22 February 1971 letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-02-22-june-14-and-the-booby-trap-death/), where his old platoon-mates remember him for it.

**The extension plan, first stated.** This is where the whole end-of-tour calculation begins: extend 32 days for a 13 June 1971 out-date, with a 90-day early-out possibly pulling it to mid-May. The [19 January 1971 ETS-extension letter](/docs/letters/charlie-to-terrie-1971-01-19-ets-extension/) and the spring day-counts ("down to 115," "77 days left") are all refinements of the arithmetic laid out here in November.
