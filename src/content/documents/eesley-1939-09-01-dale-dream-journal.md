## What the page is

A single handwritten sheet on yellowed lined paper, dated *Sept. 1, 1939* at the top in a hand that reads as a fifteen-or-sixteen-year-old's &mdash; tightly looped, schoolgirl-careful, the lines slightly drifting down across the page. The word ***"war!"*** is written at the very top in a hand that may be the same or may be a later annotation, oriented inversely to the main text &mdash; suggesting the writer turned the page over once to add the word at a later moment.

## Transcription (as legible)

> *Sept. 1, 1939*
>
> *I had a dream last night about Dale, that he had come back and had [...] memory and wandered off &mdash; no one would believe me when I told them he had come back and that the man we buried was someone else.*
>
> *I was so glad to see him &amp; was he to see me &mdash; but then I awoke and realized that in a week I'll be going back to school, without him &mdash; it's going to be hard.*

A few words are not fully legible due to fold-and-crease wear on the paper; the visible text is enough to carry the entry.

## Helen is the author

The author is [Helen](/family/helen-burnes/), confirmed by Chuck Eesley June 2026 (the page came to the archive from her daughter [Roberta](/family/roberta-burnes/)). On 1 September 1939 Helen was **fifteen years old**, about to begin her junior year at Bexley High School. She had **asked Dale to take the boat across [Black Lake](/places/black-lake-michigan/) to fetch her chocolate** on 14 July 1939; he never came back. She would carry that guilt **for the next sixty-one years**, until her own death in 2000. This page is the **earliest first-person record of that grief** in the archive.

## What the dream contains

The content is the classic shape of **early-stage grief denial** at six-to-seven weeks after a sudden death:

- **The return.** Dale comes back. He has not died &mdash; he has wandered off with memory loss.
- **The misidentification.** *"The man we buried was someone else."* The mistaken-corpse fantasy is a recurring grief motif, especially in cases of drowning where the body identification is the kind of clinical event that can sit oddly in family memory.
- **The unbelieving family.** *"No one would believe me when I told them he had come back."* The dreamer is the one who knows the truth that everyone else has refused to see.
- **The mutual recognition.** *"I was so glad to see him &amp; was he to see me."* The thing the dream gives is the reunion.
- **The awakening.** *"Then I awoke and realized that in a week I'll be going back to school, without him."* The dream's gift is canceled by the morning. The week ahead &mdash; the return to the structure of regular life without him &mdash; is what makes the loss real again.

This is the **earliest first-person record in this archive of Helen's grief over Dale's death** &mdash; forty-nine days into the sixty-one-year grief that the [chocolate-across-the-lake story](/family/dale-eesley/) would carry forward. She had been **at the lake when it happened**. She had **asked him to make the crossing**. The dream and the morning after are the first written window into what the next six decades of her life would carry.

## The "war!" annotation &mdash; the literal first day of World War II in Europe

**1 September 1939 is the day Nazi Germany invaded Poland.** Two days later (3 September 1939), Britain and France declared war on Germany; the European war began. The word **"war!"** written at the top of this page &mdash; in what may be the same hand or may be a later annotation &mdash; almost certainly refers to that day. The author was processing **personal grief 49 days into a private catastrophe** on the same day the **global catastrophe of WWII began**. Whether she wrote the annotation that morning hearing the news on the radio, or annotated the entry later as the war's frame settled on the family, is open.

The same family would go on to lose **[Lyle](/family/lyle-eesley/)** in the Pacific war: a U.S. Army medic captured on Bataan in April 1942 and dead at Cabanatuan prison camp in August 1942. Helen's brother Dale was lost in a private drowning; her brother Lyle would be lost in the war this annotation marked the beginning of.

## How the page reached the archive

The page was kept by Helen in her own papers across the rest of her life. After her 2000 death, the papers passed to her daughter [Roberta Burnes Walker](/family/roberta-burnes/) &mdash; one of the [four paternal-side keepers credited on the homepage](/) and the keeper of the [1940s Eesley letter collection](/docs/charles-leonard-letter-collection/). Roberta scanned and emailed this page to Chuck in June 2026, alongside the other Eesley-family-history material she has been surfacing to the archive across this stretch of months. The chain &mdash; Helen wrote it at fifteen, kept it sixty-one years, passed it to Roberta, who passed it on twenty-six years after Helen's death &mdash; is itself the kind of family-record-keeping pattern this archive runs on.
