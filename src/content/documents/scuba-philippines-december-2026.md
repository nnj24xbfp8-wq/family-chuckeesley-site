## Planned trip

Chuck and Lijie have booked a **scuba liveaboard trip to the Philippines** departing **24 December 2026** and returning **2 January 2027** &mdash; a ten-day window crossing one of the world's most biodiverse reef regions.

The Philippines sits at the heart of the **[Coral Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_Triangle)** &mdash; the marine area in the western Pacific covering parts of Indonesia, Malaysia, Papua New Guinea, the Philippines, the Solomon Islands, and Timor-Leste, recognized by marine biologists as **the global center of coral and reef-fish biodiversity**. Among the world's 798 species of reef-building corals, **76% are found in this region**; many of the world's named reef fish species are here. The Philippines portion includes well-known liveaboard destinations such as **Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park** (Sulu Sea), **Apo Reef** (Mindoro Strait), the **Visayan Sea**, and a number of remote outer islands.

The **specific operator, boat name, route through dive sites, photographs, and video are pending** &mdash; the trip itself has not happened at the date of this entry.

## What's expected

A Philippines liveaboard in late December into early January typically dives a route that emphasizes:

- **Tubbataha Reefs** &mdash; the UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Sulu Sea, accessible only by liveaboard, known for large schools of pelagics, manta rays, and reef sharks. The Tubbataha diving season is **mid-March through mid-June**, so a December-January trip is more likely to focus elsewhere.
- **Visayan triangle** &mdash; the region between Cebu, Negros, and Bohol, with macro-rich muck-diving (frogfish, pygmy seahorses, harlequin shrimp, nudibranchs) alongside reef diving
- **Apo Island** (not to be confused with Apo Reef) &mdash; the small island marine reserve known for its hard-coral gardens and turtle population
- **Anilao** (south of Manila on Luzon) &mdash; one of the world's best macro-photography destinations
