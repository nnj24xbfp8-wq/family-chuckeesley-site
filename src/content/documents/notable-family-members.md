Two honest tiers here: the people **documented** in the family tree, and the famous Wildermuths who share the **surname** but whom no record yet joins to the line (see [the Rielingshausen-1682 analysis](/docs/wildermuth-clan-of-rielingshausen/) — near-certain distant cousins, not proven kin).

## The documented family — and the wealthiest

**[John Franklin Eesley](/family/john-f-eesley/) (1859–1929) — the flour-mill magnate, and the wealthiest member of the documented line.** In 1891 he established the Sunshine Flour mill in **Plainwell, Michigan**; by moving and enlarging the building in 1903 he built the **J.F. Eesley Milling Co.** into the **second-largest producer of buckwheat flour in the United States**, selling under the *Sunshine Brand* label. The mill building has been on the **National Register of Historic Places since 1991** and today operates as a brewpub — the family surname still on a federally-listed landmark. He is Chuck's great-great-uncle on the paternal Eesley side.

**[Albert Robert Eesley Sr.](/family/albert-robert-eesley/) (1837–1902) — the man who taught in Shakespeare's Birthplace.** John Franklin's father, born in Stratford-on-Avon, taught school for several years in the very building where William Shakespeare was born (per his son William's obituary) before emigrating to Canada, marrying into the Scottish-Canadian Goldie milling family, and fathering the ten children — six of them millers — of the [1899 family portrait](/archive/eesley-family-group-portrait-1899/). Not "famous," but the best claim-to-fame anecdote in the documented line.

Honorable mentions in the documented family: **[Dr. George Henry Eesley](/family/george-eesley-toronto/)**, the Toronto physician of the same 1899 generation; **[Robert Earl Wildermuth](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/)**, the WWII B-24 navigator, Pentagon lieutenant colonel, and the retired genealogist whose research this whole archive is built on; and **[Josiah Way Hill](/family/josiah-way-hill/)**, the family's killed-in-action soldier of the First World War.

## The famous Wildermuths — cousins by near-certainty, not by proof

These three bear the Wildermuth name and spring from the same **Rielingshausen** stock as Chuck's line, but the [documented connection stops one generation short](/docs/wildermuth-clan-of-rielingshausen/) — treat them as *very probable distant cousins*, not established ancestors:

- **[Ottilie Wildermuth](/family/ottilie-wildermuth/) (1817–1877)** — one of 19th-century Germany's most widely read authors for young readers. Born Ottilie Rooschüz, she married *into* the Wildermuth name (the Pleidelsheim–Tübingen branch); Württemberg still carries the name in the **Wildermuth-Gymnasium** in Tübingen and the *Wildermuthstraße* in Marbach. She received the Kingdom of Württemberg's Great Gold Medal for Art and Science in 1871.
- **Hermann-Eberhard Wildermuth (1890–1952)** — Ottilie's grandson; a decorated Wehrmacht colonel (Knight's Cross of the Iron Cross; the German garrison commander at **Le Havre** in 1944), then, from 1949 until his death in office, the **Federal Republic of Germany's Minister for Housing** under Chancellor Adenauer. His **First Housing Act of 1950** underwrote roughly four million new homes in postwar West Germany by 1957.
- **Max Frisch (1911–1991)** — the major Swiss novelist and playwright (*Homo Faber*, *The Fire Raisers*, *I'm Not Stiller*), who recorded in his diary that his maternal grandfather was a **Wildermuth** whose father *"came from Württemberg"* — a craftsman who left Rielingshausen or Pleidelsheim for Switzerland around 1850, the same emigration decade as Chuck's own ancestor Johann Michael.

## The bottom line

For **wealth**, the answer is clear and documented: **John Franklin Eesley**, the Plainwell buckwheat-flour magnate. For **fame**, the biggest names — a cabinet minister, a celebrated writer, a Nobel-shortlisted novelist — are all Wildermuths, but they hang on the **unproven Rielingshausen cousin link**; within the *proven* family, John F. Eesley and his Shakespeare's-Birthplace father Albert Robert Sr. carry the day.

*(Living family are left aside here by preference — though it is worth one line that the present-day academic notable is Chuck himself, a Stanford professor.)*
