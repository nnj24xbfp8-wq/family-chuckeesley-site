## What this is

Alongside his [combat log](/docs/the-big-one/) and [memoir](/docs/robert-earl-wildermuth-memoir/), [Robert Earl Wildermuth](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/) kept a **photo album** of his war, each image captioned in his own typed voice. Four of those captioned photographs have surfaced (forwarded by Chuck in 2026). The **images themselves are not yet held in this archive** — a higher-resolution scan can be dropped in later — but his captions are transcribed here.

## The aircraft — nose art of the 90th Bomb Group

**GLADYS**
> *"This airplane was named after somebody's wife or girl friend."*

**"BELLE WRINGER"**
> *"Another much used airplane. Note bombs denot[ing] each mission."*

**CHEROKEE STRIP**
> *"A real Indian beauty. The day after this picture was taken, the airplane blew up in flight kil[ling] all aboard."*

The three captions are a compressed portrait of the [Jolly Rogers'](/docs/90th-bomb-group-jolly-rogers-context/) daily reality: planes named for wives and sweethearts, mission tallies painted bomb-by-bomb on the nose, and — in a single flat sentence about **Cherokee Strip** — the ever-present nearness of death, an aircraft and its whole crew gone the day after a routine photograph.

## The homecoming — "THE U.S.A. AT LAST"

> *"Picture taken as we approached the Coastal Range of mountains in California on our triumphal return to the United States in October 1945. Isn't it beautiful!!! No place like it. Picture was taken at about 12000 foot altitude and in the upper left hand corner you can see part of the rear stabilizer of our plane."*

This is the visual companion to the **[28 October 1945 log entry](/docs/the-big-one/)** — the flight home from Hickam Field, Honolulu, into Mather Field near Sacramento. The photograph of the California Coast Range from 12,000 feet, the tail of his own B-24 clipping the corner of the frame, is the image behind his log line that he could *"find America going east"* as surely as he had found tiny Canton Island going west a year before.
