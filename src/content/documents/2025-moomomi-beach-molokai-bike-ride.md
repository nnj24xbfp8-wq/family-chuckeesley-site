## Mo'omomi and Molokai

**[Mo'omomi Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo%CA%BBomomi_Preserve)** sits on the **northwest coast of Molokai**, in the **Mo'omomi Preserve** area maintained as part of the The Nature Conservancy's Molokai work. The preserve is one of the last remaining intact coastal dune ecosystems in Hawaii and is recognized for its **endangered native plant species, fossilized bird remains, and traditional Hawaiian cultural sites**. The cycling route runs through coastal dunelands and dry-forest landscape relatively rare in the archipelago.

**Molokai itself** is the least-developed of the main Hawaiian islands &mdash; population about 7,500, no traffic lights, and an unusually high proportion of Native Hawaiian residents (over half of the population). The 2025 ride is therefore a small documentary slice of an island that operates differently from the rest of the archipelago.

## The quiet echo — the 1953 thread

The Hawaiian setting of the 2025 ride is **also a long-distance echo of the Hawaii thread already in this archive**: the 1953 [50th-wedding-anniversary trip](/family/stella/) that [Charles Leonard and Lillie Dale Eesley](/family/charles-leonard-eesley/) took to Honolulu to celebrate with [Stella and Ted Chong](/family/stella/) &mdash; the chosen daughter brought into the family during the war and her husband. **Seventy-two years after Charles Leonard and Lillie Dale stood on a ship's deck in Honolulu Harbor with Hawaiian welcome leis around their necks**, their great-grandson Chuck and his wife Lijie are on a bicycle on Molokai. The two trips are not in any way connected as planned events &mdash; the cycling outing was its own thing &mdash; but they fold together as parts of one archive about the family's relationship with Hawaii across three generations.
