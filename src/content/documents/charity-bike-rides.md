## Why these two rides

[Charlie Eesley](/family/charles-eesley/) &mdash; Chuck's father, the Vietnam-veteran-turned-Marietta-businessman whose [1969-1971 letters home](/docs/letters-from-vietnam/) make up the largest single corpus on this archive &mdash; **died on 26 November 2015 of complications from diabetes**. He had carried Type 2 diabetes for years; the eventual cause of death sat on top of decades of the small daily diabetic toll the disease takes on heart, kidneys, eyes, and feet.

Within the first year after his father's death, Chuck and Lijie began riding annually in the **[American Diabetes Association's Tour de Cure](https://diabetes.org/tour)** &mdash; the ADA's nationwide cycling fundraiser, which raises tens of millions of dollars each year for diabetes research, awareness, and patient care. The first ride was c. 2016; the rides have continued each year since, and the corpus now spans roughly **eight to ten years**.

At a different point in the same period, Chuck and Lijie added the **[National Multiple Sclerosis Society's Waves to Wine](https://www.nationalmssociety.org/get-involved/events-and-fundraisers)** ride &mdash; the two-day Bay Area MS Society route running from San Francisco through the Sonoma wine country. The MS ride brings a separate cause into the annual practice; the diabetes ride remains the primary anchor.

## The Empo Health connection — the same impulse, made scientific

The same year-after-year ride for diabetes carries a second, harder thread: **Chuck's venture investing in [Empo Health](https://www.empohealth.com/)**, a company building diagnostic technology for **diabetic foot ulcers** &mdash; one of the most under-recognized and under-treated complications of advanced diabetes, and a leading cause of lower-limb amputation for diabetic patients in the United States. The funding decision is not separate from the rides; the same impulse runs through both. **Show up where the disease that killed your father is doing its quiet daily damage, with whatever you have &mdash; legs, money, attention, network.**

The ADA Tour de Cure raises money for the research-and-awareness machinery; the Empo Health investment funds the specific clinical-technology piece that addresses the most painful and least-treated late-stage complication. Together they are the version of *"showing up"* this generation does about diabetes.

## The video record

Each annual ride is recorded as a **roughly five-minute video** and posted to **YouTube**. The videos are short on purpose &mdash; the format works for a yearly check-in, an updated photograph-and-voiceover-and-jersey reflection on why the ride matters and who it is for that year &mdash; and they accumulate across the decade as an honest, modest, year-over-year record.

As individual ride entries land, they will sit below this parent doc the same way [individual Vietnam letters](/docs/letters-from-vietnam/) sit below their parent collection &mdash; each one a small piece of the larger picture.

> *Ride entries to be added year-by-year as the YouTube URLs and the year-specific notes are collected. The earliest rides (c. 2016-2018) are likely already on YouTube under Chuck or Lijie's account; the most recent rides will be added as they happen.*

## Where this fits in the family threads

The annual charity rides anchor the current generation's instance of **[Thread #7 (Showing up — in small, daily, unglamorous ways)](/docs/family-threads/)** in the family-threads synthesis essay. The earlier instances of the thread the essay surfaces:

- [Charles Leonard Eesley](/family/charles-leonard-eesley/) taking in [Stella Sunn](/family/stella/) during the war and keeping her in the household for the next decade
- [Will and Peggy Eesley](/family/wilbur-eesley/) at the First Congregational UCC in Marietta, on the Tree Commission and the Bicentennial Commission, decade after decade
- [Norm and Betty Wolfe](/family/norman-graham-wolfe/) at the Sixth Street Church of Christ congregation in Marietta, taking food to the sick and driving the elderly to appointments across the rest of their lives
- [Faye Angel](/family/faye-angel/) keeping young Chuck on his sick days at her Marietta house, just as [Aunt Betty Wolfe](/family/betty-davis/) did at hers
- [Charlie Eesley](/family/charles-eesley/) himself visiting his mother [Peggy](/family/margaret-mcmaster-eesley/) at Harmar Place every week of the years she lived there
- [Briana Massaro Lockett](/family/briana-massaro-lockett/) as a Stanford physician's assistant &mdash; the medical-care-as-vocation version of the same thread

The annual charity rides are the **2010s-and-2020s version** of the same pattern: not heroics, just sustained showing-up against the specific diseases the family has lost loved ones to.

It is the smallest possible practice and the most compounding. **Eight to ten years of annual five-minute videos** is a documentary record of who the rider was each year, and a financial record of the money raised, and a kinship record of who the ride was for. The page collects them as they come.
