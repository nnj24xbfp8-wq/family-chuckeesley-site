Mokmer Drome was the U.S. Army Air Forces airfield on the south coast of [Biak Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biak) in the Schouten Islands off the northwest coast of Dutch New Guinea (now part of Papua, Indonesia). It was captured by U.S. forces during the [Battle of Biak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Biak) in May&ndash;August 1944 &mdash; a bloody fight against Japanese defenders dug into the island's coral caves. After the island was secured, Mokmer became the 5th Air Force's main heavy-bomber base in the Netherlands East Indies.

## The 400th Bombardment Squadron at Biak

The [400th Bombardment Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/400th_Bombardment_Squadron) &mdash; the *"Black Pirates"* of the 90th Bombardment Group's *"Jolly Rogers"* &mdash; moved to Mokmer Drome on 12 August 1944 and ran combat operations from Biak across the second half of 1944. The squadron's B-24 Liberators struck targets across the Netherlands East Indies, the Philippines, and the Carolines from this base.

## What happened here for Robert Earl

[Robert Earl Wildermuth](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/) and the [Van Valen crew](/docs/the-big-one/) joined the 400th BS at Mokmer Drome in late November 1944, the destination of his ferry route from Travis AFB via [Canton Island](/places/canton-island/). The crew built a tent with a wooden floor (lumber was hard to come by in the Southwest Pacific and precious) and began flying combat from there.

Biak was the home base Robert Earl was nominally assigned to even when his crew was attached out to the [22nd Bomb Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22d_Bombardment_Group) for night missions over Taiwan from [Peleliu Air Field](/places/peleliu-air-field/) and then [Leyte Air Field at Tacloban](/places/leyte-air-field-tacloban/). When the crew returned to the 90th BG after the 20 January 1945 last 22nd BG mission, the squadron had moved from Biak to Mindoro by ship while they were attached out:

> *"Some kind souls had taken down our tent on Biak, secured all of our personal property and had even saved our precious and hard to get lumber so we could build another wooden floored tent abode."*

The wooden floor that had to be transported from Biak to [McGuire Field, Mindoro](/places/mcguire-field-mindoro/) is the small documentary trace of how scarce wood was in the Pacific theater &mdash; precious enough that the squadron's quartermaster moved it across 1,500 miles of ocean rather than rebuild.

## After the war

The Mokmer Drome airstrip outlived the war. It is today Frans Kaisiepo International Airport (formerly Mokmer Airport), the civilian airport serving Biak. The wartime control tower and Japanese cave defenses sit on the island as historical sites of the 1944 campaign.
