**Ie Shima** (伊江島, also Ie Jima) is a small coral island about **5 miles off the northwest coast of Okinawa** in the Ryukyu Islands. It is about 8 square miles in area &mdash; mostly low and flat, with a distinctive limestone-tuff peak (Mount Gusuku) at its eastern end. The island was captured by U.S. forces during the **[Battle of Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa)** in **April 1945**, in fighting from 16&ndash;21 April.

## Ernie Pyle's death &mdash; 18 April 1945

The most prominent American to die on Ie Shima during the battle was **[Ernie Pyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernie_Pyle)** &mdash; the Scripps-Howard war correspondent whose dispatches from the European and Pacific theaters had won the 1944 Pulitzer Prize and made him the war's most-read American voice. Pyle was killed by **Japanese sniper fire** on a forward road on Ie Shima on **18 April 1945**, four days into the battle. The Army erected a simple wooden marker at the site reading *"At this spot the 77th Infantry Division lost a buddy / Ernie Pyle / 18 April 1945."* The marker became the Ernie Pyle Memorial &mdash; today maintained as a small permanent monument, one of only a few WWII American memorials on Japanese soil. **[Robert Earl Wildermuth](/family/robert-earl-wildermuth/)** notes in [his memoir](/docs/robert-earl-wildermuth-memoir/) that the Ie Shima he flew from was *"where Ernie Pyle was killed by a sniper."* The memory is a Pacific-tour landmark for the whole American war generation.

## The 90th Bomb Group moves to Ie Shima &mdash; summer 1945

The **[90th Bombardment Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/90th_Operations_Group)** moved forward from [McGuire Field at Mindoro](/places/mcguire-field-mindoro/) to **Ie Shima** during summer 1945 as the U.S. heavy-bomber order of battle pushed north toward the Japanese home islands. The Van Valen crew was on Ie Shima for the last months of the war.

## What happened here for Robert Earl

[The Big One](/docs/the-big-one/) Phase 4 sits on Ie Shima:

- **Late July 1945** &mdash; the **cancelled-then-Shanghai-redirected suicide mission**. The Van Valen crew had finished their required missions and been assigned a brand-new airplane to fly home when their names went back up on the operations board for one more flight. At the briefing tent: only the oldest crews, the Group Chaplain (who had never attended a briefing before), and **no target marked on the map**. Sealed envelope, to be opened thirty minutes into the flight. Cancelled three days running. On the fourth day, in the air, aborted to **Shanghai aerodrome** &mdash; where the strike destroyed 54 enemy aircraft on the ground. Only after returning was the original target revealed: **the camouflaged remains of the Japanese fleet in the Inland Sea of Japan**, just discovered by reconnaissance. The flight up the inlet would have been a suicide mission. *"Hurray for the Navy!!!"*
- **c. mid-September 1945** &mdash; the [MGM newsreel cameraman over Nagasaki](/docs/the-big-one/#nagasaki-mgm) flight launched from Ie Shima.
- **The Japanese surrender delegation** &mdash; landed at Ie Shima in **three Betty bombers painted with green crosses**; *"the first thing we saw was a hand with a bottle of champagne sticking out."*
- **The peace celebration** &mdash; drunken friendly-fire firing of rifles into the air; one officer killed and another wounded. Robert Earl took his radio and hid in a foxhole. *"All in jest I hope but never the less intimidating."* The closest he came to being killed in the whole war.
- **The 185-knot typhoon** &mdash; *"the biggest typhoon on record"* &mdash; flew the mess hall Quonset hut as one piece across a baseball field. The weather station blew away.
- **The trip home** &mdash; three days of post-storm aircraft repairs, then the long flight back via Hawaii to McClellan Field, California.

## After the war

Ie Shima reverted to Japanese civilian administration after the war. The U.S. military maintained a small presence at the wartime airfield through the postwar Okinawa-administration period; today **Ie Jima Auxiliary Airfield** is a small U.S. Marine Corps training facility on the island under the Status of Forces Agreement. The Ernie Pyle memorial remains.
