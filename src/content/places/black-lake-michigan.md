**Black Lake** is a large freshwater lake in northern Michigan's Lower Peninsula, in Cheboygan County and Presque Isle County north of Onaway, about thirty miles south of the Straits of Mackinac. It is roughly 10 miles long and 2 miles wide &mdash; one of Michigan's larger inland lakes &mdash; with a mix of cottage shore and forested undeveloped frontage. In the early-to-mid twentieth century the lake was a summer-cottage destination for upper-Midwest families willing to make the drive north from Ohio, Indiana, or downstate Michigan, and the Eesleys were one such family.

## The Eesley cottage

[Charles Leonard Eesley](/family/charles-leonard-eesley/) and [Lillie Dale Chenoweth Eesley](/family/lillie-dale-chenoweth/) owned a cottage on Black Lake from sometime in the 1920s or 1930s until it burned down in the 1950s &mdash; the year is not fixed in the family record but the loss is. The cottage was not rebuilt. Through the decades while it stood, the cottage was *"the gathering place for all Eesleys."* The family would go up to the lake for a couple of months each summer, with relatives and friends coming and going across the season.

In Lillie Dale's pen, the lake stays inside the simplest phrase: ***"the lake."*** Her [September 1939 letter to Leonard and Helen](/docs/lillie-dale-1939-letter-after-dales-death/), written eight weeks after Dale's drowning, slips between conversation about babies and college and friends and lands quietly on the line: *"It is hard to go back to the lake and no matter how one reasons there is that involuntary feeling that Dale will come walking in."* The lake was the place that held him and the place that lost him.

## The Feathers of Petoskey

The Feathers were old family friends who lived in **Petoskey**, the resort town on Little Traverse Bay west of Black Lake. Whether they also kept a cottage on Black Lake itself isn't certain in the family record. Either way they stayed in the Eesley&ndash;Burnes circle for decades. Lillie Dale's September 1939 letter mentions them among the comings and goings of that summer: *"The Feathers reported a grand time with you folks... They were at Petoskey the following Sunday &amp; spent the night with Mother Feathers."*

The connection outlasted the cottage by a generation. When [Roberta](/family/roberta-burnes/) was in high school, [Mary Eesley Bean](/family/mary-eesley-bean/) and her husband Bill came up to stay with the Burnes family at **Lake Leelanau** &mdash; the lake the family had summered at since Roberta was seven &mdash; and together they made a **pilgrimage back to Black Lake.** They walked the shore where the old cottage had stood and called on the last Feather the family still kept up with, **Ralph Feather** &mdash; in Roberta's memory, *"another goofy old man who loved practical jokes."*

## A family of practical jokers

Everyone in that family was a practical joker. The brothers were forever pulling pranks on the children or on Grandpa; the children or Grandpa would retaliate; and it went on and on, summer after summer. Two of the best survive in [Helen Eesley Burnes](/family/helen-burnes/)'s telling, now in her daughters' keeping.

### The false teeth and the one morning of "Consarn it!"

[Charles Leonard](/family/charles-leonard-eesley/) got up before dawn every day to fish. He kept his false teeth in his pocket and didn't put them in until he was out on the boat. One summer [Dale](/family/dale-eesley/) and his young sister [Helen](/family/helen-burnes/) found a set of false teeth aboard an abandoned boat along the shore &mdash; *"the shipwreck,"* in their telling &mdash; and decided to play a trick on Grandpa: after he went to bed, they swapped his real teeth for the junk pair.

Sure enough, Grandpa rose early and rowed out, and waited as always until he was on the water to put his teeth in. Helen always said you could pinpoint the exact moment, because the cussing came drifting back across the lake &mdash; first a soft ***"Consarn it,"*** then louder, then ***"CONSARN IT!!!"*** *Consarn it* was Grandpa's cussword of choice, and sound carried so well over the water that the whole cottage could hear him. It was a one-morning event, not a daily one &mdash; that morning had earned it.

### The Reveille record

Charles Leonard could be gleefully obnoxious. He'd come in from a morning's fishing, find the cottage still full of sleeping people, and put a record on the old Victrola to roust everyone out of bed &mdash; ***"Reveille,"*** the bugle wake-up call, cranked as loud as it would go. People hated it. One morning a brother had finally had enough: he grabbed the record and flung it like a frisbee out into the lake. At last &mdash; quiet, and sleep.

Except that afternoon Grandpa went swimming and spotted something on the sandy bottom: his record. He fished it out, all scratched up from the lake bed, and hid it away in the cottage. The next morning, of course, the Reveille bugle blasted again &mdash; only now the record skipped and stuck and scratched its way through the call, more excruciating than ever. Grandpa had the last word.

### Chief Whitecloud by canoe

Charles Leonard's other lake performance was a character he played by canoe: *Chief Whitecloud.* When the children were having a campfire by the shore, he would paddle along the dark edge of the lake in costume, looming up as a grand "chief," and the children would believe it. A typewritten account of one Chief Whitecloud appearance is in Roberta Burnes Walker's keeping.

## What happened on 14 July 1939

The lake also took [Dale Eesley](/family/dale-eesley/). On the afternoon of 14 July 1939, Dale &mdash; thirty-two, married to [Thelma G. Haughn](/family/thelma/) &mdash; took the boat across the lake on an errand and did not return. The boat was later found; he had drowned. His body was sent home to Grove City, Ohio for burial. The fuller account, and what his youngest sister [Helen](/family/helen-burnes/) carried out of it, lives on [Dale's page](/family/dale-eesley/) and [Helen's](/family/helen-burnes/).

[Lillie Dale](/family/lillie-dale-chenoweth/)'s September 1939 letter touches the lake just once, and gently: *"It is hard to go back to the lake and no matter how one reasons there is that involuntary feeling that Dale will come walking in."* And yet the family did go back. There were more summers, more pranks, more laughter on that water; the cottage stood another decade and more. The family carried on at the lake after Dale's death &mdash; because what else can you do.

## After the cottage

The cottage burned in the 1950s and was not rebuilt. The Eesley summer ended. Whatever stayed on at Black Lake after that &mdash; family visits, return trips, the lake itself &mdash; lives now in family memory. More lake stories from the family's keeping remain to come.
