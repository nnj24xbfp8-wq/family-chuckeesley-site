**Black Lake** is a large freshwater lake in northern Michigan's Lower Peninsula, in **Cheboygan County and Presque Isle County** north of Onaway, about thirty miles south of the Straits of Mackinac. It is roughly **10 miles long and 2 miles wide** &mdash; one of Michigan's larger inland lakes &mdash; with a mix of cottage shore and forested undeveloped frontage. In the early-to-mid twentieth century the lake was a summer-cottage destination for upper-Midwest families willing to make the drive north from Ohio, Indiana, or downstate Michigan, and the Eesleys were one such family.

## The Eesley cottage

[Charles Leonard Eesley](/family/charles-leonard-eesley/) and [Lillie Dale Chenoweth Eesley](/family/lillie-dale-chenoweth/) owned a cottage on Black Lake from sometime in the 1920s or 1930s until **it burned down in the 1950s** &mdash; the year is not fixed in the family record but the loss is. The cottage was not rebuilt. Through the decades while it stood, the cottage was *"the gathering place for all Eesleys."* The family would go up to the lake for **a couple of months each summer**, with relatives and friends coming and going across the season.

In Lillie Dale's pen, the lake stays inside the simplest phrase: ***"the lake."*** Her [September 1939 letter to Leonard and Helen](/docs/lillie-dale-1939-letter-after-dales-death/), written eight weeks after Dale's drowning, slips between conversation about babies and college and friends and lands quietly on the line: *"It is hard to go back to the lake and no matter how one reasons there is that involuntary feeling that Dale will come walking in."* The lake was the place that held him and the place that lost him.

## The Feathers and the longer family circle

Among the families the Eesleys stayed with at Black Lake were **the Feathers**, who remained in the Burnes family's circle long after the cottage was gone. Lillie Dale's September 1939 letter names them too: *"The Feathers reported a grand time with you folks &amp; we were so sorry to have missed them here &mdash; we had left Tuesday morning after you folks had gone &amp; Feathers' were here the next day. They were at Petoskey the following Sunday &amp; spent the night with Mother Feathers."* The cottage was at the center of a larger summer social network &mdash; Bexley, Ohio &leftrightarrow; Black Lake &leftrightarrow; Petoskey &leftrightarrow; Maryland.

## Three stories that ran on the water

Three Black Lake stories have survived in the family-memory record, each in [Helen Eesley Burnes](/family/helen-burnes/)'s telling and now in her daughters' keeping.

### The 4 AM "Consarn it!!" — Charles Leonard's morning routine

Charles Leonard was an early-rising fisherman. He would get up at **4 o'clock in the morning** to go out on the lake. The children inside the cottage could hear him **getting ready and then walking out the door**. He kept his false teeth in his pocket and didn't put them in until he was out in the boat. Out on the dark water the children &mdash; awake in their beds back at the cottage &mdash; could hear him wrestling with the teeth, and could hear him yelling ***"Consarn it!!"*** again and again, his voice echoing across the lake. The 4 AM teeth-wrestling soundtrack was a fixture of the Eesley summer.

### The shipwreck with the false teeth

One day Helen and her nephew [Tommy](/family/wilbur-eesley/) found a **small abandoned boat** along the shore &mdash; *"a shipwreck"* in their telling. They scavenged it for artifacts. Among what they found: **a set of false teeth.** Naturally, they replaced Grandpa's teeth with the ones they had found. (The fate of the swap is not recorded; presumably Grandpa noticed at his next 4 AM "Consarn it!!" round.)

### Chief Whitecloud by canoe

Charles Leonard's other lake performance was a character he played by canoe: **Chief Whitecloud.** When the children were having a campfire by the shore, he would **paddle the canoe along the lake's edge in costume**, looming out of the dark as a grand "Indian chief," and the children would believe him. A typewritten account of one Chief Whitecloud appearance is in Roberta Burnes Walker's keeping.

These three stories &mdash; the morning teeth, the swap, the canoe theatre &mdash; sit alongside the drowning and the cottage fire as the four major Black Lake memories the Eesleys carried. The proportions are themselves the family's: the laughter occupies three out of four.

## What happened on 14 July 1939

The lake also took [Dale Eesley](/family/dale-eesley/). On the afternoon of **14 July 1939**, Dale &mdash; thirty-two, married to [Thelma Z. Haughn](/family/thelma/) for eleven and a half years &mdash; took the boat across the lake to the general store on the far shore on an **errand for his fifteen-year-old sister Helen, who had asked him to fetch chocolate**. He did not return. The boat was later found; he had drowned. His body was sent home to Grove City, Ohio for burial.

Helen kept the guilt for the rest of her life &mdash; the chocolate-across-the-lake story stayed inside the family for sixty-one years until her own death in 2000. The Lillie Dale September 1939 letter's *"it is hard to go back to the lake"* line is the same lake.

## After the cottage

The cottage burned in the 1950s and was not rebuilt. The Eesley summer ended. Whatever stayed on at Black Lake after that &mdash; family visits, return trips, the lake itself &mdash; lives now in family memory. Roberta has more lake stories than this archive has been able to carry yet.
